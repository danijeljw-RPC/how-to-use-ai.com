# RePass Cloud Legal Policy Pack

**Effective date used throughout:** 24 September 2026  
**Last updated:** 24 September 2026

This package implements a parent-first legal structure for RePass Cloud Pty Ltd and its websites, products and smaller tools.

## Legal hierarchy

The intended hierarchy is:

1. **RePass Cloud master policies**
   - Privacy Policy
   - Terms of Service
   - Refund Policy
2. **Site- or product-specific notices and supplements**
3. **Order forms, checkout terms, licence terms, enterprise agreements or promotion-specific terms**

A more specific document prevails only to the extent it expressly applies to the relevant site, product, account or transaction and is inconsistent with a more general document.

This structure avoids maintaining a separate full legal framework for every small RePass Cloud tool while still allowing products with materially different data handling or commercial models to publish their own additional terms.

### Aethon Jobs

The files in `aethonjobs.com/` are for the **public marketing website and routing of users to the correct legal documents**.

They are deliberately not a replacement for a dedicated Aethon Jobs application privacy notice or platform terms. The application handles candidate profiles, resumes, applications, employer/recruiter information, verification and AI-assisted matching, so those product-specific documents need to remain more detailed than the corporate master documents.

### Cinturon360

The files in `cinturon360.com/` are for the **public marketing website only**.

The Cinturon360 application already has more extensive product-specific privacy, terms, commercial and refund arrangements. Those should continue to govern the actual SaaS product where they are presented to customers.

### Smaller tools and one-off products

The files in `reusable-tools/` are designed to be used unchanged on small RePass Cloud tools that do not need a product-specific privacy or commercial supplement.

If a tool starts collecting materially different information, creating accounts, taking payments, tracking users, making automated decisions, or providing a regulated/high-risk service, it should receive its own supplement rather than relying only on the generic notice.
