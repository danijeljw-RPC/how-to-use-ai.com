# Chapter 8 Bibliography and Evidence Record

## Purpose and Scope

This file records the evidence materially used in `docs/02-book-01/chapters/chapter-08-ai-and-creativity.md`.

The Chapter 8 manuscript synthesises two complementary research packages:

- `docs/80-research/chapter-08-research-package/chapter-08-research-package-basic/`
- `docs/80-research/chapter-08-research-package/chapter-08-research-package-extended/`

The extended package supplements rather than replaces the basic package. Its source IDs are retained below where helpful for traceability. The manuscript uses normal Markdown footnotes; this record adds source type, claim mapping, limitations and recheck needs.

Research and time-sensitive source check: **30 September 2026 (Australia/Adelaide)**.

This is editorial research, not legal advice.

## Evidence Decisions

- Creative execution and judgement are treated as intertwined rather than separate stages.
- “AI-generated” is treated as a continuum of workflows, not a single contribution level.
- Training is distinguished from retrieval, while memorisation and reproduction remain acknowledged.
- Legal, ethical, technical and economic objections are kept separate.
- Copyright discussion is jurisdiction-specific, with Australia first.
- Provider ownership language, commercial-use permission, statutory copyright, exclusivity and third-party rights are treated as separate questions.
- Labour evidence is limited to the measured online markets, tasks and periods; no occupation-wide replacement claim is made.
- Creator surveys are used for respondent experience and attitudes, not economy-wide employment estimates.
- Short creativity experiments support immediate task-level findings, not long-term claims about skill or cultural decline.
- Company operational data and platform policies are attributed to the company/platform and do not establish industry-wide facts.

### Design Fixation — ch8-fixation (S18)

Wadinambiarachchi et al. “The Effects of Generative AI on Design Fixation and Divergent Thinking.” *CHI 2024*. <https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642919>.

- Type: peer-reviewed 60-participant experiment.
- Supports: increased fixation and reduced idea quantity, variety and originality in the studied visual-ideation setting.
- Use: shows AI can influence judgement and human exploration, not merely generate options.
- Limits: small, task-specific and short-term; interface/prompt strategy matters.
- Recheck before publication: No, unless publication is substantially delayed.

### Design Workflow Stage and Expertise — ch8-design-workflow (S21)

Hou et al. “The Double-Edged Roles of Generative AI in the Creative Process: Experiments on Design Work.” *Information Systems Research* (2025). <https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2024.0937>.

- Type: peer-reviewed design experiments.
- Supports: effects differ by workflow stage and expertise; in one implementation condition higher-expertise participants took substantially longer without a quality improvement.
- Use: rejects “AI always saves creative time.”
- Limits: specific design tasks, systems and conditions; exact percentages are not universal.
- Recheck before publication: No, unless publication is substantially delayed.

### Short Stories: Individual Uplift and Collective Similarity — ch8-stories (S17)

Doshi, Anil R. and Oliver P. Hauser. “Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content.” *Science Advances* 10 (2024). <https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290>. Open text: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/>.

- Type: peer-reviewed controlled experiment with 293 short-story writers.
- Supports: higher immediate story ratings, stronger gains among lower-baseline-creativity participants, and increased similarity across AI-assisted stories.
- Use: flagship explanation that individual improvement and group convergence can coexist.
- Limits: eight-sentence experimental stories, nonprofessional sample and short-term outcomes; no longitudinal skill claim.
- Recheck before publication: No, unless publication is substantially delayed.

### Creativity Diversity Interpretation — ch8-creativity-debate (S19, S20)

- Type: peer-reviewed analysis/commentary and reply.
- Supports: importance of separating individual performance from group diversity; caution about over-generalising from a selected brainstorming output.
- Use: preserves a meaningful scholarly disagreement.
- Limits: interpretation of specific experimental data, not new longitudinal cultural evidence.
- Recheck before publication: No, unless publication is substantially delayed.

## Exclusions and Boundaries

- No current court case is used as a universal training-law precedent.
- No provider is ranked or endorsed.
- No creator-survey percentage is used as an economy-wide loss estimate.
- No forecast is presented as an observed outcome.
- No laboratory task is presented as proof of long-term professional creativity or deskilling.
- No personal author experience is inferred from research or tool use.
- No diagram was created: the continuum and iterative creative loop were clear in prose and a new figure was not necessary for comprehension.
