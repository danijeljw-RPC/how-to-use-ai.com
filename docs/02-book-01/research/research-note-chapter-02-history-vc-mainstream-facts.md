## Source Title and Location

Multiple sources gathered via web search for the Chapter 2 expansion (history of AI hype cycles, venture capital funding levels, mainstream cultural adoption, and AI-bubble skepticism). Listed by topic below.

### AI history / "AI winters"

- General AI timeline synthesis (Dartmouth Conference 1956, Lighthill Report/first AI winter 1974–1980, expert systems boom and second AI winter late 1980s–early 1990s, Deep Blue 1997, deep learning breakthrough 2012, Transformers 2017): synthesized from multiple timeline sources including [The History of Artificial Intelligence: A Timeline from Turing to Today](https://swisscyberinstitute.com/blog/history-artificial-intelligence/) and [The History of Artificial Intelligence: Complete AI Timeline](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/tip/The-history-of-artificial-intelligence-Complete-AI-timeline).

### ChatGPT launch and growth

- [ChatGPT, the generative AI chatbot, is released](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/november-30/chatgpt-released-openai) — launch date November 30, 2022; 1 million users within five days (per OpenAI's Sam Altman).
- [ChatGPT Has Hit 100 Million Users Already — The Fastest Growing Consumer App Ever](https://wonderfulengineering.com/chatgpt-has-hit-100-million-users-already-the-fastest-growing-consumer-app-ever/) — UBS analyst estimate of 100 million monthly users by January 2023 (~2 months), versus roughly 9 months for TikTok and ~2.5 years for Instagram to reach the same milestone.

### Venture capital funding levels

- [Global Venture Capital investment in Generative AI surges to $49.2 billion in first half of 2025 (EY)](https://www.ey.com/en_ie/newsroom/2025/06/generative-ai-vc-funding-49-2b-h1-2025-ey-report)
- [Generative AI Funding Reached New Heights In 2024 (TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/03/generative-ai-funding-reached-new-heights-in-2024/)
- [6 Charts That Show The Big AI Funding Trends Of 2025 (Crunchbase News)](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/big-funding-trends-charts-eoy-2025/)
- Rough figures reported across these trackers: ~$24–29 billion in global generative-AI VC funding in 2023, roughly $45–56 billion in 2024, and $49.2 billion in the first half of 2025 alone (EY). Figures vary meaningfully by tracking firm (PitchBook, EY, Crunchbase, OECD) depending on what counts as "generative AI" investment — treat any single number as an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a precise figure.

### Circular financing deals

- [AI Circular Deals: How Microsoft, OpenAI and Nvidia Keep Paying Each Other (Bloomberg)](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-circular-deals/)
- [Should we worry about AI's circular deals? (Noah Smith)](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/should-we-worry-about-ais-circular)
- Reported examples: Microsoft and Nvidia investing roughly $15 billion combined into Anthropic, which in turn is expected to spend roughly $30 billion on Microsoft cloud services and Nvidia chips; Microsoft's roughly $13 billion investment in OpenAI alongside OpenAI's committed $250 billion in future Azure spending. These are widely reported figures from deal announcements, not the note author's own primary verification of contract terms.

### Mainstream cultural adoption

- [2023 was the year AI went mainstream — it was also the year we started to panic about it (Euronews)](https://www.euronews.com/next/2023/12/27/2023-was-the-year-ai-went-mainstream-it-was-also-the-year-we-started-to-panic-about-it) — Collins Dictionary named "AI" its word of the year for 2023.
- General reporting on 2023 pop-culture AI moments (AI-generated entries in photography competitions, AI voice-cloning of musicians) synthesized across multiple 2023 year-in-review pieces.

### AI-bubble skepticism

- [AI bubble (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_bubble)
- [Here's why concerns about an AI bubble are bigger than ever (NPR)](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/23/nx-s1-5615410/ai-bubble-nvidia-openai-revenue-bust-data-centers)
- [Economists Concerned the AI Bubble Is About to Blow (Futurism)](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/economists-late-stage-ai-bubble-federal-reserve-inflation)
- Reported figures: total AI-sector revenue estimated under $50 billion against well over $1 trillion in cumulative investment (NPR); a commonly cited (but hard to independently pin down) claim that only around 3% of people pay for AI products. MIT economist and 2024 Nobel laureate Daron Acemoglu is quoted warning that AI is "being hyped up" and that heavy circular-deal structures risk revealing "a house of cards" if demand falls short.

## Caution / Reliability Notes

- **All dollar figures and user-count figures are time-sensitive and will date.** They are accurate estimates as gathered on the access date above but should be re-verified (or re-sourced to a single named tracker with a stated date) before the manuscript is treated as final for print. The manuscript text hedges these figures accordingly ("roughly," "by some trackers' estimates," explicit year references) rather than stating them as fixed facts.
- Funding totals vary by tens of billions of dollars depending on the tracking firm's methodology (what counts as "generative AI" investment differs between PitchBook, EY, Crunchbase, and OECD). The manuscript should not present a single figure as definitive.
- The circular-deal dollar figures come from secondary reporting on company announcements, not primary contract review by this project. They are directionally reliable (multiple independent outlets report similar magnitudes) but exact numbers should be treated as approximate.
- The "3% of people pay for AI" figure appears in commentary/opinion coverage of the bubble debate rather than a single clearly-labeled primary study in the sources reviewed; it is used in the manuscript as an illustrative, attributed claim ("some researchers estimate"), not as a hard, book-verified statistic.
- Bubble-skepticism content should stay descriptive ("some economists and institutions are warning X") rather than the book taking a position on whether a crash will actually happen — consistent with the style guide's non-hype, non-doomist stance and `ADR-02-0001`'s instruction to avoid unsupported claims.
