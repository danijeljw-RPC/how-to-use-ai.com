# Chapter 7 — AI at Work

---

## Chapter Purpose

Chapter 6 showed AI removing friction from home tasks. This chapter is where the book speaks most directly to its professional readers: the same skills, applied at work, where the stakes — and the guardrails — are a little higher.

> **Key Idea:** AI is becoming a workplace multiplier, not just a novelty.

---

## Intended Reader Outcome

By the end of this chapter, the reader should understand that:

- AI has concrete, practical uses across common professional tasks
- the Chapter 5 pattern (context, specificity, examples, iteration) applies just as well at work
- confidentiality, company policy, and verification matter more at work than at home, and each has a simple practical check
- AI is best thought of as something that multiplies what you can get done, not something that replaces professional judgement

---

# From Personal Tasks to Professional Stakes

Everything from Chapter 5 and Chapter 6 still applies here — give AI good context, be specific, use examples, iterate on the result. What changes at work isn't the skill. It's what's now at stake if something goes wrong: a client's confidential information, your employer's reputation, a report someone else will make decisions from.

None of that means avoiding AI at work. It means being a little more deliberate about a few things before you rely on it. This chapter covers both halves: what AI is genuinely good for at work, and the three checks worth doing before you use it there.

---

# Drafting Reports and Documentation

**A well-formed request:** "Draft a one-page internal summary of this quarter's project status for a non-technical stakeholder audience. Structure it as: what shipped, what's delayed and why, and next steps. Here's my rough notes: [paste notes]."

This is one of the clearest workplace wins: turning scattered notes into a structured first draft, in the time it takes to describe what you need.

---

# Summarising Meetings

**A well-formed request:** "Here are my raw meeting notes [paste notes]. Summarise into: key decisions made, open questions, and action items with owners if mentioned."

Meeting notes are exactly the kind of unstructured, pattern-rich text AI handles well — the same "condense a long document" skill from Chapter 3, applied directly to a recurring workplace chore.

---

# Customer Support

AI can draft first-pass responses to common customer questions, summarise a long support thread into "here's what the customer actually needs," or help a support team maintain a consistent, on-brand tone across replies — always with a human reviewing before anything sensitive or unusual goes out.

---

# Presentations and Spreadsheet Help

For presentations, AI can turn a rough outline into structured slide content, or suggest a clearer way to sequence an argument. For spreadsheets, it can explain a formula, suggest how to structure a new tracking sheet, or help interpret what a block of data seems to be showing — genuinely useful, while final numbers still need the same human check any spreadsheet does.

---

# Research Assistance and Brainstorming

AI is well suited to a first pass on research — summarising background on a topic, generating an initial list of considerations for a decision, or brainstorming angles for a proposal — the same brainstorming and analysis strengths from Chapter 3, now aimed at work problems. As always, anything used to inform a real decision should be checked against a reliable source, not taken as the final word.

---

> **Example:** A project manager pastes a messy set of meeting notes into an AI tool with the request above, and gets back a clean list of decisions, open questions, and action items in under a minute — instead of spending twenty minutes writing it up manually after every meeting.

# Watch Out: Confidentiality and Company Policy

Before pasting anything into an AI tool at work, it's worth pausing on two practical questions.

**Confidentiality.** Would you be comfortable with this information — a client's details, an unreleased product plan, an employee's personal data — being stored somewhere outside your organisation's control? Many AI tools process what you type in ways that aren't the same as a private, internal document. If you wouldn't paste it into a public forum, don't paste it into an AI tool without knowing exactly how that tool handles your data.

**Company policy.** Many workplaces already have specific rules about which AI tools are approved for use, and for what kinds of information. Check before assuming any AI tool is fine to use for work content — this is a five-minute question to your manager or IT team, not a big undertaking, and it avoids a genuinely avoidable mistake.

> **Watch Out:** Confidentiality and company policy aren't obstacles to using AI at work — they're the same kind of common-sense check you'd already apply to emailing a sensitive document to the wrong address, or using an unapproved app for company data. A quick check up front avoids a real problem later.

---

# Watch Out: Verification at Work

Chapter 4 explained that AI can sound confident while being wrong. At work, that risk has higher stakes: a wrong number in a report, a misquoted policy in a customer reply, or a fabricated detail in a document someone else relies on can cause real damage to your credibility or your organisation's.

The practical rule: anything AI-assisted that will be seen by someone else — a client, a manager, the public — gets a human check before it goes out. Not because AI is untrustworthy in general, but because "sounds right" and "is right" are different things, and the cost of being wrong is higher when other people are relying on it.

# Myth vs Reality

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| AI at work is just a fun add-on, not a real productivity tool. | AI is a genuine workplace multiplier for reports, meeting notes, support, presentations, research, and more. |
| Any AI tool is safe to use with any work information. | Confidentiality and company policy vary by tool and by employer — check before you paste sensitive information in. |
| AI-assisted work output is ready to send as-is. | AI-assisted output should get the same human check as any other draft, especially anything client-facing or decision-relevant. |
| Using AI at work means less oversight, not more. | Using AI well at work usually means a quick upfront check (confidentiality, policy) and a quick downstream check (verification) — a small amount of added diligence, not less. |

---

# Core Takeaway

AI is becoming a workplace multiplier, not just a novelty.

Drafting, summarising, support, presentations, research, and brainstorming all get faster with a well-formed request. What makes that safe to rely on at work is the same three checks: know what you're allowed to paste in, know your company's policy, and verify anything before it goes out to someone else.

---

> **Recap:** AI genuinely multiplies professional output — reports, meeting notes, customer support, presentations, research, and brainstorming all benefit from the Chapter 5 pattern applied at work. The difference from home use is stakes, not skill: check confidentiality and company policy before you paste something in, and verify anything AI-assisted before it reaches someone else.
