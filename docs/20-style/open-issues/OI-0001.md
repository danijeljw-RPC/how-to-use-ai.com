# OI-0001 — Profanity in Author Reflections

## Status

Open

## Date Opened

2026-10-02

## Area

Style

## Question

Should strong language in the author's own reflections stay in the printed Book 1, be softened, or be masked?

## Why This Matters

Two finished author reflections, added word for word from the author's GitHub issue comments, contain profanity:

- Chapter 9 (issue #32): "*Hang on, there's no fucking way this person would say that.*", which appears twice, once at the start and once as the closing line.
- Chapter 11 (issue #31): "*That has CLEARLY got to be bullshit.*". The author uses it to show what they did *not* think.

Three more were added on 2026-10-03, also word for word:

- Chapter 7 (issue #22): "a beautifully formatted collection of bullshit", describing AI output that would have to be rebuilt from scratch.
- Chapter 14 (issue #23): "suddenly everyone should probably stop being quite so fucking excited", about agents given ambiguous, irreversible work.
- Chapter 14 (issue #23): "ask what the fuck it thinks it is doing", about a humanoid robot needing an engineer every forty minutes.

That makes five instances across four chapters (7, 9, 11 and 14). Whatever rule is chosen should be applied to all of them.

Book 1 is aimed at true beginners, and the style guide asks for a conversational, practical, "lightly humorous" tone. It does not say anything about profanity. Retailers, schools, libraries and workplace training buyers may treat strong language differently. The same text may also be adapted for the website and videos later.

## Current Assumptions

- The author's wording is kept exactly as written. Changing the author's voice is the author's decision, not an editorial default.
- Nothing has been changed or masked.

## Options

### Option 1 — Keep as written

Pros:

- An authentic voice. The Chapter 9 line is the emotional hook of that reflection.

Cons:

- May narrow the audience (schools, corporate training) and affect retailer content flags.

### Option 2 — Soften (e.g. "no way", "rubbish")

Pros:

- Suits every audience.

Cons:

- Loses some of the punch and authenticity.

### Option 3 — Mask (e.g. "f***ing")

Pros:

- Keeps the rhythm and makes the intent clear.

Cons:

- Masked words can read as coy, and they still trigger some content filters.

## Recommended Next Step

The author chooses an option. Record the result as a style ADR so later reflections follow the same rule.
