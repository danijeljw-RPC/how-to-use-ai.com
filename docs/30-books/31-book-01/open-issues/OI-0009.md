# OI-0009 — Similar Database Stories in the Chapter 4 and Chapter 12 Reflections

## Status

Resolved — 2026-10-04

## Date Opened

2026-10-03

## Area

Book 1

## Question

Do the Chapter 4 and Chapter 12 author reflections overlap too much, and if so, should either one be adjusted so they read as two distinct lessons?

## Why This Matters

Both reflections are the author's own, used word for word:

- **Chapter 4** (issue #25): AI-generated SQL to widen a text field was technically valid but ignored downstream processes that parsed the value. It closes: "The AI had written a valid database change. What it had not understood was the system."
- **Chapter 12** (issue #26, added 2026-10-03): a colleague followed instructions to purge a database schema that was not actually the problem. It closes on "Understanding the system well enough to know when the technically correct answer is solving the wrong problem."

They are different incidents with different lessons. Chapter 4 is about AI failing to reason across context it was given. Chapter 12 is about a person accepting a diagnosis and the skill of recognising the wrong problem. But a reader meets two database stories with very similar closing lines eight chapters apart, and may feel the book is repeating itself.

## Current Assumptions

- Both reflections are kept exactly as written.
- No cross-reference has been added.

## Options

### Option 1 — Keep both as they are

Pros:

- Each one fits its chapter. Repetition of a core lesson can help beginners.

Cons:

- Risk of déjà vu, especially in the closing lines.

### Option 2 — Add a one-line link in Chapter 12's lead-in

For example, a sentence before the reflection noting that Chapter 4's database story was about what AI missed, and this one is about what people miss.

Pros:

- Turns the echo into a deliberate callback. The author's words are unchanged.

Cons:

- Adds a non-author sentence that the author should approve.

### Option 3 — The author revises one closing line

Pros:

- Removes the echo at its strongest point.

Cons:

- Changes the author's wording, which is the author's decision.

## Recommended Next Step

The author reads both reflections together and chooses an option.

## Resolution

The author kept the Chapter 4 database story and replaced the Chapter 12 reflection with a different story, about AI producing a competent but empty draft from a research package and what that taught the author about recognising the right result (the author's own edit to `chapter-12-how-to-stay-relevant-in-the-ai-era.md`, still uncommitted in the main checkout when this OI was closed). The two reflections no longer overlap. The new Chapter 12 story overlaps with the Chapter 8 reflection instead; that is tracked in `OI-0010.md`.
