## Priority 1 — Needs Confirmation Before It Can Stand

These claims are in the manuscript but are **not** fully supported by the two research packages. They came from general knowledge or had a citation problem.

### 1. The 2025 trial ruling in the Anthropic case

- [x] Resolved on 2026-10-04: confirmed against the *Bartz v. Anthropic PBC* Order on Fair Use (No. 3:24-cv-05417-WHA, N.D. Cal., 23 June 2025, Alsup J.), now cited in `ch8-us-cases`. Final approval of the US$1.5 billion settlement on 20 July 2026 (Martínez-Olguín J., after Alsup J. retired) confirmed via JURIST. The text stands.

- **Where:** "Why the Answer Changes at the Border", United States paragraph (about line 313).
- **Text:** "the trial judge ruled in 2025 that training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, but that building a library from pirated copies was a separate matter"
- **Why flagged:** The research packages only cover the US$1.5 billion settlement and its final approval, from an Authors Guild summary. The description of the earlier ruling came from general knowledge of the June 2025 order in *Bartz v. Anthropic*.
- **What to check:** The court's summary-judgment order (N.D. Cal., June 2025), and that the settlement figure and final-approval date match the court record, not only the Authors Guild summary.
- **If it can't be confirmed:** Delete the clause about the trial ruling and keep only the settlement sentence.

### 2. Australia's lack of a general "right of publicity"

- [x] Resolved on 2026-10-04: sourced to the Arts Law Centre of Australia information sheet "Unauthorised use of your image" (no specific Australian law equivalent to the US right of publicity; passing off, the ACL and defamation are the routes). Added to `ch8-replicas` and the bibliography. The text stands.

- **Where:** "Music and Audio", voice-cloning paragraph (about line 125).
- **Text:** "Australia, for instance, has no single general 'right of publicity' of the kind recognised in some US states; complaints about the unauthorised use of someone's identity tend to be argued through other routes, such as passing off or misleading-conduct law."
- **Why flagged:** This is standard Australian legal background, but it has no source in either research package and no footnote.
- **What to check:** Find a citable source, such as an Arts Law Centre information sheet on personality or publicity rights, or ACCC guidance on misleading endorsements. Add it to the `ch8-replicas` footnote and the bibliography.
- **If it can't be sourced:** Soften it to "Rules protecting a person's identity differ from country to country" and drop the Australia-specific detail.

### 3. Author names for the image-bias study

The abstract (Stable Diffusion; 32 professions; racial and gender stereotypes) still supports the manuscript sentence. Footnote and bibliography corrected.

- **Where:** Footnote `ch8-bias` (about line 534).
- **Text:** "AlDahoul, Rahwan and Zaki"
- **Why flagged:** Earlier drafts gave the authors' given names inconsistently ("Zubair Rahwan" in the bibliography). Surnames only are used until confirmed.
- **What to check:** The *Scientific Reports* article page (<https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-99623-3>). Correct both the footnote and the bibliography.

### Priority 2 Recheck Results (2026-10-04)

- **Australia:** confirmed. The government is not considering a TDM exception (October 2025, restated since), and CAIRG is focused on licensing. No change.
- **ROSS:** the opinion (29 September 2026) was unsealed days later. Chapter updated with its reasoning (commercial, minimally transformative, harm to an AI-training licensing market; generative models distinguished). ROSS says it will seek Supreme Court review, so recheck at lock.
- **Copyright Office Part 3:** still the pre-publication version. No change.
- **Newer US decisions:** none that outdate the two-case illustration (Kadrey v. Meta, June 2025, predates it). No change.
- **EU:** GPAI obligations have applied since 2 August 2025, with Commission enforcement powers from 2 August 2026. The Digital Omnibus delayed only the high-risk rules. No change.
- **UK:** the March 2026 report and the May 2026 response confirm the opt-out is no longer preferred; removing computer-generated-works protection is still only proposed. No change.
- **SAG-AFTRA:** "clear and conspicuous", reasonably specific, separate consent is confirmed across contracts. No change.
- **Suno:** wording changed. The help centre now says paid downloads are "granted commercial use rights" and no longer calls that ownership (terms effective 3 September 2026). Free-plan songs are personal, non-commercial use. Chapter sentence corrected; the ownership table already said "commercial-use rights".
- **Canva:** AI Product Terms (updated 26 June 2026) still say you own your Output "as between you and Canva" and that outputs may not be unique. No change.
- **Adobe Firefly:** the contributor FAQ (updated 16 September 2026) was already current. No change.
- **Adobe Content Credentials:** still applied automatically to 100%-Firefly-generated assets. No change.
- **OpenAI:** C2PA metadata plus an invisible SynthID watermark on new images (announced 19 May 2026). Footnote date added.
- **Premiere Generative Extend and Canva Magic Design:** both still exist and work as described. No change.
- **KDP:** still requires disclosure of AI-generated, not AI-assisted, content. No change.
- **YouTube:** the disclosure policy and its examples (another person's cloned voice, AI-generated music) still apply. No change.
- **Deezer:** no newer official figure than the July 2026 release; the chapter and `creative-abundance-bottleneck.mmd` stand.
- **Spotify:** the September 2025 "75 million spammy tracks" figure is historical and correctly attributed. No change.
- **UMG–Udio and WMG–Suno:** Suno v6 launched on 9 September 2026 with licensed WMG, BMG and Believe catalogues; the Udio platform is announced but not confirmed as launched. Chapter sentence and footnote updated.
- **Anthropic settlement:** see Priority 1, item 1.
