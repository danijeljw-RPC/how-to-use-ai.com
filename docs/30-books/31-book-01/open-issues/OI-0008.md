# OI-0008 — Technical Depth of the Chapter 3 CurseDelete Reflection

## Status

Open

## Date Opened

2026-10-03

## Area

Book 1

## Question

Should the Chapter 3 author reflection keep its operating-system detail, or should some of it be simplified or moved for Book 1's non-technical reader?

## Why This Matters

The Chapter 3 reflection (GitHub issue #21, added word for word on 2026-10-03) tells how AI helped the author rebuild CurseDelete in Rust. Its lesson is clear for any reader: AI got the author to "80 or 90 per cent of the answer" quickly, and the rest of the engineering, judgement and testing was still the author's job.

But it also names system calls and tools (`openat`, `unlinkat`, `fstatat`, `statx`, `getdents64`, `readdir`, `/proc/[pid]/fd`, `lsof`, Restart Manager) and terms such as TOCTOU and producer-and-consumer pipeline. CLAUDE.md says Book 1 must be "truly non-technical" and must not assume the reader knows coding. At about 145 lines, it is also the longest reflection in the chapter.

Some of this is helped by the author's own humour and plain explanations. TOCTOU and birth timestamps are both explained in passing. Still, a beginner may skim the middle.

## Current Assumptions

- The text is kept exactly as written. Shortening or simplifying the author's voice is the author's decision.
- Nothing has been changed.

## Options

### Option 1 — Keep as written

Pros:

- Authentic, specific and credible. Shows professional readers (the 35 per cent) a real engineering case.

Cons:

- Dense for true beginners.

### Option 2 — Light trim in the book, full version on the companion website

Keep the story, the timestamp decision, "benchmark first" and the 80–90 per cent lesson in print. Move the system-call names to the website or an endnote.

Pros:

- Keeps the lesson and the voice, and makes the print version easier to read.

Cons:

- Needs the author to approve a cut.

### Option 3 — Add a short Plain English callout after the reflection

Pros:

- No change to the author's words.

Cons:

- Adds length to an already long section.

## Recommended Next Step

The author reads the reflection in the next draft PDF and chooses an option.
