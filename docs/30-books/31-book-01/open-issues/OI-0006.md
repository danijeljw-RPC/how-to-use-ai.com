# OI-0006 — Chapter 14 Must Use Chapter 11's Revised Evaluation Method

## Status

Resolved — 2026-10-03

## Date Opened

2026-10-01

## Area

Book 1

## Question

The current Chapter 14 template (`docs/30-books/31-book-01/chapters/chapter-14-where-ai-goes-next.md`) refers to Chapter 11's **old** three-question method: "a demo-stage possibility, a maturing product, or a settled outcome?" and "Who benefits if you believe a stronger version of these claims?". Chapter 11 now teaches five questions, adds **forecast** as its own claim type, and treats "who benefits?" as setting verification effort rather than deciding truth. How should Chapter 14 be updated?

## Why This Matters

The Chapter 14 plan says the chapter must visibly apply Chapter 11's method to its own forward-looking claims. If it quotes a method the reader was never taught, the book's main cross-chapter device breaks. The old "who benefits?" wording also risks teaching the motive reasoning Chapter 11 warns against.

## Current Assumptions

- Chapter 14 was not edited in this run (out of scope for the Chapter 11 task).
- The five questions are: What kind of claim is this? What exactly is claimed, and compared with what? Where did it come from? Who else has checked it? What would change my mind?
- For forecasts, Chapter 11 asks for a clear definition, a date and an observable sign.

## Options

### Option 1 — Update Chapter 14 during its research-backed rewrite (recommended)

Rewrite Chapter 14's Watch Out and "Why This Chapter Avoids Hard Predictions" section to use the five questions, and label each forward-looking claim as current capability, announced or deploying, forecast, or speculative scenario (research package file 15 §12).

### Option 2 — Patch the Watch Out callout now

A small edit to Chapter 14's existing callout. Quick, but the rest of the template would still predate the method.

## Resolution

Done as Option 1 in the research-backed Chapter 14 rewrite (commit c1b5657). Chapter 14 now asks readers to run its claims through Chapter 11's five questions, labels claims as demonstrations, deployments, forecasts or scenarios ("Scenarios Are Not Forecasts"), and treats "who has a stake?" as a reason to check more carefully, not as a verdict.
