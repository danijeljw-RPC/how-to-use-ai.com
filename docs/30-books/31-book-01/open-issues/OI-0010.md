# OI-0010 — Overlap Between the Chapter 8 and Chapter 12 Reflections on Writing This Book

## Status

Open

## Date Opened

2026-10-04

## Area

Book 1

## Question

The new Chapter 12 reflection and the Chapter 8 reflection both tell the story of how the author uses AI to write this book. Should one of them change, so the two read as distinct lessons and so what they say about AI's role in the writing is consistent?

## Why This Matters

The author replaced the Chapter 12 database story to avoid repeating Chapter 4 (OI-0009). The new story raises two issues of its own:

1. **Repetition.** Both reflections open by listing how the author uses AI around the work. Chapter 8: "I use it to research subjects, find sources, challenge claims I've made…, look for opposing arguments, poke holes in my reasoning…". Chapter 12: "I use it to research subjects, find primary sources, compare studies, challenge claims, look for opposing evidence, identify gaps…". A reader meets nearly the same list four chapters apart.
2. **Consistency about AI drafting.** Chapter 12 says the author gave "that material, the chapter plan and the surrounding book context to an AI system", got back a "clean", "organised" but "underwhelming" draft, and then worked with the AI ("send it back into the research…, expand an example") until the chapter did its job. Chapter 8 says: "I didn't want AI to write the book for me", "What I don't want is to become the editor of a book that an AI wrote", and that generating substantial parts of the finished book with AI is something "I would want the reader to know". Read together, a careful reader may ask whether Chapter 12 describes exactly what Chapter 8 rules out, or why it isn't disclosed.

The book's own disclosure practice (ADR-04-0003) and the epilogue's note ("developed from the author's viewpoint with drafting assistance from Claude") make the second point worth settling deliberately.

The lessons themselves are different. Chapter 8 is about using AI to think, not to write. Chapter 12 is about recognising the right result and being able to explain why a competent draft fails. It is the shared framing that overlaps.

## Current Assumptions

- Both reflections are kept exactly as written. Changing the author's words is the author's decision.
- The Chapter 12 edit is the author's own. When this OI was opened, it was an uncommitted change in the main checkout.

## Options

### Option 1 — Keep both as written

Pros:

- Both are authentic, and each lands its own lesson.

Cons:

- The repeated opening list, and a possible contradiction about AI drafting.

### Option 2 — Trim Chapter 12's opening list

Cut or shorten the "I use it to research subjects…" paragraph in Chapter 12, perhaps replacing it with a one-line nod back to Chapter 8.

Pros:

- Removes the most visible repetition with a small change.

Cons:

- Leaves the drafting-consistency question.

### Option 3 — Reconcile the drafting claim

Adjust either reflection so they agree on what AI did in the writing. For example, Chapter 12 could make clear that the AI draft was an experiment that failed and was not used, or Chapter 8 could acknowledge drafting assistance in line with the book's disclosure note.

Pros:

- Makes the book's account of its own process consistent, which matters in a book about trusting AI.

Cons:

- Changes the author's wording; the author has to decide what is accurate.

## Recommended Next Step

The author reads both reflections together, chooses an option, and checks that the result matches the book's AI-assistance disclosure (ADR-04-0003).
