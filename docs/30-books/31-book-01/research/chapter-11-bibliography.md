# Chapter 11 Bibliography and Evidence Record

## Purpose and Scope

This file records the evidence materially used in `docs/30-books/31-book-01/chapters/chapter-11-ai-hype-vs-reality.md`.

The manuscript synthesises the Chapter 11 research package at `docs/80-research/chapter-11-research-package/` (files `00-readme.md` to `15-additional-findings-and-boundaries.md`, plus `source-material/`). The package's source catalogue (`12-source-catalogue.md`) remains the fuller working bibliography, and its real-world examples file (`08-real-world-examples.md`) holds the named-case detail. This file records only what reached the manuscript, how it was used, how it was anonymised in the prose, its limits and its recheck needs. The manuscript uses Markdown footnotes (`[^ch11-…]`).

Research package date and time-sensitive spot check: **1 October 2026 (Australia/Adelaide)**.

This is editorial research, not legal or financial advice.

## Evidence Decisions

- **Generic prose, named endnotes.** The approved plan forbids naming companies, products or individuals in the printed prose. Every real case is described generically in the text; the footnotes identify the source so the claim can be audited. Regulators and public institutions (SEC, FTC, ASIC, Treasury, TEQSA, the International AI Safety Report) are named because they are evidence sources, not targets.
- **Five claim types, not three.** Forecast was added to demo / product / research result / company announcement (with anecdote as an aside), following package files 01, 06 and 10 (Observation 8). This is the change Chapter 14 depends on.
- **Possible → dependable ladder** (possible, repeatable, reliable, available, affordable, safe to depend on) adopted from package file 01 §5 and 11 §7.
- **Operating envelope** replaces the outdated self-driving example (package 10, Observation 10).
- **Evaluation method rebuilt as five questions:** What kind of claim? What exactly, compared with what? Where did it come from? Who else has checked it (with incentives as verification effort, not verdict)? What would change my mind (definition, date, observable sign for forecasts)? Built from lateral reading (Wineburg and McGrew), SIFT, prebunking logic and package files 06 §7–9 and 11 §8–10. SIFT and the DATE mnemonic are not named in print.
- **"Who benefits?"** survives inside question 4 and in a Watch Out callout, explicitly framed as setting verification effort rather than deciding truth.
- **Demo vocabulary.** "Fake" is reserved for fabricated output. The multimodal demo is described as assembled from still images and text prompts, following the company's own explanation, with the capability described as real.
- **Survey wording.** The expert survey is described with sample, framing sensitivity and "at least a 10 per cent chance" wording; it is never described as consensus or as the share who believe extinction will happen.
- **Forecasts labelled as forecasts** throughout (AGI timelines, disease forecasts, bubble claims, investment expectations).
- **Consciousness.** Chapter 11 defers to Chapter 4's house position (no conversational test; science has not proved impossibility) and does not reopen the debate.
- **Analogies.** Horses-to-cars is explained and rejected; factory electrification is used with its limits; "calculator for words" is used with its break stated and its source in the endnote; the microwave is used only for "unfamiliar technology becomes ordinary" with its break stated; satnav is offered as the better everyday analogy. "Bicycle for the mind" and the Gartner Hype Cycle were considered and **not used** (an extra framework competing with the chapter's own method; naming rule). The Amara heuristic appears once, unattributed in prose, as a heuristic.
- **Continuity.** METR, the jagged frontier, customer-support and electricity-productivity material is referenced back to Chapters 7 and 10 rather than retold. AI-writing detectors are not repeated (Chapter 9 covers them). Lateral reading is presented as the habit Chapter 9 introduced.

## Corrections and Refinements to the Research Package

- **"95 per cent" report wording.** Checked against the public copy of the report on 1 October 2026. The package summarised the report as using "multiple methods and outcome concepts". The text shows the inflation began partly in the report itself: the executive summary says "95% of organizations are getting zero return", while section 3.2 reports 5 per cent of *custom* enterprise tools reaching production, ~83 per cent pilot-to-implementation for general-purpose chatbots, a success definition based on what "users or executives have remarked", and a limitation that figures are "directionally accurate based on individual interviews rather than official company reporting". Section 8.2 gives a different success definition ("deployment beyond pilot phase with measurable KPIs"). The worked example uses these verified details.
- **Bar-exam percentiles.** Package gave no figures; the manuscript uses Martínez's abstract figures (≈68th July; ≈63rd first-time, 42nd essays; ≈48th among passers, 15th essays) and the February/repeat-taker explanation.
- **Driverless ride-hailing.** Confirmed 14 US cities on 1 September 2026 (company source). The package's later "15 cities" figure was not independently confirmed; print says "more than a dozen".
- **Expert survey.** Confirmed 2,778 respondents and "between 38% and 51%" wording from the abstract; the thirteen-year shift (2060 → 2047) is attributed to the survey rounds without asserting its cause.
- **International AI Safety Report.** Quotations ("jagged"; "already materialising"; "100 independent experts … more than 30 countries") checked against the executive summary.
- **Student study.** Confirmed ~1,000 students, +48 per cent / +127 per cent practice, −17 per cent unassisted for the generic interface.
