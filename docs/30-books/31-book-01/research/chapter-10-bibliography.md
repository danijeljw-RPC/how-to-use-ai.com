## Evidence Decisions

- The chapter's organising idea is that AI meets work **task by task**. Exposure, adoption, workflow change, complement-or-substitute, demand and labour-market impact are kept as separate links (diagram `ai-capability-to-labour-impact.mmd`). A yes at one link is never treated as proof of the next.
- Every exposure figure (IMF, ILO, Eloundou et al.) is labelled as exposure and immediately distinguished from job loss. The ILO's April 2026 warning is quoted.
- WEF *Future of Jobs* figures are labelled as employer expectations plus modelling of several macro trends, not AI job creation.
- Frey and Osborne versus Arntz, Gregory and Zierahn is used only to teach that method changes the headline ("What exactly did they count?"), not as a current forecast.
- Productivity studies are described with their population, task and period. None is used as an employment forecast. The writing, consultant, customer-support and METR studies are presented as callbacks to Chapter 7 and are used here for the labour-market question.
- Customer-support figures follow the published *QJE* version (5,172 agents, about 15 per cent), as Chapter 7 does, rather than the package's working-paper figures (5,179 agents, 14 per cent, 34 per cent for novices). No novice percentage is quoted.
- Observed labour-market evidence (Stanford payroll, Danish administrative data, ABS, Productivity Commission) is distinguished from experiments, exposure indices, surveys, forecasts, company filings and provider telemetry.
- Freelance-platform studies are described as platform evidence and early-warning signals, not national employment data. Detailed figures are cross-referenced to Chapter 8 rather than re-explained.
- Klarna figures are attributed to the company's filing; "work equivalent of over 850 full-time agents" is described as a company estimate, not verified layoffs. The manuscript does not claim human support remains available, because the filing text searched on 1 October 2026 did not state it (see Corrections).
- Anthropic usage data is described as one provider's telemetry using its own classification. The manuscript reports how the shares moved between three reports rather than a single figure.
- Entry-level evidence is presented as an emerging, observational concern. The 41-country paper is used to show that a falling junior *share* can come from faster senior growth.
- Historical evidence is used for mechanisms and costs: Luddites (distribution, not technophobia), Engels' pause (productivity versus wages), robots (local displacement), French robots (firm gain versus industry loss), displaced-worker losses (Watch Out callout), new work since 1940 (with its changing composition). The ATM example is kept short and explicitly bounded.
- Australian evidence is a dedicated subsection: ABS adoption (adoption, not displacement), JSA (augmentation more likely, not a guarantee), Productivity Commission (no visible aggregate boom), Fair Work consultation (institutions; not legal advice), ABS and FWO transparency statements (augmentation with human accountability).
- No occupation is labelled safe or doomed. Non-office examples (receptionist, electrician, aged-care worker) are used for mixed task exposure.
- The self-assessment keeps the effort/judgement heuristic, adds ten questions and four output groups, and explicitly rejects automation-probability scores.
- Adaptability is framed as increasing controllable options, not as protection, with explicit discussion of structural constraints and institutional responsibility.
- No personal experience is attributed to the author. The receptionist, claims team and self-assessment examples are labelled as teaching illustrations in the Chapter Notes.

## Corrections and Updates to the Research Package

- **Folder name.** The drafting prompt referred to `docs/80-research/chapter-109-research-package`; the package is at `docs/80-research/chapter-10-research-package`.
- **Anthropic Economic Index.** The package cites the February 2025 split (57 per cent augmentation, 43 per cent automation on Claude.ai) and an unspecified API skew. Checked 1 October 2026: the September 2025 report found automation (49.1 per cent) slightly ahead of augmentation (47 per cent) on Claude.ai, and 77 per cent of first-party API conversations showing automation patterns; the January 2026 report (November 2025 data) found augmentation at 52 per cent and automation at 45 per cent on Claude.ai. The January 2026 API share could not be confirmed consistently (a page summary gave about 64 per cent, a search snippet about 75 per cent), so the manuscript quotes only the September 2025 API figure.
- **Klarna.** The package says "human support remains available". The 2025 Form 20-F text searched on 1 October 2026 confirms the 80 per cent chat share, the "work equivalent of over 850 full-time agents" estimate (based on the reduction in agent-handled chat and telephone conversations) and about US$59 million in 2025 cost savings, and compares AI-handled chats with human agents on internal satisfaction surveys. It did not explicitly state that human support remains available, so the manuscript does not claim it.
- **Customer-support study.** See Evidence Decisions: published-version figures used for consistency with Chapter 7.
- **Randall citation.** The package's DOI (`10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199259908.003.0000`) points to an Oxford Academic chapter, not to Randall's 1991 Cambridge book *Before the Luddites*. The footnote cites the DOI as given and flags the book and chapter details for confirmation.
- **Fair Work consultation.** Wording narrowed to "modern awards and enterprise agreements generally contain consultation terms, and enterprise agreements must include them", consistent with the FWO guide as summarised in the package.
