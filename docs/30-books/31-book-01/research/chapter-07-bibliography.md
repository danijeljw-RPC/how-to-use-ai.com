## Source Entries and Manuscript Claim Mapping

### Writing Experiment — ch7-writing

Shakked Noy and Whitney Zhang. “Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence.” *Science* 381 (2023), 187–192. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586>.

- Type: peer-reviewed preregistered experiment.
- Supports: 453 college-educated professionals; assigned mid-level writing tasks; approximately 40% lower completion time and 18% higher assessed quality.
- Use: opening evidence for substantial drafting benefits, with experimental-task limitation.
- Check: both research packages and the existing Chapter 6 bibliography agree; MIT's report of its own study independently confirms the sample and effects: <https://news.mit.edu/2023/study-finds-chatgpt-boosts-worker-productivity-writing-0714>. Publisher full-text access failed in this pass. No additional findings beyond these records are introduced.
- Limits: 2023 tools and bounded writing tasks; not a claim about end-to-end organisational reporting or every profession.

### Consultant Experiment — ch7-frontier

Fabrizio Dell'Acqua et al. “Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality.” *Organization Science* 37 (2026), 403–423. <https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838>. HBS research overview: <https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/>.

- Type: experiment with professionals; journal publication metadata supplied by package-01.
- Supports: 758 consultants; higher task completion, quality and speed within the tested frontier; worse correctness on a task outside it.
- Use: opening explanation of uneven task fit.
- Check: primary HBS overview and HBS-hosted published article retrieved; the article confirms sample and positive/negative task effects. Published article: <https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf>. Manuscript uses a rounded speed figure and the direction of the adverse result to keep the opening readable.
- Limits: GPT-4-era task selection; neither the frontier nor measured gains are universal or permanent.

### Customer-Support Deployment — ch7-support

Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey R. Raymond. “Generative AI at Work.” *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 140 (2025), 889–942. <https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044>. Author manuscript: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771>. Earlier working-paper landing page: <https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161>.

- Type: real-workplace staggered deployment / quasi-experimental evidence; not described as a randomised trial.
- Supports: 5,172 agents; about 15% higher issues resolved per hour; experience-dependent speed/quality effects; evidence consistent with learning.
- Use: support benefits, junior/expert differences and qualified learning discussion.
- Check: current author abstract and publisher-indexed material confirm the published-version figures and heterogeneous effects. NBER landing page retrieved directly still reports 5,179 / 14% / 34% novice-worker gain.
- Version decision: use the published-version sample and effect together. Package-02's older figures are not a replacement for package-01's final-paper figures. Do not mix the versions or describe the discrepancy as contradictory experiments.
- Limits: one organisation/tool context. Learning evidence does not prove every assisted task develops durable skill. Higher throughput is not automatic authority to issue remedies.

### Integrated Workplace Assistant — ch7-workpatterns

Eleanor W. Dillon, Sonia Jaffe, Nicole Immorlica and Christopher T. Stanton. “Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI.” NBER Working Paper 33795, May 2025; revised November 2025. <https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795>.

- Type: randomised field experiment reported in a working paper.
- Supports: six months, 66 firms, 7,137 workers; about two fewer hours of email weekly among treated users in the later period; no detected broader quantity/composition shifts from individual-level provision.
- Use: modest, activity-specific gain without whole-job transformation.
- Check: NBER abstract and disclosures retrieved directly.
- Version decision: use package-02's revised paper, not package-01's earlier Microsoft summary reporting 6,000 workers and roughly three hours. The estimate for users is not the effect for everyone assigned access.
- Limits: working paper, Microsoft product setting and author employment relationships; email time is not a complete output/quality measure.

### Experienced Developers and Follow-Up — ch7-metr, ch7-metr-update

Joel Becker, Nate Rush, Beth Barnes and David Rein. METR, “Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity,” 10 July 2025. <https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/>.

METR. “We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design,” 24 February 2026. <https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/>.

- Type: primary research-organisation account of a randomised study; methodological follow-up.
- Supports: 16 experienced developers, 246 real tasks, 19% longer completion with AI allowed; perceived speed-up despite slowdown.
- Use: perception versus completed-work measurement.
- Check: both primary pages retrieved. Original page links to the newer update; follow-up is additional verification beyond the packages.
- Follow-up qualification: selection out of no-AI work, lower compensation and concurrent-agent time measurement complicate the later experiment. Researchers consider increased benefit plausible but their newer data weak evidence for its size. Do not present original slowdown as a current universal result or later estimates as a clean reversal.
- Metadata: use Beth Barnes as shown on the primary original page; package-02's abbreviated author metadata differs.

### Job Advertisements — ch7-jobposts

Emma Wiles and John J. Horton. *More, but Worse: The Marketplace Effects of AI-Generated Job Posts*. Working paper, 4 September 2026. <https://emmawiles.github.io/storage/jobot.pdf>.

- Type: author-hosted working paper reporting a large field experiment.
- Supports: 44% less employer writing time, 19% more posting, no discernible increase in matches, more generic and less informative posts.
- Use: downstream usefulness can diverge from local generation efficiency; retain concrete role details.
- Check: current author PDF retrieved and abstract/introduction inspected. The title/date differ from the packages' older *Generative AI and Labor Market Matching Efficiency* reference. No research-package files were altered.
- Limits: one online marketplace intervention, working-paper status, not every AI-edited job ad. Do not conflate generative substitution with editing a human-written draft. Manuscript does not use the secondary report's 15% hiring figure or make labour-market forecasts.

### Meeting Summaries — ch7-meetings

- Type: meeting-summarisation research and human-annotated error dataset.

- Use: why plausible sentences alone do not establish a faithful record.

### Ideation and Diversity Debate — ch7-ideation

### Creative Writing — ch7-creativity

## Editorial Verification and Exclusions

- All eight required example categories have contextual requests, source boundaries, explanation and concrete checks. Meeting raw notes, good record and bad summary are new fictional material rather than copied package scaffolding.
- Arithmetic: location completion is 120/150 = 80% of that period's locations, not the project; revenue variance examples use (actual − target)/target; combined revenue variance is 20/1,000 = 2%, not the unweighted mean of branch percentages. Blank/zero behaviours are explicit business rules.
- The workday timings are synthetic: 6 + 3 + 5 + 6 = 20 minutes against a 30-minute baseline. They are not author experience or research results.
- Three original Mermaid diagrams now teach distinctions within the worked examples: meeting estimates versus commitments, support preparation versus remedy authority, and full-task time accounting. The HR and induction scenarios are synthetic applications of the same documented techniques; no additional product or empirical claim is introduced.
- OECD adoption statistics, detailed governance, NIST taxonomy, cybersecurity mechanics, court cases and product catalogues are omitted to protect the practical scope. Their exclusion does not discard either package or weaken the contradictory productivity evidence retained.
- The author reflection remains explicit. All other examples are labelled illustrations. Prompt constraints are presented as aids to review, not guarantees against invented information.
