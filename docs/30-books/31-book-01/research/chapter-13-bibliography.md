# Chapter 13 Bibliography and Evidence Record

## Purpose and Scope

This file records the evidence materially used in `docs/30-books/31-book-01/chapters/chapter-13-building-your-personal-ai-toolkit.md`.

The manuscript synthesises the Chapter 13 research package at `docs/80-research/chapter-13-research-package/` (README, `inputs/`, and `research/00-readme.md` to `13-recheck-before-publication.md`). The package's source catalogue (`12-source-catalogue.md`) remains the fuller working bibliography, its real-world examples file (`07-real-world-examples.md`) holds case detail, and `11-companion-website-material.md` holds the named, dated product detail deliberately kept out of print. This file records only what reached the manuscript, how it was used, its evidence type and limits, and its recheck needs. The manuscript uses Markdown footnotes (`[^ch13-…]`).

Research package date and spot check: **1 October 2026 (Australia/Adelaide)**.

This is editorial research, not legal, financial or security advice.

## Evidence Decisions

- **Products generic in prose.** Per the chapter plan and package 00, no product is named, ranked or endorsed in the main text. Providers are identified only in endnotes, to establish principles (rebrand, model retirement, training vs storage, temporary-mode retention, human-review retention, consumer vs commercial terms, on-device vs cloud). Chapter 6's dated named-tool map is referenced as the beginner's doorway; current names live on the companion website.
- **Literacy qualified.** "Literacy over loyalty" is defined as examined loyalty (package 01 §1, 08 §1; observation 25): staying can be rational; literacy is knowing why and being able to re-evaluate and leave.
- **Six categories as kinds of help.** All six plan categories kept as H3 sections, framed as capabilities, with convergence and embedded AI explained (package 02; observations 2–8). Adjacent categories folded in under "A Map, Not a Taxonomy"; agents and prompt injection kept to one short present-day paragraph (Chapter 14 boundary).
- **Two-tier evaluation.** First-pass check (need, maker, data, result, cost and exit) plus a deeper check triggered by payment, sensitive data, connected accounts, extensions, consequential reliance, actions or child use (package 03 §14, 10 §3; observation 10). Named distinctly from Chapter 11's five questions for claims.
- **Privacy as several questions.** A six-row reading table (stored, trained, remembered, reviewed, shared, deleted/exported) builds on Chapter 9's list rather than repeating it; training ≠ storage, temporary ≠ zero, deleting a chat ≠ deleting everything, consumer vs business vs on-device (package 04 §4–10).
- **Australian law proportionate.** APP coverage, small-business exemption, access/correction rights and no general erasure right; OAIC AI guidance as an organisational default; Children's Online Privacy Code pending; Unfair Trading Practices Act passed but not commenced (1 July 2027). Jurisdiction and "not legal advice" stated.
- **Security incidents not prevalence.** Fleeceware (2023), malicious extension (2023) and fake AI video sites (2025) are described as documented cases; the chapter explicitly declines both "most AI apps are scams" and "scams are negligible" (package 06 §18; observations 18–19).
- **Wrappers neutral.** Defined, defended as normal architecture, with conduct-based concerns and a "what am I paying this company to add?" test (package 06 §12; observation 14).
- **Callouts.** Per ADR-04-0002: Key Idea, Watch Out (scam/subscription red flags), Try This (one-task evaluation exercise), Recap (with reader-reflection questions). Myth vs Reality is an H2 section, as in Chapters 11 and 12.
- **Diagram and tables.** New task-first Mermaid diagram `diagrams/toolkit-task-first-decision.mmd` (replaces the plan's six-bubble placeholder, per observation 24 and package 10 §1; rendered with mmdc on 1 October 2026). Two tables: six kinds of help at a glance; six privacy questions.
- **Teaching illustrations.** The two people in the opening, the club-committee recording task and the $9.99 weekly price are invented illustrations; no author experience invented.
- **Not used in prose:** Mozilla companion-app review (eSafety evidence stronger and Australian); US FTC fake-review and click-to-cancel material (jurisdiction; Australian sources sufficient); EU AI Act literacy pages; ING subscription surveys and Westpac figures beyond a general reference; OpenAI usage telemetry; Vision Australia resources (Chapter 6 already carries accessibility depth; website candidate); Microsoft 365 / Google AI plan pricing (website only).

## Corrections and Refinements to the Research Package

- **ACCC JustAnswer matter.** Checked 1 October 2026: release dated 8 July 2026; $10 million penalties; "AU$2" offers between November 2022 and August 2025 enrolled consumers in ongoing monthly subscriptions of $45–$75; refunds ordered. Also included misleading affiliation claims (not used). Described generically in prose as "an online question-and-answer service".
- **Unfair Trading Practices reforms.** Treasury release checked 1 October 2026: bill passed (release dated 2 July 2026), protections start 1 July 2027 after a 12-month preparation period. The Federal Register page shows a status of "In force" without commencement detail; as the package warned, the manuscript treats the provisions as enacted but not yet operative. The package gives assent as 6 July 2026; not independently confirmed in this run.
- **eSafety survey.** Page timed out twice on 1 October 2026. Figures (1,950 children aged 10–17; 78% assistant, 8% companion; among users 32% shared personal/sensitive information, 20% potentially harmful interactions; over half personal/social use) are from the package and match Chapter 9. Mandatory recheck.
- **Overlap with earlier chapters.** Chapter 9 already used the eSafety 32%/20% figures, OAIC guidance and Gemini human-review retention; Chapter 13 reuses the same sources and figures, cross-referencing rather than re-explaining.
