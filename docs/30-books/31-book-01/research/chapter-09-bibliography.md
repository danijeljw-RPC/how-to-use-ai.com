## Evidence Decisions

- Every risk is framed with four separate questions: capability, prevalence, impact and response. A yes at one stage is never treated as proof of the next.
- Risks are described as "old problem, new economics" unless the evidence supports a genuinely new harm (synthetic sexual imagery of real people is treated as the clearest case).
- Australian scam totals are always labelled as **all reported scams**, never AI scams. No AI-specific national loss figure exists and none is implied.
- Data-centre electricity is never relabelled as AI electricity. Forecasts and projections are labelled as such.
- Company sources (OpenAI threat reports, Google per-prompt figures, provider privacy pages and terms) are attributed to the company and treated as evidence of what that company observes, measures or offers, not as independent or ecosystem-wide measurement.
- Regulator report and complaint counts (eSafety, ASIC, NASC) are described as reports or enforcement, not prevalence.
- Robodebt is explicitly described as automation, not AI.
- Deepfake advice favours source verification and separate-channel verification over visual "tells", following the 2024 meta-analysis.
- Copyright is treated as unresolved risk and ordinary-user implications only; Chapter 8 carries the full debate.
- Concentration is described by layer of the industry; competition and concentration are presented as coexisting.
- The individual / organisational / collective safeguard model replaces the plan's individual-versus-society diagram.
- The Bunnings description follows the OAIC's statement on the Tribunal decision, which the research package summarised incompletely (see Corrections).

## Corrections to the Research Package

- **Bunnings (ART, 4 February 2026).** The research package said the Tribunal affirmed the APP 1 and APP 5 findings and "examined" the consent exception. The OAIC statement, checked 1 October 2026, says the Tribunal **set aside** the APP 3.3 collection finding and decided Bunnings was entitled to rely on exceptions to consent for the limited purpose of combating retail crime and protecting staff and customers, subject to robust criteria. The manuscript reflects this.
- **AEMO share.** The package said "grid-supplied electricity"; AEMO's 2026 ESOO reporting describes the 3% to 13% figures as a share of NEM **operational consumption**. The manuscript uses "operational consumption".
