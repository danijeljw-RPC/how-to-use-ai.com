## Purpose and Verification Record

This file maps Chapter 6's published endnotes and editorial choices to their sources. The chapter follows the approved plan, with both supplied research packages treated as complementary inputs. Examples were written for this manuscript; fictional requests, ingredient allocations and budget figures are not reported product tests or author experiences.

Codex reviewed the packages and checked the sources below on 29 September 2026, with research and drafting assistance acknowledged in the manuscript under ADR-04-0003. A live page check confirms the cited material was available to inspect; it does not constitute an independent test of a product. Recheck current guidance, product availability and research corrections before publication. Where access was limited to an abstract or publisher preview, that limit is recorded.

### Writing Productivity and Voice — ch6-writing and ch6-writing-voice

Noy, Shakked, and Whitney Zhang. “Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence.” *Science* 381 (2023). <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586>.

Agarwal, Dhruv, Mor Naaman and Aditya Vashistha. “AI Suggestions Homogenize Writing Toward Western Styles and Diminish Cultural Nuances.” *CHI 2025*. <https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713564>.

- Type: peer-reviewed controlled research; publication records checked.
- Supports: 453-person professional-writing experiment with 40% lower average completion time and 18% higher rated quality; counterevidence that suggestions can shift style and cultural expression.
- Limits: professional and experimental writing tasks are not household messages. The cultural-style study does not show every AI-assisted text loses its user's voice. The manuscript presents drafting benefit and voice risk together.

### Tutoring Benefits and Their Limits — ch6-tutoring

Kestin, Greg, Kelly Miller, Anna Klales, Timothy Milbourne and Gregorio Ponti. “AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting.” *Scientific Reports* 15 (2025), article 17458. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6>.

- Type: peer-reviewed randomised controlled trial; publisher abstract, methods and results checked.
- Supports: a deliberately designed AI tutor improved measured learning relative to the comparison in a university physics setting.
- Origin: package-02, `06-parenting-learning-and-hobbies.md`.
- Limits: specific course, two lessons and short-term assessments; not a finding about all tutors, long-term retention or children using general chatbots at home.

Slijepcevic, Nedim, and Ali Yaylali. “Leveraging ‘Khanmigo’ Generative AI-Powered Tool for Personalized Tutoring to Learn Scientific Concepts.” *Journal of Teaching and Learning* 19(4) (2025). <https://doi.org/10.22329/jtl.v19i4.10052>. Journal page: <https://jtl.uwindsor.ca/index.php/jtl/article/view/10052>.

- Type: peer-reviewed mixed-methods study; journal abstract checked.
- Supports: learning gains without a statistically significant between-group advantage in the studied comparison.
- Origin: package-01, learning research and contrasting viewpoints.
- Limits: 69 undergraduates; a paper-only group emerged during the study, rather than being a clean third randomised arm. Short exposure and materials could affect results. Absence of statistical significance does not prove equivalence. The manuscript keeps the claim narrow and does not combine the two studies into an overall effect estimate.

Oreopoulos, Philip, and Nina Low. “One Click Away: AI Tutoring with Khanmigo in a Two-Year School Experiment.” NBER Working Paper 35620, 2026. <https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620>.

- Type: working paper reporting a two-year cluster-randomised trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools; current NBER page checked.
- Supports: 0.06–0.08 standard-deviation annual gains, 96% trying the tool, limited median engagement and the authors' conclusion that access alone did not ensure substantive use.
- Limits: working paper, US middle-school remedial maths setting and a specific product configuration. It is presented as a counterweight to universal tutoring claims, not a verdict on all AI tutoring.

Chapekis, Athena, and Anna Lieb. “Google users are less likely to click on links when an AI summary appears in the results.” Pew Research Center, 22 July 2025. <https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/>.

## Editorial Synthesis and Material Deliberately Excluded

- Meal planning, writing, hobbies and organisation use original hypothetical situations informed by both packages, rather than presenting the research prompts as transcript evidence. The pantry allocations and $60 + $40 budget scenario were checked arithmetically; cooking time, portion suitability and prices remain checks for the reader, not validated promises.
- The education section deliberately uses university, VET and Free TAFE figures because omitting them hid the conflict the expanded research brief was designed to examine. It presents outcome and completion measures separately, demonstrates the invalid cumulative-ratio calculation, and does not convert aggregate evidence into a universal pathway ranking.
- The retracted article “The effect of ChatGPT on students' learning performance, learning perception, and higher-order thinking: insights from a meta-analysis” is excluded. The publisher page confirms its retracted status: <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04787-y>. This link is an exclusion record, not supporting evidence.
- The MIT essay-writing preprint, Barcaui retention study, critical-thinking self-report survey and AI-search trust preprint remain excluded. The NBER Khanmigo working paper and selected consumer/adoption statistics are now used with explicit population, design and limitation labels because they materially improve the reader's ability to judge competing claims.
- No broad claims about reduced intelligence, inevitable dependence, loss of personal voice or universal time savings are made. The chapter applies Chapter 4's citation-verification lesson without treating the presence of references as proof of correctness.
