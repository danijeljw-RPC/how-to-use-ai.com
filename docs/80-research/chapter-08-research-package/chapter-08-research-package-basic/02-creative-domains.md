# Creative Domains — Detailed Research Notes

# 1. Writing

## Current practical uses

### Ideation
- alternate plot directions
- character motivations
- conflict generation
- prompts for overcoming a blank page
- title/subtitle brainstorming
- metaphor and analogy generation

### Structural assistance
- chapter or scene outlines
- continuity checks against supplied notes
- pacing suggestions
- identifying unresolved threads

### Editing assistance
- sentence alternatives
- reduction of repetition
- tone variants
- clarity editing
- copyediting support

### Production assistance
- metadata
- synopses
- promotional copy
- translations/localisation drafts
- accessibility descriptions

## Capabilities

Language models are strong at rapidly producing plausible alternatives and transforming supplied text.

They can be especially useful when the user's problem is **option scarcity**: “I have no idea how to start this scene” or “give me five possible structures.”

## Limitations

- cliché and conventionality
- loss of a distinctive voice if outputs are adopted uncritically
- factual hallucination when creative writing intersects with real history or research
- continuity errors in long works
- over-smoothing prose into generic professional language
- imitation of familiar genre tropes
- lack of actual lived experience despite being able to write as though it has one
- potential training-data and authorship concerns

## Evidence

### Doshi & Hauser (2024)
AI-assisted short stories received higher average creativity evaluations, particularly for participants with lower baseline creative scores, but the stories became more similar to one another.

### Lee & Chung (2024)
Across five experiments, ChatGPT improved average performance on several constrained creative ideation tasks compared with no technology or conventional web search.

## Misconception to avoid

**“If AI can write a readable story, writers are unnecessary.”**

Readable prose production is one component of writing. Long-form authorship also includes intention, research, selection, continuity, revision, structural decisions, voice, perspective and responsibility for the work.

## Demonstration idea

Ask the same model ten times:

> “Give me a surprising premise for a story about a person who discovers a locked room.”

Compare thematic overlap.

Then change the process:

1. Human generates three premises first.
2. AI is instructed to avoid every element in those premises.
3. AI is asked for ideas from three unrelated domains.
4. Human combines and rejects elements.

The lesson is not “AI is uncreative.” It is that process design affects whether AI becomes an anchor or an exploration tool.

# 2. Art and image generation

## Current practical uses

- concept art
- thumbnail exploration
- moodboards
- visual references
- composition variants
- rough advertising concepts
- generative fill
- outpainting
- object/background replacement
- texture concepts
- product visualisation
- storyboards
- temporary placeholder assets

## Capability shift

By 2025–2026, leading image generators improved substantially in:

- text rendering
- instruction following
- image editing
- consistency with reference images
- photorealism
- iterative editing

For the book, exact model rankings would date quickly. Describe capability categories rather than declaring a “best” tool.

## Limitations

- exact control remains imperfect
- visual continuity can drift
- detailed object relationships can still fail
- representations can reproduce stereotypes
- outputs may resemble existing styles
- commercial/legal status varies by tool and jurisdiction
- generated logos may not be sufficiently distinctive or registrable as trademarks
- visual originality is not guaranteed

## Bias evidence

AlDahoul, Rahwan & Zaki (2025), *Scientific Reports*, documented racial and gender stereotypes and racial homogenisation in Stable Diffusion outputs across professions and attributes.

### Editorial use

A beginner may believe an image generator is returning a neutral visual average. It is more accurate to explain that visual outputs inherit patterns from data and model design.

## Style imitation

The US Copyright Office recorded creator concern about systems capable of producing material “in the style of” particular artists at enormous scale.

Important distinction:
- copying a protected work
- producing something similar to a creator's style
- falsely attributing a work to a creator

are not identical legal questions.

## Practical commercial-use example

A small business owner uses AI to explore six logo directions.

Useful:
- discovering preference for geometric vs illustrative
- testing colour families
- giving a professional designer a clearer brief

Risky:
- assuming the raw generated mark is legally distinctive
- using an image that resembles an existing logo
- assuming “I generated it” guarantees exclusive rights

This is a better Chapter 8 example than suggesting a company should simply replace a logo designer.

# 3. Music and audio

## Current practical uses

- complete song drafts
- lyric-to-song generation
- instrumental backing
- chord/melody exploration
- production demos
- sound effects
- synthetic vocals
- voice conversion
- background music
- jingles
- adaptive game/media music

## Why music is uniquely complicated

A single track may involve separate rights/interests in:

- composition
- lyrics
- sound recording
- performance
- voice/likeness
- samples
- publishing
- distribution

AI can touch several simultaneously.

## Current market evidence

Deezer reported that in June 2026 fully AI-generated music exceeded 50% of new daily uploads at peak, with about 90,000 AI-generated tracks per day on average for the month.

Yet Deezer said those tracks represented only 1–3% of listening.

### What this supports

- generation has become extremely cheap and scalable
- platforms face content-volume and fraud problems
- high upload volume does not prove listener preference

### What it does NOT support

- half of music listened to is AI
- half of musicians have been replaced
- AI music is economically dominant

## Streaming fraud

Deezer stated that up to 85% of streams associated with fully AI-generated tracks were detected as fraudulent in 2025.

This is a platform claim based on Deezer's own detection and fraud systems; attribute it accordingly.

## Licensing transition

Universal Music Group and Udio announced in October 2025 that they had settled copyright litigation and entered strategic agreements for a licensed AI music platform using authorised/licensed music.

This is significant because it demonstrates a market path between:
- unrestricted scraping
- complete prohibition

namely **licensing**.

## Tool-rights example: Suno

Suno's September 2026 help material states:

- Basic/free: Suno owns the generated songs and permits personal non-commercial use
- Pro/Premier: user owns songs generated while subscribed and receives commercial-use rights
- copyright protection is a separate question determined by law

This is an excellent beginner example of why “commercial-use rights” and “copyright” are different.

## Voice cloning / replicas

Voice generation raises identity questions beyond music copyright.

The US Copyright Office's Part 1 AI report recommended a federal law addressing unauthorised digital replicas that realistically but falsely depict an individual.

A singer's **voice identity** is therefore a different issue from whether a new melody infringes a composition.

# 4. Video

## Current practical uses

- storyboard frames
- previsualisation
- generated B-roll
- image-to-video
- short clips
- background replacement
- object editing
- synthetic actors/avatars
- dubbing
- voice generation
- subtitle/caption assistance
- rough shot exploration

## Capabilities

Video generation has moved from short surreal demonstrations toward:
- more coherent motion
- stronger prompt adherence
- shot controls
- reference-based generation
- integration with editing workflows

Nevertheless, the chapter should avoid implying “type a prompt, receive a finished film.”

## Persistent limitations

- multi-shot consistency
- character continuity
- spatial continuity
- exact timing and choreography
- lip sync
- identity stability
- product/brand accuracy
- long-duration narrative coherence
- reproducibility
- cost/compute
- rights around faces and voices

## Disclosure

YouTube's synthetic-content policy requires disclosure when realistically altered/generated content makes a real person appear to do something they did not do, alters a real event/place, or generates a realistic-looking scene that did not occur.

This is useful because it distinguishes ordinary creative effects from potentially misleading realism.

## Demonstration idea

Show three stages for a 30-second advertisement:

1. AI storyboard/concept frames
2. AI-generated rough moving previsualisation
3. final professionally shot or heavily edited piece

The lesson: AI can compress **pre-production exploration** without necessarily replacing final production.

# 5. Design

## Current practical uses

- layout variants
- colour/palette suggestions
- design-system exploration
- mockups
- presentation concepts
- advertising variants
- social assets
- background and object generation
- resize/reformat automation

## What AI does well

Design systems benefit from rapid variant generation because many early decisions are comparative:

- Which hierarchy reads best?
- Which composition is clearer?
- Which palette fits?
- Which crop works?

AI can cheaply expand the option set.

## What professional design still contains

- research
- user needs
- accessibility
- brand strategy
- information hierarchy
- production constraints
- print specifications
- responsive behaviour
- legal requirements
- cultural context
- stakeholder negotiation
- testing
- systems thinking

A design chapter example should therefore avoid equating “making a graphic” with “doing design.”

## Canva as a terms example

Canva's current AI terms (effective June 2026) state that, as between user and Canva and to the maximum extent permitted by law, users generally own AI outputs except where licensed Canva content is involved.

They also state:
- outputs may not be unique
- users are responsible for lawful input/output
- provenance metadata must not be removed
- third-party technology partners may be involved

This is useful because a mainstream beginner-facing tool now explicitly incorporates provenance and AI-specific contractual rules.

# 6. Cross-domain workflow patterns

## Pattern A — AI as blank-page breaker

Human has goal -> AI creates starting options -> human chooses/rejects -> human develops.

Best when:
- inertia is the problem
- output is exploratory
- stakes are low

Risk:
- anchoring on the first suggestion

## Pattern B — AI as variation engine

Human creates source material -> AI produces variants -> human compares -> human refines.

Best when:
- selection is valuable
- iteration cost is high

Risk:
- accepting superficial difference as genuine conceptual variety

## Pattern C — AI as production accelerator

Human decides concept -> AI handles repetitive transformations -> human verifies.

Examples:
- resizing
- rough background options
- subtitle drafts
- alternate crops
- cleanup

Risk:
- invisible errors entering production

## Pattern D — AI as co-generator

AI contributes substantial expressive material.

Benefit:
- speed
- surprising combinations
- accessibility

Risk:
- authorship ambiguity
- homogenisation
- loss of voice
- rights questions

## Pattern E — automated content factory

Prompts/data -> high-volume generation -> automated publishing.

This is qualitatively different from an individual artist using AI interactively.

Risks include:
- spam
- market flooding
- low accountability
- discoverability problems
- fraud
- platform countermeasures

Deezer's AI-music experience is an excellent real-world example.
