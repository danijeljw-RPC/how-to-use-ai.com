# 06 — Selected Real-World Examples and Case Studies

## Selection principle

These examples were selected because they help a beginner understand a **capability, limitation, workflow, legal distinction, economic effect or unexpected result**. They are not selected merely because the AI output was visually or technically impressive.

The package intentionally prefers approximately 10–20 strong examples over a long anecdote dump.

---

# Example 1 — AI-assisted short stories: better individual ratings, more group similarity

- **Domain:** Writing / creativity research
- **What happened:** Participants wrote short stories under different conditions involving access to generative-AI story ideas.
- **Who documented it:** Anil R. Doshi and Oliver P. Hauser.
- **Date:** 2024.
- **Source:** [S17] — https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290
- **What was measured/observed:** Story ratings and similarity/diversity patterns across outputs. The study involved 293 writers.
- **Result:** Access to AI ideas improved average ratings, with larger gains among initially less-creative participants, while AI-assisted stories were more similar to one another.
- **What it demonstrates:** Trade-off; assistance; homogenisation risk.
- **Why it matters to Chapter 8:** It gives empirical content to "Does AI kill creativity?" without answering yes/no.
- **Best chapter location:** `Does AI Kill Creativity?`, or a boxed study inside `Effort vs Judgement`.
- **Why useful to intended reader:** Easy to understand: individual benefit and group-level cost can coexist.
- **Print suitability:** Excellent.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent; could become a simple diagram or reader experiment.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** Yes. Ask people to draft an idea before and after AI suggestions and compare similarity.
- **Caveats:** Short experimental task; not professional novelists; immediate outcome rather than long-term skill development.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Low.

# Example 2 — Generative images can cause design fixation

- **Domain:** Visual design / creativity research
- **What happened:** A CHI study tested how generative-AI image support affected visual ideation.
- **Who documented it:** Wadinambiarachchi et al.
- **Date:** 2024.
- **Source:** [S18] — https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642919
- **What was measured/observed:** Idea quantity, variety, originality and fixation in a 60-participant design task.
- **Result:** AI support increased fixation and was associated with fewer ideas, lower variety and lower originality in the studied setting.
- **What it demonstrates:** Limitation; anchoring/fixation; misconception that "more generated pictures = more human creativity."
- **Why it matters:** Strong evidence that AI can shape human judgement rather than merely reduce effort.
- **Best chapter location:** `Does AI Kill Creativity?` or Design domain.
- **Print suitability:** Excellent.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent; interactive "human-first vs AI-first" ideation demo.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** Yes, but only as an informal exercise, not a scientific replication.
- **Caveats:** Small/task-specific; prompting/interface matter.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Low.

# Example 3 — Experienced designers can be slower with AI in the wrong workflow stage

- **Domain:** Design / professional workflow
- **What happened:** Research compared generative-AI assistance across design stages and expertise levels.
- **Who documented it:** Hou et al.
- **Date:** Published online 2025.
- **Source:** [S21] — https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2024.0937
- **What was measured/observed:** Design quality/time across ideation and implementation tasks with different expertise levels.
- **Result:** Effects varied by stage and expertise; lower-expertise participants gained more in implementation, while high-expertise participants in one implementation condition took about 57% longer without a quality gain.
- **What it demonstrates:** Trade-off; workflow dependence; expertise effects.
- **Why it matters:** Counters the slogan "AI always saves time."
- **Best chapter location:** `Effort vs Judgement` or Design.
- **Print suitability:** Strong.
- **Website suitability:** Strong, especially as an "AI may not be the right tool" example.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** Informally: time one precise edit with an AI tool and a deterministic tool.
- **Caveats:** Specific design tasks/tools; exact percentage should not be generalised.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Medium because tools improve, but the workflow lesson is durable.

# Example 4 — Freelance writing/coding and image tasks show measurable demand decline

- **Domain:** Labour economics
- **What happened:** Researchers analysed online freelancing before/after major generative-AI releases.
- **Who documented it:** Demirci, Hannane and Zhu.
- **Date:** 2025.
- **Source:** [S23] — https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/88113a91-b994-4717-8526-dadac657f1cb
- **What was measured/observed:** Job postings and characteristics in exposed versus less-exposed freelance categories.
- **Result:** Around 21% lower postings in automation-prone writing/coding categories relative to comparison categories and around 17% lower image-creation posts associated with image-generation exposure; remaining exposed work became more complex/higher-paid.
- **What it demonstrates:** Economic substitution; job redesign; market segmentation.
- **Why it matters:** Replaces speculative "will artists be replaced?" with observed task-market evidence.
- **Best chapter location:** `Is AI Replacing Artists?`
- **Print suitability:** Excellent if clearly scoped.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent for a source/evidence box.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** No sensible small-reader replication.
- **Caveats:** Online platform market; not all writing/art employment; correlation/design limitations.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Medium; early evidence remains historically useful but newer labour research will arrive.

# Example 5 — Three million freelancer postings: no simple collapse, but big shifts underneath

- **Domain:** Labour economics
- **What happened:** Researchers examined more than three million freelance job postings around the emergence of ChatGPT.
- **Who documented it:** Teutloff et al.
- **Date:** 2025.
- **Source:** [S24] — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268124004591
- **What was measured/observed:** Aggregate and skill-cluster demand.
- **Result:** Aggregate demand was comparatively stable, while substitutable writing/translation clusters fell substantially and complementary AI/ML/chatbot work grew.
- **What it demonstrates:** Substitution and complementarity can occur simultaneously.
- **Why it matters:** Prevents both "nothing happened" and "all freelance work collapsed" readings.
- **Best chapter location:** `Is AI Replacing Artists?`
- **Print suitability:** Excellent.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent; a before/after skill-map graphic.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** No.
- **Caveats:** Platform-specific, early period, classification methods.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Medium.

# Example 6 — Deezer: AI music supply explodes without matching listening demand

- **Domain:** Music / platform economics
- **What happened:** Deezer deployed detection/tagging for fully AI-generated music and published platform statistics.
- **Who documented it:** Deezer.
- **Date:** July 2026.
- **Source:** [S34] — https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/
- **What was measured/observed:** Daily uploads, stream share and fraud share on Deezer.
- **Result:** Fully AI-generated tracks exceeded 50% of daily uploads at a peak (around 90,000/day), but drew roughly 1–3% of streams. Deezer reported very high fraud among streams on AI-generated tracks and demonetised identified fraudulent streams.
- **What it demonstrates:** Creative abundance; discoverability; fraud; supply is not demand.
- **Why it matters:** One of the strongest Chapter 8 examples because it shifts discussion from "can AI make music?" to "what happens when production is nearly free?"
- **Best chapter location:** Music, `Is AI Replacing Artists?`, or an additional "Abundance" callout.
- **Print suitability:** Excellent with date and company-source caveat.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent; maintain current figures online.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** No.
- **Caveats:** Deezer's own detector/metrics; one platform; do not extrapolate global listening.
- **Likely to date quickly:** High. **Recheck before publication.**

- **Best chapter location:** Music / authenticity / provenance.
- **Print suitability:** Good.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent and updateable.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** No.
- **Caveats:** Do **not** call 75 million tracks "AI-generated"; Spotify called them spammy.
- **Likely to date quickly:** High.

# Example 17 — Australia explicitly rejects a TDM-exception proposal for now

- **Domain:** Copyright / Australian policy
- **What happened:** The Australian government announced in October 2025 that it was not considering a text-and-data-mining exception and asked CAIRG to focus on licensing, certainty around AI-generated material and lower-cost enforcement.
- **Who documented it:** Attorney-General / Attorney-General's Department.
- **Date:** 26–28 October 2025; current CAIRG page accessed 30 September 2026.
- **Sources:** [S01] [S02]
- **What it demonstrates:** Copyright answers are jurisdiction-specific; Australia cannot be described using an assumed US/EU rule.
- **Why it matters:** The author and likely initial audience are Australian.
- **Best chapter location:** `Is AI Stealing?` Plain English callout.
- **Print suitability:** Excellent if kept brief.
- **Website suitability:** Essential for current tracker.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** No.
- **Caveats:** Policy process continues; absence of a new TDM exception is not the same as a court ruling that every training use infringes.
- **Likely to date quickly:** High. **Recheck before publication.**

# Example 19 — Researchers extract memorised text and images from trained models

- **Domain:** Training-data explanation / limitation
- **What happened:** Security researchers demonstrated extraction of memorised text from language models and examples from diffusion models; related work shows deduplication reduces memorisation.
- **Who documented it:** Carlini et al.; Kandpal et al.; Somepalli et al.
- **Dates:** 2021–2023.
- **Sources:** [S26] [S27] [S28] [S29]
- **What it demonstrates:** Two opposing myths are both wrong: the model is not simply a searchable scrapbook, but "it never reproduces training data" is false.
- **Why it matters:** Gives the chapter a technically accurate beginner explanation of training.
- **Best chapter location:** Plain English training-data callout / `Is AI Stealing?`
- **Print suitability:** Excellent.
- **Website suitability:** Strong, preferably diagram rather than copied examples.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** Do not encourage extraction of potentially private/copyrighted memorised data; use a diagram or synthetic demo instead.
- **Caveats:** Older/model-specific experiments; do not convert extraction demonstrations into universal copy rates.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Low/medium; mechanism remains important, rates change.

# Example 20 — Generated professional imagery silently fills demographic details

- **Domain:** Art/design / bias
- **What happened:** Researchers audited generated images of professions, including Australian-relevant paramedic imagery and broader healthcare/profession sets.
- **Who documented it:** Peer-reviewed research indexed in PubMed and Scientific Reports.
- **Dates:** 2024–2025.
- **Sources:** [S45] [S46] [S47]
- **What was measured/observed:** Gender, skin tone/race and other demographic representation in generated professional images.
- **Result:** The studies report representation imbalances/stereotyped patterns in the tested models/prompts.
- **What it demonstrates:** AI can make creative choices the user did not explicitly specify.
- **Why it matters:** Turns "bias" into a direct creative-work issue: the model casts the scene for you.
- **Best chapter location:** Art/Design, a short Watch Out or companion website.
- **Print suitability:** Good if one example only.
- **Website suitability:** Excellent for a careful reproducible audit using neutral prompts.
- **Could reader recreate/adapt?:** Yes, as an informal bias audit; should document prompts/model/version and avoid claiming scientific generality.
- **Caveats:** Model versions change quickly; sample/prompts matter.
- **Likely to date quickly:** Medium/high.
