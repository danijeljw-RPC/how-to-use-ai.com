# Personal Assistants — Research Notes

## Core conclusion

The draft's "personal assistants evolved" direction is strongly supported, but should be framed as a **convergence** of four capabilities:

1. conversation;
2. memory/personal context;
3. tool access;
4. agents / multi-step action.

Each additional capability makes the assistant more useful **and** increases the amount of trust/data/authority involved.

---

## Historical path

Earlier voice assistants were primarily:
- command-and-control interfaces;
- search/query systems;
- timers/reminders;
- smart-home controls.

Modern assistants increasingly:
- maintain conversational context;
- interpret images/screens;
- access personal messages/files;
- remember preferences;
- call tools;
- perform multi-step tasks.

This is an evolutionary path rather than a wholly new invention.

---

## Memory and personalisation

Meta announced memory/personalisation features in 2025, allowing its assistant to remember details from one-to-one conversations and use broader platform context for recommendations in supported regions.

**Source**
- Meta, *Building Toward a Smarter, More Personalized Assistant*, 27 Jan 2025: https://about.fb.com/news/2025/01/building-toward-a-smarter-more-personalized-assistant/

### Privacy implication

The better an assistant understands:
- relationships;
- location;
- messages;
- calendar;
- files;
- preferences;
- routines

the more damaging unauthorised access or mistaken inference can become.

This is a structural trade-off, not merely a company-policy issue.

---

## Apple / on-device versus cloud

Apple's privacy explanation for Siri emphasises on-device processing where possible and data minimisation.

This is useful for explaining why on-device AI matters:
- data may not need to leave the device;
- latency can improve;
- offline functions become possible;
- cloud cost can fall.

But "on-device" is not synonymous with "private" or "safe." Device compromise, app permissions, synced data and cloud fallback still matter.

**Source**
- Apple, *Our longstanding privacy commitment with Siri*, 9 Jan 2025: https://www.apple.com/au/newsroom/2025/01/our-longstanding-privacy-commitment-with-siri/

## Siri as a release-history caution

Apple's assistant roadmap is useful because the company publicly announced major personal-context AI capabilities and subsequently changed/delayed aspects of rollout across regions and versions.

By June 2026 Apple announced a more capable Siri AI with personal context and on-screen awareness, while also stating its EU iPhone/iPad rollout was delayed because of DMA-related issues.

**Sources**
- Apple, *Apple introduces Siri AI*, 9 Jun 2026: https://www.apple.com/au/newsroom/2026/06/apple-introduces-siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant/
- Apple, EU delay announcement, 9 Jun 2026: https://www.apple.com/au/newsroom/2026/06/due-to-dma-siri-ai-delayed-in-eu-for-ios-27-and-ipados-27/

**Durable lesson:** product roadmaps are shaped by engineering, regulation and platform strategy; announcements are not deployments.

## AI hardware hype: Rabbit R1

The Rabbit R1 launched in 2024 with an ambitious vision of replacing app-by-app interaction with natural-language task execution.

Early independent reviews found:
- slow responses;
- limited functionality;
- unreliable app integrations;
- inaccurate answers;
- privacy concerns.

Later reviews reported substantial improvements.

This makes it a better example than a simple "failed gadget":
- launch hype can outrun product readiness;
- software can improve after launch;
- dedicated hardware must compete with phones that can gain similar AI capabilities through software.

**Sources**
- WIRED, Rabbit R1 review (May 2024): https://www.wired.com/review/rabbit-r1
- Tom's Guide review (May 2024): https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/rabbit-r1-review
- Tom's Guide one-year revisit (Feb 2025): https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/one-year-later-the-rabbit-r1-is-actually-good-now-heres-why

Use as an independent review example, not as proof that AI hardware broadly fails.

## Assistants versus companions

Australia's eSafety Commissioner usefully distinguishes:
- **assistants:** framed around tasks, information and learning;
- **companions:** framed around connection, friendship and emotional support.

The boundaries increasingly blur because both can use memory and human-like conversation.

**Source**
- eSafety Commissioner, *Talking to machines*, 2026: https://www.esafety.gov.au/research/talking-to-machines-childrens-experiences-with-ai-assistants-and-companions

Chapter 14 should mention this only briefly; emotional reliance belongs primarily in Chapter 9.

## Accessibility

Potential benefits include:
- natural-language control for people who cannot use conventional interfaces easily;
- real-time description of visual content;
- transcription/captioning;
- simplified interaction;
- reminders and cognitive support;
- translation.

The chapter should avoid claiming that AI automatically improves accessibility. Poor recognition, hallucination, inaccessible UI design and cloud dependence can create new barriers.

---

## Gradual versus sudden change

The draft's observation is good.

### Gradual
Voice assistants accumulated capabilities over years through phones, speakers, cars and appliances.

### Sudden/visible
ChatGPT's public launch made conversational AI feel abrupt even though language-model research had developed for years.

The future personal assistant may follow both patterns:
- underlying integrations and models improve gradually;
- a product/interface may suddenly make the accumulated capability visible.

This is a useful explanation for why technology forecasts are often wrong in **visibility**, not just capability.

## Maturity assessment

| Capability | Maturity |
|---|---|
| Voice commands / reminders | Mature |
| Conversational assistant | Widespread |
| Personal memory | Deployment |
| Cross-app personal context | Expanding deployment |
| Tool/action execution | Limited/expanding |
| Always-on proactive life management | Early deployment |
| Emotionally companion-like behaviour | Widespread in some products, governance concern |

---

## Signals to watch

- whether assistants can act across apps without unsafe credential sharing;
- on-device capability growth;
- granular permissions;
- audit history ("what did my assistant do?");
- independent prompt-injection testing;
- user retention beyond novelty;
- accessibility evaluations;
- privacy/regulatory enforcement;
- clear separation of recommendation from action.
