# 13 — Publication Recheck List

This file exists because Chapter 11 is unusually vulnerable to dating. Re-run these checks near manuscript lock and again before major new editions.

## A. Mandatory rechecks

### 1. International AI Safety Report

- Is 2026 still the latest full report?
- Has a 2027/key update been published?
- Have capability/risk conclusions materially changed?
- URL: https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publications

### 2. Waymo / autonomous ride-hailing

- Current number of cities with public fully autonomous service.
- Whether services remain geofenced/limited and under what operational conditions.
- Any major regulatory/safety event that changes the teaching example.
- URL: https://waymo.com/blog/

### 3. Project NANDA “95%” report

- Find canonical MIT/Project NANDA host if available.
- Check whether report remains labelled preliminary.
- Check whether authors published a peer-reviewed/follow-up version.
- Check exact denominator/definition used for 5%/95% statements.
- Do not rely on a media paraphrase for wording.

### 4. Builder.ai

- Final insolvency outcome.
- Any official criminal/regulatory/court findings.
- Whether revenue allegations were formally established or disputed.
- If no final official finding, keep named details on website and genericise in print.

### 5. METR developer productivity

- Has larger follow-up work replicated/reversed the 2025 finding?
- Which model/tool generations were tested?
- Keep “experienced developers on familiar open-source tasks using early-2025 tools” attached to the result.

### 6. Australian AI institutions/policy

- Is Australia's AI Safety Institute still named/structured the same way?
- Has the National AI Plan been revised?
- Has ACL/AI-specific legislation changed Treasury's 2025 conclusion?
- Have ASIC/ACCC issued new AI marketing/enforcement guidance?

### 7. Education policy

- Latest Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools.
- Latest TEQSA assessment-integrity/generative-AI guidance.
- Major new evidence on AI tutoring/learning.
- Latest detector studies before making current claims about AI detectors.

### 8. Stanford AI Index / adoption figures

Any printed percentage about:
- organisational AI adoption;
- genAI use;
- private/corporate investment;
- consumer adoption;
- labour-market effects

should be updated to the latest Index/data and dated explicitly.

## B. Quotations and attribution rechecks

### Steve Jobs — “bicycle of the mind”

- Verify exact wording against archival audio/transcript.
- Keep quotation short.
- Identify that Jobs was speaking about computers, not AI.
- Source: https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-15-qj77s7j327

### “Calculator for text/words”

- If an attribution is printed, locate Simon Willison's original post/interview wording rather than relying on secondary attribution.
- If original cannot be confidently pinned down, use the analogy without a named attribution or state “often described as.”

### Amara's Law

- Do not quote as a scientifically established law.
- If used, say “a heuristic often attributed to Roy Amara.”
- Prefer not to print exact wording unless original provenance is established.

### AI executive forecasts

For any “cure disease,” AGI timeline or similar quote:
- locate original video/transcript;
- preserve modal verbs (“could,” “might,” “I think”);
- keep date and horizon;
- distinguish forecast from result.

## C. Legal/regulatory wording checks

### SEC cases

- preserve “settled,” “without admitting or denying” where applicable;
- do not upgrade “false/misleading statements” in an administrative order into a broader accusation about the company/person.

### FTC cases

- check final order/procedural status;
- distinguish allegations from final settlement requirements.

### Australia

- never imply AI is governed by one standalone “AI law” unless that has become true at publication;
- check ACL and sector-specific rules current at that time;
- this chapter should explain regulation, not give legal advice.

## D. Demo/product status checks

### Gemini 2023 demo

Historical facts are stable, but confirm original Google developer post remains accessible. Archive/source locally if the production workflow permits.

### Humane/Rabbit

If named in print, frame as **launch-period product evidence**, not current performance, unless retested/currently sourced.

### Amazon Just Walk Out

Architecture and human-review processes may change. If used, date both the Reuters account and Amazon's response/current description.

## E. Benchmark/evaluation checks

- Do not use current “#1 model” rankings in print unless absolutely necessary.
- If a benchmark statistic is used, record exact model version, test version, date, scoring method and source.
- Check contamination/invalid-question concerns.
- For “beats humans,” identify human comparator population.

## F. Claims that are stable enough for print

These are comparatively durable if phrased carefully:

- demos show selected capability, not automatically reliability;
- research results are conditional on method/sample/metric;
- forecasts are not observations;
- conflicts/incentives justify verification, not automatic disbelief;
- independent checking/lateral reading is useful;
- benchmark scores do not automatically establish job competence;
- a technical breakthrough does not automatically solve the surrounding social/clinical/economic problem;
- hype-scepticism can itself become overconfident;
- AI can be genuinely useful while marketing around it is exaggerated.

## G. Suggested production workflow

Before final manuscript lock:

1. open this file;
2. update all mandatory items;
3. search the chapter for every percentage, date, “current,” “today,” “now,” “latest,” “#1,” “most,” “all,” “never,” “will,” and named product/company;
4. verify every quotation from primary source;
5. ensure every named legal/regulatory case reflects current procedural status;
6. ensure Chapter 14 uses the same prediction discipline Chapter 11 teaches;
7. update the companion website case library after print lock rather than constantly changing print examples.
