# 10 — Template Improvement Observations

This file critiques the current Chapter 11 template and DJ's notes. It does **not** rewrite the chapter.

## Observation 1 — “AI is transformative” needs an operational meaning

**Current topic:** Key Idea / Core Takeaway.  
**Weakness:** “transformative” is broad enough to become hype itself.  
**Research suggests:** ground it in observable adoption, task capability, scientific uses and changed workflows; do not imply a settled endpoint.  
**Stronger evidence:** Stanford AI Index 2026; AlphaFold; controlled workplace studies.  
**Possible replacement example:** AlphaFold + enterprise adoption contrast.  
**Issue type:** factual precision/balance.  
**Sources:**  
https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03819-2

## Observation 2 — Chapter 1's “pattern recognition at scale” cannot carry all of Chapter 11's argument

**Current topic:** Utopian Promises uses Chapter 1 definition as corrective.  
**Weakness:** pedagogically useful but reductive if treated as a complete description of modern systems, especially tool-using/agentic systems and non-LLM scientific AI.  
**Research suggests:** use observed limits/deployment chain rather than “it is just pattern recognition” as the main rebuttal.  
**Issue:** accuracy/credibility.

## Observation 3 — Doom section needs a stronger evidence anchor

**Current topic:** “legitimate long-term research is serious.”  
**Weakness:** true in spirit but currently asserted generically.  
**Research suggests:** anchor with International AI Safety Report 2026 plus expert-survey disagreement.  
**Evidence:** 100+ expert international assessment; Grace et al. survey.  
**Issue:** depth/balance.  
**Sources:**  
https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026  
https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843

## Observation 4 — Avoid “serious people” as an authority shortcut

**Current topic:** Doom claims.  
**Weakness:** “serious researchers” is rhetorically understandable but does not teach the reader why the claim is worth attention.  
**Research suggests:** describe the mechanism, evidence type, uncertainty and institutional assessment instead.  
**Issue:** usefulness/critical-thinking consistency.

## Observation 5 — Utopian section needs genuine success cases

**Current topic:** AI cannot solve societal problems alone.  
**Weakness:** without positive examples it can sound like a generic debunking chapter.  
**Research suggests:** contrast AlphaFold/GraphCast/GNoME technical breakthroughs with the downstream steps required for health/climate/material deployment.  
**Issue:** balance/depth.  
**Sources:** see `03-utopian-claims-and-genuine-benefits.md`.

## Observation 6 — Generic startup-hype section is currently too abstract

**Current topic:** Startup Hype, Fake Demos, Investor Marketing.  
**Weakness:** says incentives exist but gives the reader little evidence to recognise the pattern.  
**Research suggests:** add one genericised demo case, one regulator AI-washing case, and perhaps one shipped-product/review contrast.  
**Replacement examples:** Gemini demo; SEC AI washing; Humane AI Pin.  
**Issue:** usefulness/depth.

## Observation 7 — “Fake demo” is too blunt as a category label in prose

**Current topic:** plan-required fake demos.  
**Weakness:** demos can be edited, selected, scripted, simulated or human-assisted without the output itself being fabricated.  
**Research suggests:** define the subtypes and use “misleading/edited/staged” unless facts support “fake.”  
**Issue:** factual/legal/editorial precision.

## Observation 8 — The demo/product/research triad is incomplete

**Current topic:** practical evaluation tool.  
**Weakness:** AGI timelines, job predictions, disease forecasts and Chapter 14 future claims are **forecasts**, not any of the three categories.  
**Research suggests:** add prediction/forecast as at least a fourth type; advertisement/anecdote are optional additional types.  
**Issue:** usefulness/robustness.  
**Source basis:** evaluation-method research + chapter subject matter.

## Observation 9 — “Product works reliably day to day” needs scope

**Current topic:** definition of product.  
**Weakness:** some legitimate products are limited/geofenced/early-access. A product does not have to work universally.  
**Research suggests:** “reliably usable by ordinary users within its stated operating scope.”  
**Issue:** accuracy.

## Observation 10 — Self-driving example is outdated/too absolute

**Current topic:** “one pre-mapped route on sunny day vs every road/every city/rain.”  
**Weakness:** by September 2026, fully autonomous public ride-hailing is commercially operating across multiple US cities.  
**Research suggests:** teach **operating envelope**: real product within bounded geography/weather/road/regulatory conditions.  
**Evidence:** Waymo September 2026 operational announcements.  
**Issue:** factual accuracy/currentness.  
**Source:** https://waymo.com/blog/2026/09/ride-in-denver-san-diego-tampa/

## Observation 11 — Research claim definition needs benchmark/preprint detail

**Current topic:** “specific tested result.”  
**Weakness:** readers need to know a result can be a preprint, benchmark, lab experiment, field study or company technical report.  
**Research suggests:** explain peer review as one layer; ask what was measured and compared.  
**Issue:** depth/media literacy.

## Observation 12 — Current three-question method lacks original-source tracing

**Current topic:** Try This.  
**Weakness:** a reader can answer all three while still evaluating a distorted headline rather than the source.  
**Research suggests:** explicitly trace to original evidence and use lateral reading/independent checks.  
**Evidence:** Wineburg/McGrew; SIFT.  
**Sources:** https://doi.org/10.1177/016146811912101102  
https://umsystem.pressbooks.pub/information/chapter/the-sift-method-evaluating-web-sources/  
**Issue:** usefulness/robustness.

## Observation 13 — “Who benefits?” can encourage cynical motive reasoning

**Current topic:** evaluation method.  
**Weakness:** a true claim can benefit a company; critics/researchers also have incentives.  
**Research suggests:** reframe as “what incentives/conflicts mean I should verify this more carefully?”  
**Issue:** balance.

## Observation 14 — Add “what exactly was measured, compared with what?”

**Current topic:** evaluation method.  
**Weakness:** benchmark/headline distortion often occurs through comparator and denominator, not outright falsehood.  
**Research suggests:** make measurement/comparison a core question.  
**Examples:** GPT-4 bar percentile; 95% enterprise claim; PNAS student performance.  
**Issue:** practical usefulness.

## Observation 15 — Add a worked anti-hype example

**Current topic:** Watch Out / calibration.  
**Weakness:** warning against cynicism is currently asserted rather than demonstrated.  
**Research suggests:** use the “95% of AI projects fail” mutation or Builder.ai viral simplification.  
**Issue:** balance/credibility.

## Observation 16 — Executive note “task automation, not job automation” is too absolute

**Current topic:** DJ notes — executives.  
**Weakness:** jobs are task bundles; automation can change headcount/roles and sometimes eliminate roles.  
**Research suggests:** say capability is usually measured at task level and translating to whole-job replacement requires additional assumptions.  
**Reserve:** detailed jobs evidence to Chapter 10.  
**Issue:** factual accuracy.

## Observation 17 — Horses-to-cars analogy should be replaced
