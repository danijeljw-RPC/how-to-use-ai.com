# Australian Context

## Why Australia deserves dedicated treatment

The book is Australian-authored and likely to reach Australian readers first. U.S. evidence is valuable but should not automatically be treated as Australian evidence because of differences in:
- industrial composition;
- awards and enterprise agreements;
- labour law;
- union coverage;
- vocational education;
- public-sector employment;
- geographic concentration;
- business size.

---

## Jobs and Skills Australia — whole-of-labour-market study

JSA's *Australia's AI Transition: Jobs, Skills and the Future of Work* is the strongest Australian anchor.

Its public summary states:
- GenAI is more likely to augment human work than replace it;
- adoption remains early and uneven;
- impacts vary across occupations, industries, regions and groups;
- skills will be central to adaptation.

Source:
https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study

Overarching report:
https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/publications/generative-ai-capacity-study-report

Final release:
https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/publications/our-gen-ai-transition-final-release

### Editorial value

Use JSA to support an Australia-specific version of the chapter's augmentation framing rather than relying only on U.S. consultancy forecasts.

### Caution

JSA's conclusion is a study-level interpretation based on exposure, adoption and case-study evidence. It should not be turned into a guarantee that Australian jobs will not be lost.

## ABS — measured business adoption

ABS *Characteristics of Australian Business* for 2024–25 reports:
- 12% of businesses used AI;
- compared with 1% in 2022–23;
- adoption was higher among innovation-active businesses;
- highest reported industry usage included Information Media and Telecommunications (38%), Professional Scientific and Technical Services (24%), and Financial and Insurance Services (24%).

Source:
https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/technology-and-innovation/characteristics-australian-business/latest-release

### What this supports

AI adoption is rising quickly from a low base.

### What this does not support

The survey does not measure:
- intensity of use;
- whether AI displaced labour;
- which system was used;
- productivity effects.

This distinction is important.

## ABS — public-sector example

The ABS 2026 AI transparency statement lists uses including:
- machine learning for classification;
- a Census generative-AI chatbot;
- generative AI for writing/review, summarisation and meeting-note transcription;
- human review of outputs.

Source:
https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/ai-transparency-statement

Why useful:
A concrete Australian example of **role redesign and augmentation with governance**, rather than a dramatic layoff case.

## Australian business R&D

ABS reported business AI R&D spending of $668.3 million in 2023–24, up from $276.3 million in 2021–22 (142% increase).

Source:
https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/ai-now-fastest-growing-area-business-rd

Use:
Evidence that investment is accelerating.

Do not use:
as proof of labour displacement or productivity.

## Productivity context

The Productivity Commission's September 2026 update reports stagnant Australian labour productivity in the June 2026 quarter and a 0.2% decline over the year.

Source:
https://www.pc.gov.au/ongoing/productivity-insights/update-september-2026/

Why relevant:
It is too early to claim that generative AI has already produced a visible Australian productivity boom in aggregate statistics.

This is an important counterweight to task-level studies showing large gains.

## Fair Work — consultation over technological change

Fair Work Ombudsman guidance explains that consultation clauses generally require consultation where an employer decides to introduce major changes in production, programming, organisation, structure or **technology** likely to significantly affect employees. Enterprise agreements must contain consultation terms.

Source:
https://www.fairwork.gov.au/tools-and-resources/best-practice-guides/consultation-and-cooperation-in-the-workplace

### Editorial implication

For Australian workers, "my employer introduces AI" is not only a technology question. Existing industrial relations structures may create consultation obligations depending on the workplace instrument and effect.

Do not turn this into legal advice.

---

## Fair Work Ombudsman — AI is advisory in its own operations

FWO states that AI may provide administrative support or insights but compliance, enforcement and entitlement decisions are made by authorised staff, with AI outputs reviewed and verified.

Source:
https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/responsible-use-of-ai/ai-transparency-statement

Why useful:
Concrete illustration of a high-stakes organisation choosing augmentation and human accountability.

---

## Fair Work — warning about workplace-law answers

FWO also warns that AI-generated workplace-law information can be wrong, outdated or drawn from another jurisdiction.

Source:
https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/responsible-use-of-ai/using-ai-for-help-with-workplace-questions

Why relevant to Chapter 10:
It demonstrates a legitimate case where limiting AI reliance is sound professional behaviour. This qualifies the core claim that AI users outperform refusers.

## Regional and non-desk Australia

The chapter should not imply that "AI exposure" means sitting at a laptop.

For:
- trades;
- agriculture;
- transport;
- care;
- hospitality;
- construction;

generative AI may affect:
- quoting;
- scheduling;
- compliance paperwork;
- diagnostics;
- training;
- customer communication;
- logistics;

while core physical tasks may require robotics, machinery or embodied systems.

ABS adoption differences by industry are useful here: 2024–25 reported AI use was 3% in Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing, 6% in Construction and 5% in Accommodation and Food Services, compared with much higher rates in information and professional services.

This is not a "safety ranking." It is current adoption evidence.

## Australian chapter recommendation

Use at least one Australian evidence box:

### Possible box: "Australia is adopting AI — but adoption is not the same thing as job loss"

- 12% of Australian businesses reported AI use in 2024–25.
- JSA finds augmentation more likely than replacement overall.
- aggregate Australian productivity statistics do not yet show an AI boom.
- Fair Work structures may require consultation for major technology-driven workplace changes.

This would make the chapter feel genuinely Australian without making it parochial.
