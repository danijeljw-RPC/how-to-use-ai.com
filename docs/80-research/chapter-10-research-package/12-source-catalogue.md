# Working Source Catalogue

## Labour economics — experiments and field studies

### Generative AI at Work
- **Authors:** Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, Lindsey R. Raymond
- **Date:** 2023 working paper; QJE publication 2025
- **Type:** workplace field study
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
- **Use:** 5,179 support agents; heterogeneous productivity effects.
- **Limitation:** one employer / work setting.

### Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence
- **Authors:** Shakked Noy, Whitney Zhang
- **Date:** 2023
- **Type:** peer-reviewed randomized experiment, Science
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586
- **Use:** 453 professionals; task time -40%, quality +18%.
- **Limitation:** bounded writing tasks.

### Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier
- **Authors:** Fabrizio Dell'Acqua et al.
- **Date:** 2023 working paper; Organization Science 2026
- **Type:** field experiment
- **URL:** https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/
- **Use:** task-dependent gains and failures.
- **Limitation:** consultants and selected tasks.

### METR experienced open-source developer study
- **Organisation:** METR
- **Date:** 10 Jul 2025
- **Type:** randomized controlled trial
- **URL:** https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/
- **Use:** measured 19% slowdown.
- **Limitation:** 16 experienced developers, mature repositories.
- **Recheck:** yes; tools changed quickly.

## Administrative and national data

### Still Waters, Rapid Currents
- **Authors:** Anders Humlum, Emilie Vestergaard
- **Date:** 2025; revised Mar 2026
- **Type:** surveys linked to Danish administrative data
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- **Use:** work reorganises before wages/hours change materially.

### Canaries in the Coal Mine?
- **Authors:** Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, Ruyu Chen
- **Date:** revised 12 Aug 2026
- **Type:** U.S. payroll-data working paper
- **URL:** https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/
- **Use:** no widespread displacement + young-worker divergence.
- **Recheck:** yes.

### How Does AI Change Labor Demand? Evidence from 41 Countries
- **Authors:** Bharat Chandar, Bouke Klein Teeselink
- **Date:** 21 Sep 2026
- **Type:** working paper, job postings + employment records
- **URL:** https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/how-does-ai-change-labor-demand/
- **Use:** junior/senior composition after firm AI adoption.
- **Recheck:** yes; very recent working paper.

## Exposure measures

### GPTs are GPTs
- **Authors:** Tyna Eloundou, Sam Manning, Pamela Mishkin, Daniel Rock
- **Date:** 2023
- **Type:** occupational exposure study
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130
- **Use:** task exposure; explicit non-prediction of adoption timing.
- **Limitation:** capability-scoring exercise; OpenAI-affiliated authors.

### ILO Refined Global Index
- **Authors:** Pawel Gmyrek et al.
- **Date:** 20 May 2025
- **Type:** ILO working paper
- **URL:** https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure
- **Use:** global exposure, gender/income-country differences.
- **Limitation:** exposure is not observed impact.

### ILO — what exposure indicators tell us
- **Organisation:** International Labour Organization
- **Date:** 17 Apr 2026
- **URL:** https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/new-ilo-brief-explains-what-ai-exposure-indicators-reveal-about-jobs

### Frey & Osborne
- **Date:** 2013
- **URL:** https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/the-future-of-employment
- **Use:** famous 47% occupation-level computerisation estimate.

### Arntz, Gregory & Zierahn
- **Date:** 2016
- **Organisation:** OECD
- **URL:** https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-risk-of-automation-for-jobs-in-oecd-countries_5jlz9h56dvq7-en.html

# Source-selection note

The package deliberately prioritises primary research, official statistics, government/international institutions and company filings. Company and advocacy sources are used when they can establish their own actions, usage data or stated positions, not as independent proof of broad labour-market outcomes.
