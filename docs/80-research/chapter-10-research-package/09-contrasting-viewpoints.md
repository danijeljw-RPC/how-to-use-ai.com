# Contrasting Viewpoints and Genuine Disagreements

## Position A — AI will mainly transform jobs rather than eliminate them

### Strong evidence
- ILO task-based exposure work.
- Jobs and Skills Australia.
- Danish administrative evidence showing task reorganisation with little average earnings/hours effect.
- many jobs bundle multiple tasks requiring human input.

### Strongest argument
Technical capability rarely maps one-to-one onto occupations. Adoption, complementary tasks and demand effects matter.

### Weak point
"Transformation" can still mean fewer positions, weaker entry routes, lower bargaining power or higher work intensity. It should not be used as reassurance.

---

## Position B — AI can materially reduce labour demand even without whole-job automation

### Strong evidence
- online freelance demand reductions;
- Klarna's reported customer-service automation;
- historic robot evidence;
- younger-worker divergence in exposed U.S. occupations.

### Strongest argument
If one worker can produce substantially more and demand does not expand proportionally, fewer workers may be needed.

### Weak point
Current evidence is uneven and sector-specific. It does not establish economy-wide mass unemployment.

---

## Position C — productivity gains will create enough demand/new work to offset displacement

### Evidence in favour
- long historical record of new occupational specialties;
- productivity can lower prices and expand demand;
- innovative firms can grow after adoption.

### Evidence against simple version
- displaced workers can experience persistent losses;
- gains can go to other firms, places or generations;
- French robot evidence shows adopter employment growth alongside negative industry employment;
- timing can be long.

### Correct chapter treatment
Possible mechanism, not guaranteed outcome.

---

## Position D — current AI productivity claims are overstated

### Evidence
- Acemoglu's modest macro estimate;
- METR experienced-developer slowdown;
- weak aggregate productivity signal in countries such as Australia;
- early experiments often use bounded tasks with clear outputs.

### Counterevidence
- strong effects in customer support, professional writing and consulting tasks;
- real employer adoption and operational cost reductions.

### Synthesis
Micro productivity effects can be real while aggregate productivity effects remain small because:
- adoption is incomplete;
- implementation is costly;
- only some tasks improve;
- saved time is not always converted into output;
- organisational change lags technology.

---

## Position E — AI will disproportionately hurt entry-level workers

### Evidence in favour
- 2026 Stanford payroll analysis;
- emerging 41-country labour-demand research;
- many easily delegated junior tasks overlap with generative AI.

### Countervailing evidence
- customer-support study shows the largest productivity gains among less-experienced workers;
- AI can accelerate learning and give novices access to expert-like guidance.

### Key unresolved issue
Does higher novice productivity:
- make juniors more valuable and train them faster,
or
- allow firms to hire fewer juniors?

Both mechanisms are plausible and may occur in different firms.

---

## Position F — "people using AI will outperform people refusing to use it"

### Evidence in favour
- writing experiment;
- customer-support field study;
- consultant experiment;
- widespread employer adoption.

### Necessary exceptions
- tasks outside the AI frontier;
- high verification burden;
- confidentiality/regulatory restrictions;
- skill-learning stages where doing the work matters;
- experienced developer RCT;
- cases where AI introduces false confidence.

### Recommended treatment
A strong heuristic, not a law.

---

## Position G — technology direction is a choice

Some labour economists, including Daron Acemoglu, stress that innovation can be directed toward:
- replacing workers;
- or augmenting workers.

Organisational incentives, product design and policy influence which path is taken.

Source:
Acemoglu's task-based AI work and broader research programme, including  
https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487

This argument is useful because "AI's impact" is not produced by technical capability alone. Employers decide workflows.

# Where apparent contradictions are not contradictions

### "AI gives large productivity gains" AND "aggregate earnings have barely moved"
Different scale and time horizon.

### "Novices gain most from AI" AND "junior hiring may weaken"
Worker productivity and number of workers demanded are distinct outcomes.

### "An adopting firm hires more" AND "industry employment falls"
Market-share reallocation.

### "Most exposed jobs transform" AND "some workers are displaced"
Occupation-level persistence can coexist with individual job loss.

### "New jobs emerge" AND "displaced workers suffer"
New work may not go to the same people.
