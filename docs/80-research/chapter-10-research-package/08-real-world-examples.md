# Strong Real-World and Historical Examples

The goal is 15–20 examples that teach a mechanism, not dramatic anecdotes.

---

## 1. Customer-support AI assistance

**Period:** workplace rollout studied 2020–2021; published 2023/2025  
**What happened:** 5,179 agents gained access to an AI assistant.  
**Observed:** ~14% average productivity increase; ~34% for novice/lower-skill workers.  
**Demonstrates:** augmentation; uneven gains; potential learning.  
**Chapter fit:** Augmentation vs Replacement; adaptability.  
**Print:** excellent.  
**Website:** interactive chart possible.  
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161  
**Caveat:** one company/occupation.

---

## 2. Professional writing experiment

**Observed:** 453 professionals; time -40%, assessed quality +18%.  
**Demonstrates:** strong bounded-task productivity.  
**Fit:** opening evidence; myth "AI always/never helps."  
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586  
**Caveat:** short writing tasks, not whole jobs.

---

## 3. BCG consultants and the jagged frontier

**Observed:** 758 consultants; strong gains on tasks inside the AI frontier; poorer outcomes can occur beyond it.  
**Demonstrates:** task dependence; misuse risk.  
**Fit:** self-assessment; effort/judgement nuance.  
**Source:** https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/  
**Print:** excellent.

---

## 4. Experienced developers slowed by AI

**Observed:** 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 tasks; 19% slower with early-2025 AI tools despite believing they were faster.  
**Demonstrates:** perceived vs measured productivity; expertise/task context.  
**Fit:** Myth vs Reality.  
**Source:** https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/  
**Caveat:** very narrow sample; do not generalise.

---

## 5. Denmark: work changes before wages/hours

**Observed:** employer adoption, task reorganisation and new AI-related tasks; no significant average earnings/hours effects in first two years.  
**Demonstrates:** job evolution without immediate displacement.  
**Fit:** "Many jobs evolve."  
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777

---

## 6. U.S. young-worker employment divergence

**Observed through Jun 2026:** ages 22–25 in highly AI-exposed occupations about 19% below comparison trajectory; no economy-wide displacement.  
**Demonstrates:** concentrated effects can coexist with small aggregate effects.  
**Fit:** entry-level work; balanced evidence.  
**Source:** https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/  
**Recheck:** yes.

---

## 7. 41-country junior-share evidence

**Observed:** 1.25b job postings + 154m employment records; AI-adopting foreign affiliates reduce junior workforce share, mainly because senior employment grows faster.  
**Demonstrates:** ratios can change without absolute junior collapse.  
**Fit:** entry-level nuance.  
**Source:** https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/how-does-ai-change-labor-demand/  
**Recheck:** yes; new working paper.

---

## 8. Online freelance writing/coding demand

**Observed:** one study reports 21% relative decline in automation-prone writing/coding job posts within eight months of ChatGPT; remaining work more complex/higher paid.  
**Demonstrates:** substitution and task upgrading in a fast-moving digital market.  
**Fit:** "Some work disappears / gets harder to enter."  
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID4991774_code2969338.pdf?abstractid=4991774&mirid=1  
**Caveat:** platform market, not economy-wide employment.

---

## 9. Fiverr content-generation demand

**Observed:** reduced demand for human content-generation services; idea-planning demand increased in the study.  
**Demonstrates:** task reallocation, not uniform collapse.  
**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4686658

---

## 10. Klarna customer service

**Observed in company filing:** AI assistant handled 80% of customer-service chats in 2025; estimated equivalent work of >850 agents and ~$59m cost savings; human support retained.  
**Demonstrates:** automation at scale plus retained human channel.  
**Fit:** current replacement/augmentation boundary.  
**Source:** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2003292/000200329226000007/klar-20251231.htm  
**Caveat:** company self-report; "equivalent work" is not independently verified job-loss count.

---

## 11. Anthropic Claude.ai usage

**Observed:** initial dataset classified 57% of usage as augmentation, 43% automation.  
**Demonstrates:** current interactive usage leans collaborative.  
**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-anthropic-economic-index  
**Caveat:** one provider and self-selected user base.

---

## 12. Anthropic Claude Code

**Observed:** coding-agent interactions classified 79% automation.  
**Demonstrates:** more agentic interfaces shift use toward direct execution.  
**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/news/impact-software-development  
**Fit:** qualification to "most current use is augmentation."

---

## 13. Australian businesses — adoption jump

**Observed:** ABS 2024–25: 12% of businesses report AI use vs 1% in 2022–23.  
**Demonstrates:** rapid adoption from a low base.  
**Fit:** Australian context.  
**Source:** https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/technology-and-innovation/characteristics-australian-business/latest-release  
**Caveat:** use, not intensity or employment impact.

---

## 14. ABS internal/public AI use

**Observed:** classification ML, Census chatbot, drafting/summarising/transcription with human review.  
**Demonstrates:** augmentation with governance in public sector.  
**Source:** https://www.abs.gov.au/about/legislation-and-policy/ai-transparency-statement  
**Print:** very suitable Australian case.

---

## 15. Industrial robots in U.S. local labour markets

**Observed:** greater robot exposure associated with lower employment-to-population ratio and wages in affected commuting zones.  
**Demonstrates:** local displacement can be real even without "all jobs" disappearing.  
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w23285  
**Fit:** Historical shifts honestly.

---

## 16. French robot adopters

**Observed:** adopting firms grew productivity and employment, but industry employment fell as competitors lost share.  
**Demonstrates:** firm-level and economy/industry-level effects can point in opposite directions.  
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w26738  
**Print:** excellent counterintuitive example.

---

## 17. Long-term displaced-worker losses

**Observed:** high-tenure displaced workers in the study experienced long-term earnings losses averaging about 25% per year.  
**Demonstrates:** transition costs can persist.  
**Source:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v83y1993i4p685-709.html  
**Fit:** Watch Out callout.

---

## 18. New work since 1940

**Observed:** majority of current U.S. employment in job specialties introduced after 1940; new-work composition changed over time.  
**Demonstrates:** economies create new tasks/jobs, but distribution matters.  
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w30389  
**Fit:** "New jobs emerge."

---

# Best five for the printed chapter

Not a ranking of political or occupational outcomes; simply the strongest editorial mix:

- customer support augmentation;
- experienced developers slowed;
- U.S. young-worker payroll divergence;
- displaced-worker long-run losses;
- Australian JSA/ABS adoption evidence.

Together they prevent the chapter from leaning either utopian or alarmist.
