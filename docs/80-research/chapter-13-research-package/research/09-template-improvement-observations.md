# 09 — Template Improvement Observations

These are research-based observations about the current Chapter 13 draft/template. They are **not rewrites**. The later writing system should decide what to change.

---

## Observation 1 — Opening rationale is good but unsupported

**Current section/topic:** opening paragraph — no named products because they date quickly.  
**Weak/incomplete:** makes a plausible assertion without concrete evidence.  
**Research suggests:** briefly ground it with rebrands/model retirement/terms changes.  
**Stronger evidence:** Google Bard→Gemini rebrand; OpenAI/Anthropic model retirements; Microsoft's 2026 privacy-page version warning.  
**Possible replacement example:** generic "a tool can keep the same service while changing its name, models or privacy controls within a year."  
**Affects:** depth, credibility.  
**Sources:** Google rebrand; OpenAI retirement; Anthropic model deprecations.

---

## Observation 2 — "Six categories" reads too much like six product silos

**Current section/topic:** The Six Categories.  
**Weak/incomplete:** by 2026, general assistants combine chat, research/search, images, voice, files and coding; productivity/OS/search products embed AI.  
**Research suggests:** retain all six required headings but explicitly frame them as capabilities/jobs rather than six separate subscriptions.  
**Stronger evidence:** current provider feature sets in `11-companion-website-material.md`.  
**Possible replacement example:** task-first diagram rather than six logos/apps around a hub.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, clarity, usefulness.  
**Sources:** provider plan/feature pages.

---

## Observation 3 — Chat AI description is narrower than current capability

**Current section/topic:** Chat AI = writing, summarising, brainstorming, explaining.  
**Weak/incomplete:** chat is increasingly the umbrella interface for research, images, files, voice, coding and connected actions.  
**Research suggests:** explain chat as conversational interface that may expose multiple capabilities.  
**Affects:** accuracy, durability.  
**Boundary:** don't turn section into current feature catalogue.

---

## Observation 4 — Image AI example (flyer/birthday card) undersells modern use

**Current section/topic:** Image AI.  
**Weak/incomplete:** examples are harmless but make the category feel like novelty generation. Current systems edit existing images, support mockups, analysis/description and accessibility.  
**Research suggests:** include editing/visual concepts/accessibility, while Chapter 8 owns creative depth.  
**Possible example:** "remove an object from a photo or create a visual mock-up before paying a designer."  
**Affects:** usefulness/depth.  
**Sources:** current platform capability/accessibility pages.

---

## Observation 5 — Research AI may imply more accuracy than evidence supports

**Current section/topic:** Research AI "usually with more emphasis on sourcing and accuracy checks".  
**Weak/incomplete:** a beginner can read this as "research AI doesn't hallucinate".  
**Research suggests:** distinguish source visibility from source correctness. Citation presence enables checking; it does not certify the synthesis.  
**Possible evidence box:** open a citation and check whether it supports the exact generated sentence.  
**Affects:** safety, accuracy, connection to Chapter 4.  
**Sources:** conceptual; no provider can guarantee all cited synthesis is correct.

---

## Observation 6 — Productivity AI needs an access trade-off

**Current section/topic:** "easiest category to start with, since it's already sitting inside software you're paying for anyway."  
**Weak/incomplete:** convenience is true in some cases, but embedded AI can have access to email/files/calendar/screen and AI may be an additional paid feature or bundled into a changed subscription.  
**Research suggests:** "already included" is a reason to evaluate first, not to assume free/safe.  
**Affects:** balance, privacy, cost.  
**Sources:** Microsoft/Google current plan and privacy pages.

---

## Observation 7 — Coding AI audience sentence is fine, but category now includes agents

**Current section/topic:** coding AI writes/explains/debugs code.  
**Weak/incomplete:** current tools may navigate repositories, run commands and make multi-file changes.  
**Research suggests:** mention action-taking capability only enough to explain that permissions/risk increase; reserve implementation/agent detail for later books.  
**Affects:** currency, scope control.  
**Source:** current coding-agent product changes; OpenAI Lockdown/agent-risk material.

---

## Observation 8 — Voice AI should include transcription/accessibility/privacy

**Current section/topic:** spoken interaction where typing isn't practical.  
**Weak/incomplete:** misses transcription, captions, translation and accessibility; also misses microphone/recording privacy.  
**Research suggests:** broaden use cases while noting transcript errors/consent.  
**Affects:** inclusion/usefulness.

---

## Observation 9 — "Most people use one or two" needs evidence or softer phrasing

**Current section/topic:** paragraph after six categories.  
**Weak/incomplete:** the draft states a behavioural generalisation without cited evidence. Because products themselves converge, a person can use many capabilities through one subscription.  
**Research suggests:** avoid numeric-ish behavioural implication unless sourced. Say readers do not need a tool in every category.  
**Affects:** factual precision.  
**Source:** usage studies show task variety but not this exact one-or-two-tool claim.

---

## Observation 10 — Evaluation checklist is too compressed for riskier use

**Current section/topic:** How to Evaluate a New AI Tool.  
**Weak/incomplete:** misses developer legitimacy, cancellation route, permissions/account connections, human review/retention, export/deletion, age suitability, accessibility, source behaviour.  
**Research suggests:** two-tier evaluation model (short everyday + deeper paid/sensitive/connected check).  
**Affects:** usefulness, safety, depth.  
**Sources:** OAIC, ACCC, eSafety, platform permission docs, provider policies.

---

## Observation 11 — "Where it's stored and who can see it" needs a better privacy model

**Current section/topic:** evaluation Try This callout.  
**Weak/incomplete:** geography/access alone misses training, retention, memory, human review, sharing, third-party connectors, deletion.  
**Research suggests:** introduce a plain-language separation of "used to train / kept / remembered / reviewed / shared".  
**Affects:** factual usefulness.  
**Sources:** OpenAI/Google/Microsoft/Anthropic current privacy docs.

---

## Observation 12 — Safe experimentation needs concrete "do not start with" examples

**Current section/topic:** Experimenting Safely.  
**Weak/incomplete:** "don't hand it sensitive information" is sound but abstract for beginners.  
**Research suggests:** give concise examples: passwords/recovery codes/identity docs/bank logins/customer records/private health/legal material/other people's personal data.  
**Affects:** safety, practical value.  
**Source:** OAIC best-practice position + ordinary security hygiene.

---

## Observation 13 — Known-answer testing needs a limitation sentence

**Current section/topic:** test a question you already know.  
**Weak/incomplete:** could imply one successful test establishes reliability.  
**Research suggests:** say it is a smoke test, not proof of performance on harder unknown tasks.  
**Affects:** accuracy/critical thinking.

---

## Observation 14 — Scam section conflates "thin wrapper" with low value

**Current section/topic:** "Thin wrappers charge for access to a free or cheap underlying AI service..."  
**Weak/incomplete:** architecture is not misconduct. Many legitimate AI products are built on third-party models.  
**Research suggests:** evaluate added value/transparency/data handling instead of using wrapper status as a red flag by itself.  
**Affects:** fairness, factual balance.  
**Possible replacement question:** "What value is this app adding, and what data/access does it receive?"

---

## Observation 15 — "Unclear pricing ... sales call" is too broad

**Current section/topic:** unclear pricing warning.  
**Weak/incomplete:** sales-assisted pricing is normal for complex enterprise software and is not inherently suspicious.  
**Research suggests:** for a consumer AI app, hidden recurring pricing is a warning; for enterprise services, quote-based pricing may be normal.  
**Affects:** balance/precision.

---

## Observation 16 — Excessive permissions needs proportionality, not blanket suspicion

**Current section/topic:** excessive permissions.  
**Weak/incomplete:** microphone/files/calendar can be legitimate depending on feature.  
**Research suggests:** teach "permission must match function" and highlight browser extension breadth/action permissions.  
**Affects:** clarity/safety.  
**Sources:** Apple/Android/Chrome permission docs.

---

## Observation 17 — Fake reviews section can be stronger and Australian

**Current section/topic:** fake reviews/inflated claims.  
**Weak/incomplete:** no evidence/check method.  
**Research suggests:** use ACCC guidance: fake/misleading reviews unlawful; look for bursts, generic wording, similarity, and multiple independent sources.  
**Affects:** practical usefulness, jurisdiction relevance.  
**Source:** https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/online-reviews-for-product-and-services

---

## Observation 18 — "Most AI products aren't scams" is unnecessary and unquantified

**Current section/topic:** scam-section qualifier.  
**Weak/incomplete:** likely intended to prevent alarmism, but the package has no representative denominator for "AI products".  
**Research suggests:** replace with a non-quantified balanced statement: legitimate products coexist with documented imitations, malware lures and deceptive subscriptions.  
**Affects:** evidence quality/balance.

---

## Observation 19 — "Scams aren't rare or obvious" is also too broad

**Current section/topic:** myths paragraph.  
**Weak/incomplete:** "not rare" is a prevalence claim. Vendor reports prove existence and campaign reach, not market prevalence.  
**Research suggests:** "documented often enough to justify basic checks" only if writer is comfortable with qualitative phrasing; stronger is simply "documented in app stores, browser extensions and fake websites."  
**Affects:** evidence precision.

## Observation 24 — Diagram should probably be task-first

**Current section/topic:** six categories around "what task do I need this for?"  
**Weak/incomplete:** six spokes can still visually imply six tools.  
**Research suggests:** start with task question, branch to capability, then a final shared box: "Can a tool you already use do this?"  
**Affects:** clarity/anti-catalogue goal.  
**See:** `10-editorial-opportunities.md`.

---

## Observation 25 — Core takeaway is strong but can absorb switching-cost nuance

**Current section/topic:** Core Takeaway.  
**Weak/incomplete:** "literacy over loyalty" is strong but could sound like frequent switching is virtuous.  
**Research suggests:** add concept that staying with a useful tool is fine; literacy means knowing why it still earns a place in the toolkit and being able to reassess when conditions change.  
**Affects:** balance.
