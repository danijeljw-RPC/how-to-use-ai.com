# Source Catalogue / Working Bibliography — Chapter 13

**Research/access date:** 2026-10-01 unless another access date is stated.  
**Purpose:** Working bibliography for the Chapter 13 research package. URLs are direct where possible. Dynamic product/help pages should be rechecked immediately before publication.

## How to read this catalogue

- **Primary** means legislation, regulator/government material, provider/platform documentation, or the original research publication.
- **Independent** means research/reporting not controlled by the product provider being assessed.
- **Provider operational data** can show what happens within that provider's service but is not representative of the whole population.
- **Security-vendor research** establishes documented threats/campaigns; detection counts should not be converted into population prevalence.
- **Consumer-organisation research** is useful for consumer experience/testing but has an advocacy role and often represents a snapshot in time.
- **Recheck = Yes** means the fact, law, setting, plan, price, interface or product status is volatile enough to verify shortly before publication.

# Source-selection notes for the later writer

## Prefer these sources for durable printed-book claims

1. OAIC AI/privacy guidance.
2. ACCC consumer guidance and Australian legislation.
3. eSafety Australian child research.
4. Long & Magerko / UNESCO for literacy framing.
5. Independent survey data such as Pew only when the population is clearly identified.
6. Platform permission/cancellation documentation for generic practical facts.

## Prefer current provider pages for website-only specifics

Use provider documentation for:

- what the company says its current setting does;
- which plan/tier the statement applies to;
- retention period stated by the provider;
- current model/product lifecycle;
- current plan name/price.

Always label these as **provider-stated** rather than independently verified.

## Use security reports with this wording discipline

Good:

> Security researchers documented AI-themed malicious extensions/sites/apps using the popularity of generative AI as a lure.

Avoid unless representative evidence exists:

> AI apps are commonly malicious.

The first statement is supported by campaign evidence. The second is a prevalence claim that these sources do not establish.

## Use subscription research with this population discipline

Good:

> Australian consumer research has documented substantial frustration with subscription cancellation generally, which is relevant because many consumer AI tools are sold as recurring subscriptions.

Avoid:

> Three quarters of AI subscribers struggle to cancel.

The CPRC statistic is about subscriptions broadly, not AI subscriptions specifically.
