# Recheck Before Publication — Chapter 13

**Research baseline:** 2026-10-01  
**Purpose:** A publication-time verification list for facts likely to change faster than the chapter's durable principles.

The chapter can remain durable only if volatile examples, laws and provider settings are either omitted from print or reverified shortly before publication.

# Priority 1 — Australian law and regulation

## Children's Online Privacy Code

- **Current status:** OAIC is developing the Code; the legislated timetable requires the final code to be developed and registered by **10 December 2026**.
- **Recheck:** immediately after 2026-12-10 and again before publication.
- **Verify:** final scope, covered services, age-assurance/consent requirements, commencement/transitional dates, OAIC guidance.
- **Source:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-registers/privacy-codes/childrens-online-privacy-code

## Unfair Trading Practices Act 2026 / subscription contracts

- **Current status:** the Competition and Consumer Amendment (Unfair Trading Practices) Act 2026 is enacted; relevant commencement is **1 July 2027**.
- **Recheck:** before publication and around 2027-07-01 if the book/site remains in production.
- **Verify:** commencement, regulations, ACCC guidance, subscription-specific obligations and enforcement approach.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00064
  - https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/andrew-leigh-2025/media-releases/unfair-trading-tricks-and-traps-be-banned

## Privacy Act / APP reform

- **Recheck:** any amendments enacted after the research date, especially small-business coverage, deletion/access rights, automated decision/AI-specific obligations and children's privacy.
- **Start at:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles

# Priority 7 — Scam/security examples

The existing examples are useful historical cases. Recheck whether stronger, more current primary cases exist before publication.

Look specifically for:

- fake AI apps in official stores;
- malicious browser extensions;
- malware through fake AI image/video sites or ads;
- phishing/billing scams impersonating AI providers;
- deceptive AI subscription/fleeceware enforcement;
- Australian Scamwatch/ACSC alerts specifically involving AI-product impersonation.

For every new security report ask:

1. Is this a documented incident/campaign or representative prevalence data?
2. Is the source a vendor selling security products?
3. Is there corroborating platform/regulator evidence?
4. Does it teach a durable user action?

Do not turn detection counts into "your chance of being scammed".

# Publication-day verification questions

Before the Chapter 13 manuscript is frozen, the writer/editor should be able to answer:

- [ ] Does every product-specific claim have an as-of date?
- [ ] Are all current provider rules linked to the exact product/tier they describe?
- [ ] Have "training", "retention", "memory", "human review", "sharing" and "deletion" remained separate concepts?
- [ ] Are pending Australian reforms clearly distinguished from law already in force?
- [ ] Has the Children's Online Privacy Code reached final form?
- [ ] Are any statistics being applied only to the population actually studied?
- [ ] Are security-vendor reports described as documented cases/telemetry rather than market prevalence?
- [ ] Are wrappers treated neutrally unless deceptive conduct/value/data handling is evidenced?
- [ ] Does the category section make clear that one service may cover several categories?
- [ ] Does the chapter avoid named-product endorsement/ranking?
- [ ] Are prices absent from print or unmistakably dated?
- [ ] Are platform cancellation/refund instructions current?
- [ ] Are accessibility features/limitations current for Australia?
- [ ] Has material drifted into Chapters 7/8/9/11/14 or Books 2/4/5 unnecessarily?
- [ ] Does the core takeaway still read as "understand and reassess" rather than "switch tools frequently"?

If all volatile details are moved to the companion website, this checklist becomes much shorter and the printed chapter remains considerably more durable.
