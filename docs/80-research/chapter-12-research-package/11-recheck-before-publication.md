## Research gaps worth filling if time allows

1. **Australian observed hiring/wage evidence** specifically linking GenAI adoption to changing demand for communication, judgement, leadership or systems skills.
2. **Care/trades/hospitality examples** where AI changes tasks but physical presence, trust or practical skill remains central; avoid office-only evidence.
3. **Longitudinal evidence** on whether novice workers using GenAI develop equivalent domain expertise over several years.
4. **Real-world communication disclosure studies** beyond simulated messages, especially long-term relationship effects.
5. **Systems-thinking and AI field cases** with measurable outcomes; current evidence is stronger educationally/conceptually than in AI-assisted workplace trials.
6. **Leadership during AI adoption** from independently documented organisations, not vendor case studies.
7. **Adult AI-assisted learning trials** comparable to the Bastani school study.
8. **Older-adult AI learning evidence** relevant to the book's broad audience.
9. **Union/worker perspectives in Australia** on capability, deskilling and algorithmic management.
10. **Physical/practical skills** as an omitted category: determine whether Chapter 12 should acknowledge them briefly or leave them outside its approved list.

## Claims that should not appear without qualification

Do not publish these formulations as unqualified facts:

- "AI cannot be empathetic."
- "AI cannot be creative."
- "AI cannot lead."
- "AI cannot understand context."
- "Systems thinking stays human."
- "Human plus AI is always better than either alone."
- "Keeping a human in the loop prevents AI errors."
- "Using AI weakens critical thinking."
- "Using AI improves critical thinking."
- "Soft skills will become more valuable in every job."
- "Emotional intelligence is a single proven measurable trait."
- "Growth mindset makes people adaptable."
- "AI literacy is the most important skill of the future."
- "Prompt engineering is the most important future skill."
- "Older workers cannot keep up with AI."
- "Learning to code is necessary to remain relevant."

Each has either counter-evidence, construct ambiguity or a context-dependent truth condition.

## Final publication test for each skill section

Before finalising Chapter 12, verify that every section answers all six questions:

1. **What does this skill mean in plain English?**
2. **What evidence says it matters?**
3. **What can current AI already do here?**
4. **What human contribution remains important—and why?**
5. **How could AI use weaken or change the skill?**
6. **What can an ordinary reader realistically do to build/protect it?**

If a section cannot answer #3, it is probably too reassuring. If it cannot answer #6, it is probably still motivational rather than practical.
