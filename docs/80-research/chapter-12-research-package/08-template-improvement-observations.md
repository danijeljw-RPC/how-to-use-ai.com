# 08 — Research-Based Template Improvement Observations

This file critiques the existing Chapter 12 template. It does **not** rewrite it.

---

## Observation 1 — Opening claim needs a definition of "more important"

**Current topic:** Introduction / Key Idea.  
**Weakness:** "Human capability becomes more important, not less" can be read as an economic forecast, a universal labour-market claim, or a philosophical claim.  
**Research suggests:** distinguish complementary economic value, share of remaining human work, safety/accountability importance and personal/social importance.  
**Stronger evidence:** Deming; Agrawal/Gans/Goldfarb; OECD; JSA.  
**Possible replacement example:** AI customer-support productivity narrowing novice gaps paired with judgement/verification examples.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, balance, clarity.  
**Sources:**  
https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/132/4/1593/3861633  
https://hbr.org/2017/10/how-ai-will-change-strategy-a-thought-experiment  
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html

## Observation 2 — The effort-versus-judgement split should be a spectrum

**Current topic:** Intro and all skill sections.  
**Weakness:** The existing wording can imply stable categories in which AI does effort and humans do judgement.  
**Research suggests:** task boundaries move as systems improve. AI already performs components of evaluation, persuasion, creativity and social inference. Human judgement is also vulnerable to automation bias.  
**Stronger evidence:** Vaccaro meta-analysis; Cecil over-reliance experiments; AI human-skill studies.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, depth.  
**Sources:**  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60220-5

---

## Observation 3 — Communication example is plausible but unsourced and too clean

**Current topic:** Communication.  
**Weakness:** Two people receive identical AI drafts and one human edits better; useful but generic. It assumes the central distinction rather than demonstrating it.  
**Research suggests:** source disclosure, sincerity/trust and customer-support field evidence provide stronger grounding.  
**Possible replacement example:** AI-attributed corporate apology perceived as less sincere; or novice support agents improving with GenAI, showing AI can supply part of communication competence.  
**Affects:** depth, balance, usefulness.  
**Sources:**  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0363811124000997  
https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

## Observation 4 — Judgement is underdeveloped relative to its importance

**Current topic:** Judgement.  
**Weakness:** Current draft says judgement becomes the bottleneck but gives no concrete evidence of humans succeeding or failing at it.  
**Research suggests:** make over-reliance, verification, domain expertise and accountability central.  
**Stronger examples:** wrong AI advice in personnel selection; Australian lawyer sanctions; Federal Court AI Practice Note; jagged frontier.  
**Affects:** depth, usefulness, evidence.  
**Sources:**  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60220-5  
https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/news-updates/news/statement-mr-dayal-matter  
https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/law-and-practice/practice-documents/practice-notes/gpn-ai

## Observation 5 — "None of that is something AI does" in Leadership is too broad

**Current topic:** Leadership.  
**Weakness:** AI can generate priorities, analyse uncertainty, coach, schedule, assign and evaluate; algorithmic management already automates management-like activity.  
**Research suggests:** define durable leadership around coordination, legitimacy, relationships, commitment and accountability rather than task incapability.  
**Possible replacement example:** NBER AI-agent leadership measurement; a documented organisational change case could be added if a high-quality one is sourced before drafting.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, balance.  
**Source:**  
https://www.nber.org/papers/w33662

## Observation 6 — Leadership is too workplace-manager centred

**Current topic:** Leadership.  
**Weakness:** Team-lead quarterly priority example addresses the 35% professional audience but under-serves the 65% consumer emphasis.  
**Research suggests:** include informal leadership—family care decisions, community/volunteer groups, clubs, parenting boundaries—without inventing personal stories.  
**Affects:** audience fit.  
**Evidence status:** general application rather than a single empirical case; label scenarios as scenarios.

## Observation 7 — Creativity section should avoid repeating Chapter 8

**Current topic:** Creativity.  
**Weakness:** Current draft mostly restates Chapter 8's claim.  
**Research suggests:** narrow Chapter 12 to problem framing, creative problem-solving, selection, experimentation and preserving human divergence when AI makes ideas cheap.  
**Stronger evidence:** current AI ideation performance; 2026 linguistic-homogenisation evidence, with cross-reference to Chapter 8 for the full debate.  
**Affects:** structure, repetition, depth.

## Observation 8 — Systems-thinking claim about AI is not durable

**Current topic:** Systems Thinking.  
**Weakness:** "broad, contextual judgement AI ... doesn't reliably have" and supplier-change example risk becoming outdated. With enough data/context, AI could identify the correlation itself.  
**Research suggests:** relocate human value to system boundary selection, missing-context recognition, causal testing, second-order effects, values and intervention responsibility.  
**Possible replacement example:** keep the supplier scenario only as a question-framing example, not as something AI inherently cannot find.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, future-proofing.  
**Sources:** systems-thinking reviews and Meadows conceptual work.  
https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/14/9/1115  
https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/

---

## Observation 9 — Emotional intelligence contains the template's weakest categorical claim

**Current topic:** Emotional Intelligence.  
**Weakness:** "stays squarely human" conflicts with evidence that AI responses can be rated highly empathetic and that models can perform well on social-inference tests.  
**Research suggests:** distinguish empathetic output/task performance from felt emotion, relationship, presence, continuity and accountability.  
**Possible replacement example:** JAMA physician/chatbot comparison followed by the manager feedback scenario to show what text ratings do not capture.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, balance, credibility.  
**Sources:**  
https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2804309  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01882-z

## Observation 10 — Emotional intelligence itself needs a caveat

**Current topic:** Emotional Intelligence.  
**Weakness:** Draft treats EI as an uncontroversial single skill.  
**Research suggests:** briefly acknowledge competing ability, trait and mixed models; then use concrete components such as emotion regulation, perspective-taking, empathy and relationship judgement.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, educational value.  
**Source:**  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6546921/

## Observation 11 — Missing domain expertise

**Current topic:** Primarily Judgement.  
**Weakness:** The draft can be read as though generic human judgement is enough to supervise AI in any domain.  
**Research suggests:** explicitly state that good judgement often depends on substantive knowledge; people cannot reliably verify what they do not understand.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy, practical usefulness.  
**Source:**  
https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.03968

## Observation 13 — Missing deskilling/supervision paradox

**Current topic:** Entire chapter.  
**Weakness:** Current draft assumes people possess the skills needed to judge AI but does not ask how they continue developing those skills if AI performs the practice work.  
**Research suggests:** add a short section or recurring caution on protecting capability.  
**Strong examples:** PNAS maths tutor; endoscopy observational study; Bainbridge; Microsoft/CMU survey.  
**Affects:** depth, practicality, originality.  
**Sources:**  
https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/  
https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8

## Observation 14 — "Soft skills aren't vague" needs qualification

**Current topic:** Myth vs Reality paragraph.  
**Weakness:** Some constructs really are defined/measured inconsistently. Emotional intelligence and systems thinking are examples.  
**Research suggests:** say they can be made concrete by focusing on observable capabilities and practices, not that all are precisely settled constructs.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy.  
**Sources:**  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6546921/  
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09500693.2023.2245106

## Observation 15 — The chapter lacks evidence on how skills are developed

**Current topic:** All six skill sections.  
**Weakness:** It tells readers these skills matter but gives almost no actionable development path.  
**Research suggests:** include small practice actions: decision feedback, human-first drafting, role-play, system mapping, reflective learning, community participation and periodic unaided work.  
**Affects:** usefulness, learning outcomes.  
**Sources:** emotional-competency meta-analysis; adult-learning OECD; AI role-play studies.  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11616120/  
https://doi.org/10.1787/ec0624a6-en  
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/tesq.70010

## Observation 17 — Current draft is heavily professional despite consumer-majority audience

**Current topic:** examples across sections.  
**Weakness:** colleague message, team lead, business owner and manager performance review dominate.  
**Research suggests:** add everyday examples for systems thinking, communication, learning and emotional capability: family care, community groups, household decisions, education and personal health-information decisions.  
**Affects:** audience fit.  
**Caution:** generic scenarios must be clearly presented as scenarios unless a sourced case is used.

---

## Observation 18 — Chapter currently lacks counter-evidence about AI's "human" performance

**Current topic:** Myth vs Reality.  
**Weakness:** Reader could leave believing the six skills are safe zones AI cannot enter.  
**Research suggests:** devote at least one concise passage/table to what AI can already do in each area and what the human responsibility is.  
**Affects:** balance, credibility, future-proofing.  
**Sources:** JAMA empathy; Nature ToM; persuasion; creativity; meta-analysis of human-AI combinations.

---

## Observation 19 — Core takeaway can stay, but should not imply immunity from automation

**Current topic:** Core Takeaway / Recap.  
**Weakness:** Current wording could be read as a guarantee that all named skills "become more valuable" in all contexts.  
**Research suggests:** preserve the approved intent while qualifying that AI can assist/perform parts of each and that the relative value shifts toward framing, verification, context, relationships and responsibility.  
**Affects:** factual accuracy and longevity.

---

## Observation 20 — The plan says no diagram is required, but one table is strongly justified

**Current topic:** presentation.  
**Weakness:** Six prose sections risk feeling list-like.  
**Research suggests:** a compact comparison table may be more useful than a diagram:

| Capability | AI can already help/do | Human responsibility to protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communication | draft/rewrite/translate/persuade | audience, intent, relationship, accountability |
| Judgement | analyse/recommend/rank | evidence, calibration, context, consequence |
| Leadership | analyse/plan/coordinate components | legitimacy, commitment, trust, responsibility |
| Creativity | generate/recombine/critique | framing, taste, experimentation, selection |
| Systems thinking | analyse data/maps/connections | boundaries, missing context, values, intervention |
| Emotional capability | empathetic language/social inference | presence, regulation, relationship, accountability |

**Affects:** clarity, practical usefulness.  
**Status:** editorial opportunity, not required by plan.
