# 07 — Contrasting Viewpoints and Uncertainties

The chapter needs disagreement where evidence genuinely disagrees, not artificial balance. Several apparently contradictory claims can both be true because they refer to different outcomes, populations or timescales.

---

# Debate 1 — Do human skills become more valuable as AI improves?

## Position A: complements rise in value

Reasoning:

- AI lowers the cost of prediction/production.
- Scarce complementary inputs—judgement, context, accountability, relationships—become more important to the combined process.
- Historical labour-market data show social skills growing in importance alongside technical/cognitive capabilities.

Evidence:

- Agrawal, Gans & Goldfarb economic complementarity argument: https://hbr.org/2017/10/how-ai-will-change-strategy-a-thought-experiment
- Deming historical labour evidence: https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/132/4/1593/3861633
- JSA augmentation framing: https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study

## Position B: AI makes some human skills less scarce

Reasoning:

- GenAI can produce competent language, analysis and social-style responses.
- Novices can inherit patterns from stronger performers through AI assistance.
- If everyone's baseline improves, a capability may become less differentiating even while remaining useful.

Evidence:

- Customer support field study: https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
- AI empathy: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2804309
- AI persuasion: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61345-5
- AI creativity: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01953-1

## Research synthesis

Both can be true. A capability can remain essential to the process while becoming less scarce at the level of first-pass output. Value may move **within** the capability: from drafting to audience judgement, from idea production to taste, from analysis to problem framing, from empathy-like language to relationship/accountability.

# Debate 2 — Will AI democratise expertise or increase the premium on experts?

## Democratisation argument

AI can give less-experienced workers access to patterns, explanations and support that previously required expert assistance. Customer-support evidence shows larger productivity gains for novices.

Autor argues AI could extend the reach of expertise and enable more workers to perform higher-stakes work.

Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w32140

## Expertise-premium argument

When AI output is plausible but fallible, people with deep domain knowledge are better positioned to spot subtle mistakes, formulate better questions and know when exceptions matter. If routine work disappears, organisations may also concentrate responsibility among fewer experienced people.

Tambe's work supports complementarity between domain expertise and algorithmic literacy.

Source: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.03968

## Research synthesis

AI can democratise **access to competent output** while simultaneously increasing the importance of expertise for **verification and high-consequence exceptions**. Which effect dominates depends on task and workflow.

# Debate 3 — Does AI improve or weaken judgement?

## Improvement evidence

AI can expose alternatives, catch errors, provide second opinions and expand information access. In many bounded tasks, AI assistance improves immediate performance.

## Weakening evidence

- automation bias: wrong advice can pull human decisions in the wrong direction;
- deskilling: repeated assistance may reduce unaided performance;
- reduced practice: learners can complete work without internalising the underlying skill;
- anchoring: first AI output can shape subsequent search.

Sources:

- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60220-5
- https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/

## Research synthesis

The useful question is not "Does AI improve thinking?" but **"What role is AI playing in this workflow, and what capability is the person expected to retain?"**

# Debate 4 — Is emotional intelligence a uniquely human advantage?

## Human-centred argument

Human relationships involve presence, reciprocal history, accountability, felt emotion, embodied cues and ongoing consequences. Many care, family and leadership settings depend on these dimensions.

## AI-performance counter-evidence

AI responses can receive higher empathy ratings than human responses in bounded text studies; models also perform strongly on several theory-of-mind-style tasks.

Sources:

- https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2804309
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01882-z

## Research synthesis

"AI cannot be empathetic" is too broad. "AI can write empathetically, therefore it has human emotional intelligence" is also too broad. Task performance and human relationship are different objects of evaluation.

# Debate 5 — Are "soft skills" measurable and teachable?

## Evidence for trainability

Workplace emotional-competency meta-analysis finds moderate average improvement across interventions, though heterogeneity and quality limitations are substantial.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11616120/

Systems-thinking education uses mapping/simulation/experiential approaches and some measurement tools exist.

Sources:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7403244/
- https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/14/9/1115

## Reasons for caution

- constructs such as EI have competing definitions;
- self-report scales can overlap with personality or confidence;
- training studies can be small, heterogeneous or commercially motivated;
- transfer from course to real-world behaviour is not guaranteed.

## Research synthesis

Avoid both extremes: these capabilities are neither mystical innate traits nor simple competencies mastered after a short course.

# Debate 6 — Does growth mindset make people more adaptable?

## Supportive interpretation

Some interventions, particularly targeted/high-fidelity ones, report small-to-moderate benefits for selected populations or outcomes.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36227318/

## Critical interpretation

A 2023 meta-analysis found very small overall achievement effects and no significant effect in the highest-quality subset after publication-bias analysis.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000352

## Research synthesis

Do not build the chapter's adaptability advice on mindset slogans. Continued learning depends on practice, opportunity, feedback, time, resources and context as well as beliefs.

# Debate 7 — Will AI make communication more personalised or more homogeneous?

## Personalisation argument

AI can adapt tone, language, reading level and format quickly, potentially making communication more accessible and tailored.

## Homogenisation evidence

Large-scale 2026 evidence indicates LLM rewriting can reduce linguistic variation and amplify dominant characteristics.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02550-0

## Research synthesis

AI may increase **surface personalisation** while reducing **population-level diversity**. These outcomes are not mutually exclusive.

# Debate 8 — Do people always want a human when the issue is personal?

Evidence on algorithm aversion/appreciation is context-dependent. People sometimes prefer algorithms, especially when they perceive them as objective or accurate; in other settings, disclosure that communication came from AI can reduce sincerity or trust.

Useful sources:

- AI delegation experiment: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0298037
- source disclosure in AI messages: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882124000185
- corporate apology attribution: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0363811124000997

The chapter should avoid saying "people always want humans for important matters." Preferences depend on task, perceived stakes, identity, transparency, accuracy and relationship.

# Debate 9 — Is systems thinking a distinct durable skill?

## Case for inclusion

Systems methods encourage attention to feedback, delays, interactions, boundaries and unintended consequences—valuable where narrow optimisation causes side effects.

## Caveat

Definitions and measurement are fragmented across disciplines, and modern AI can increasingly analyse broad contexts if given data and tools. It is unsafe to define systems thinking merely as "seeing connections AI misses."

## Research synthesis

Keep the skill because it is useful to readers, but explain it behaviourally and avoid claiming a settled universal construct.

# Debate 10 — Does staying relevant mean learning AI tools?

## Yes, partly

JSA, UNESCO and regulatory developments treat AI literacy as an increasingly relevant capability. A person who refuses to learn any AI tooling may lose opportunities to work efficiently with changing systems.

## But not as Chapter 12's main argument

Tool knowledge dates quickly. Chapter 13 exists precisely to cover practical AI-tool literacy. Chapter 12 is stronger when it explains the capabilities needed to use changing tools well.

## Research synthesis

The two chapters are complements:

- Chapter 12: **what to invest in about yourself**;
- Chapter 13: **how to assemble and evaluate the tools**.
