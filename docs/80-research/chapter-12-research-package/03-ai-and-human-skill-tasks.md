# 03 — AI and Tasks That Look Like "Human Skills"

## Why this file exists

The current Chapter 12 template understates how capable AI already is at tasks commonly labelled communication, empathy, creativity, persuasion and social inference. A credible chapter should present this evidence directly, then make more precise distinctions about human contribution.

The studies below do **not** establish that AI possesses human emotions, consciousness, moral responsibility or lived experience. They do establish that "AI cannot do X" becomes fragile when X is defined by observable task performance.

---

## 1. Empathetic response writing

**Study:** Ayers et al. (2023), *JAMA Internal Medicine*  
**Question:** How do physician and chatbot responses to patient questions compare when evaluated for quality and empathy?  
**Material:** 195 question-response exchanges from Reddit's r/AskDocs; three licensed health-professional evaluators per pair, producing 585 evaluations.  
**Finding:** Evaluators preferred chatbot responses in 78.6% of comparisons and rated them higher for quality and empathy.  
**Important confound:** chatbot responses were substantially longer on average than physician replies.  
**Supports:** AI can produce written responses that trained evaluators perceive as highly empathetic in this bounded setting.  
**Does not support:** AI feels empathy, provides better clinical care, understands a patient as a person over time, or should replace clinicians.

Source:  
https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2804309

**Chapter use:** Emotional intelligence counter-evidence; Myth vs Reality; distinction between empathetic language and relationship/accountability.

**Recheck before publication:** newer model studies likely exist.

## 2. Theory-of-mind-style inference

**Study:** Strachan et al. (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour*  
**Participants/comparison:** 1,907 human participants plus GPT and LLaMA models.  
**Tasks:** established theory-of-mind tests covering indirect requests, false beliefs, misdirection and faux pas.  
**Finding:** GPT-4 matched or exceeded human performance on several tasks while showing weaknesses on others; model families exhibited distinctive failure patterns.  
**Supports:** modern language models can solve several text-based tasks requiring inference about beliefs and intentions.  
**Does not support:** the system possesses subjective mental states, real social relationships or general human social understanding.

Source:  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01882-z

**Chapter use:** Emotional intelligence; challenge to "AI lacks any social understanding" wording.

---

## 3. Persuasion

**Study:** 2025 *Nature Communications* paper on LLM persuasion  
**Design:** three preregistered experiments, total N=4,829.  
**Task:** participants read persuasive policy messages generated under experimental conditions.  
**Finding:** LLM messages shifted attitudes relative to neutral controls and could perform comparably to messages from lay humans in the tested conditions.  
**Supports:** persuasive language production is not uniquely human.  
**Does not support:** universal persuasive superiority, durable behaviour change, ethical acceptability, or success in every real social setting.

Source:  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61345-5

**Chapter use:** Communication; a concrete rebuttal to "soft skills are what AI cannot do."

**Recheck before publication.**

---

## 4. Creative ideation

**Study:** Lee & Chung (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour*  
**Design:** five experiments comparing use of GPT-3.5 with comparison conditions on everyday and innovation-oriented ideation tasks.  
**Finding:** AI assistance improved judged creativity on multiple tasks, with effects differing by type of idea generation.  
**Supports:** AI can materially assist creative ideation and raise immediate creative-task performance.  
**Does not support:** AI eliminates human creativity, produces culturally valuable work autonomously, or preserves collective diversity.

Source:  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01953-1

**Chapter use:** limited Chapter 12 creativity evidence; do not duplicate Chapter 8.

## 5. Linguistic homogenisation under AI rewriting

**Study:** Sourati, Karimi-Malekabadi & Dehghani (2026), *Nature Human Behaviour*  
**Data:** three studies, seven datasets, more than 880,000 texts.  
**Finding:** LLM rewriting largely preserved central content while reducing variation in writing complexity and amplifying dominant linguistic characteristics.  
**Supports:** widespread AI-mediated rewriting can change population-level linguistic diversity even when individual text improves or remains competent.  
**Does not support:** every AI-assisted message sounds the same, or that variation necessarily equals quality.

Source:  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02550-0

**Chapter use:** Communication and creativity; why human voice/taste may matter even when AI output is competent.

**Recheck before publication:** new 2026 evidence.

## 6. Human-AI combinations are not automatically superior

**Study:** Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour*  
**Design:** preregistered systematic review/meta-analysis; 106 experimental studies, 370 effect sizes.  
**Finding:** human-AI systems on average underperformed the better of human-alone or AI-alone, though effects varied by task; creation tasks showed more potential for beneficial combination than decision tasks.  
**Supports:** "human in the loop" is not sufficient for good outcomes; collaboration design and relative competence matter.  
**Does not support:** humans and AI should not collaborate.

Source:  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1

**Chapter use:** Judgement; strongest evidence against automatic augmentation optimism.

---

## 7. AI can transfer expert-like patterns to novices

**Study:** Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, *Generative AI at Work*  
**Setting:** 5,179 customer-support agents in a field deployment.  
**Finding:** AI assistance raised productivity by about 14% on average and substantially more for novice/lower-skilled workers, with much smaller gains for highly experienced staff.  
**Supports:** AI can compress immediate performance gaps by exposing less-experienced workers to patterns associated with stronger performers.  
**Does not support:** novices thereby acquire the same underlying expertise or judgement as experienced workers.

Source:  
https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

**Chapter use:** Communication, adaptability, domain expertise; counterpoint to "human skill becomes scarcer."

---

## 8. AI role-play can help practise social language—but human interaction differs

**Study:** Sim, Kim & Ku (2025), *TESOL Quarterly*  
**Participants:** 40 Korean EFL learners randomly assigned to ChatGPT or peer interaction.  
**Task:** role-play requests/refusals with feedback.  
**Finding:** ChatGPT interaction produced stronger short-term learning on requests; peer interaction showed better long-term retention in that part of the study.  
**Supports:** AI can be a useful low-cost practice partner.  
**Does not support:** AI role-play is equivalent to real social practice.

Source:  
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/tesq.70010

A separate 2025 study of 75 Japanese university students comparing AI and human role-play found AI could elicit interactionally relevant behaviour but produced distinctive turn-taking differences such as overlaps and gaps.

Source:  
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/tesq.70028

**Chapter use:** practical skill-building; useful website exercise.

# The distinctions that survive the evidence

## Performing a task vs holding responsibility

A model can produce legal analysis, medical suggestions, a performance-review script or a strategic memo. Professional and organisational responsibility still sits with people and institutions. The Federal Court of Australia's 2026 practice note makes this explicit for legal work: AI use does not remove the duty to verify material presented to the court.

Source:  
https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/law-and-practice/practice-documents/practice-notes/gpn-ai

## Generating options vs choosing and committing

AI can make the option-generation step nearly free. That makes criteria, trade-offs and follow-through more visible—not automatically more difficult or more highly paid, but more central to the remaining human decision.

## Empathetic language vs relationship

The JAMA result proves readers should not be told that AI cannot produce empathetic responses. It does not erase differences between a generated response and a person who has history, obligations, reciprocal vulnerability and responsibility for what happens next.

## Single-turn evaluation vs long-term trust

Most AI capability benchmarks and experiments are short and bounded. Leadership, care, family relationships and reputation unfold over months or years. Evidence from a one-turn test should not be silently generalised to those domains.

## General knowledge vs local/lived context

AI can be supplied with increasing amounts of context, so "AI lacks context" is not a durable absolute. The practical human issue is often recognising **which** context matters and ensuring it enters the decision.

## What people accept from AI vs what they want from people

Research on source disclosure and algorithm attitudes suggests provenance can change trust or sincerity judgments even when content is identical. Preference for human involvement is contextual, not a universal rule.

# Recommended Myth vs Reality candidates

### Myth: "Soft skills are safe because AI cannot do them."

**Reality:** AI already performs many bounded tasks associated with empathy, persuasion, creativity and social inference. What remains especially important is context, domain knowledge, verification, relationships, decision criteria and responsibility.

### Myth: "If AI performs a skill-like task well, the human skill no longer matters."

**Reality:** automation can make supervision more consequential, but only if the supervising person retains enough knowledge and independence to detect when the system is wrong.

### Myth: "The best arrangement is always AI plus a human."

**Reality:** large-scale meta-analysis finds human-AI combinations do not automatically beat the stronger party working alone. Collaboration design matters.

### Myth: "AI being rated empathetic proves it has emotional intelligence like a person."

**Reality:** high ratings show effective task performance. They do not answer philosophical or relational questions about felt emotion, responsibility or sustained human relationships.
