# Chapter 9 Research — Source Catalogue

**Research date:** 2026-10-01  
**Purpose:** Working bibliography for Chapter 9 research. URLs point to the strongest original or primary source located where practical. Dates and status-sensitive material should be rechecked close to publication.

## How to use this catalogue

- **Primary** means legislation, regulator/government material, original research, court material, standards, or a provider's own policy/disclosure.
- A provider source can establish what that provider says, measures, changes, or observes in its own systems; it does **not** automatically establish ecosystem-wide prevalence.
- Complaint, scam-report and enforcement totals are usually **reported/observed counts**, not complete prevalence estimates.
- Forecasts, scenarios and modelled estimates are labelled so they are not mistaken for observed facts.
- **Recheck: Yes** means the legal, product, market, annual-statistics, litigation, or policy status can change materially before publication.

## Source gaps and cautions for the later writer

1. **No credible Australian national figure isolates losses caused specifically by AI-enabled scams.** Use all-scam totals only when labelled as such.
2. **Deepfake prevalence is fragmented.** Complaint counts, research samples and platform enforcement data measure different things and should not be combined casually.
3. **Election-impact evidence is not the same as content-volume evidence.** A large amount of synthetic content can coexist with limited measurable effect on an election result.
4. **Current facial-recognition performance must not be inferred from 2018 audits.** Use historic studies for lessons and current NIST testing for contemporary capability claims.
5. **Provider privacy pages are snapshots.** Capture date and plan/account type whenever used in prose.
6. **Energy figures need a denominator and workload.** Do not use a text-prompt figure as a proxy for image/video generation or multi-step agentic work.
7. **Competition evidence differs by layer.** Chips, fabrication, cloud, frontier models, distribution and applications may have very different concentration patterns.
8. **Copyright outcomes are fact- and jurisdiction-specific.** A settlement is not a blanket legal ruling; one court decision does not settle Australian law.
9. **The AI Incident Database is a lead generator, not an incidence-rate dataset.**
10. **Regulatory status should be checked at manuscript lock.** Australia, the EU and provider terms are changing too quickly for an undated summary to remain safe.
