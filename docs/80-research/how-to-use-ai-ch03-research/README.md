# How To Use AI.com — Chapter 3 Research Pack

## Purpose

This pack supports Chapter 3, **“What AI Can Actually Do”**, for the planned book and website **How To Use AI.com**.

It is designed to be ingested by another AI system as source material for drafting the chapter. The research deliberately goes deeper than the final chapter should. The writer can select the strongest examples and evidence while keeping the reader-facing chapter light, practical and beginner-friendly.

The supplied chapter plan is preserved as `00-chapter-03-plan.md`.

## Research date

23 September 2026.

## Core editorial position supported by the research

A useful, durable framing is:

> AI is not useful because it is perfect. It is useful because, for the right tasks, it can reduce effort, accelerate iteration, expose alternative ideas, transform information between formats, and provide a fast first pass. Its capabilities are uneven, so the human still has to choose the task, judge the output and verify important claims.

This aligns well with the existing chapter plan while avoiding two bad extremes:

- **“AI is useless because it sometimes gets things wrong.”**
- **“AI can do everything, so human judgement no longer matters.”**

A recurring term in the research is the **jagged technological frontier**: AI may perform extremely well on one task and surprisingly poorly on another task that appears similar in difficulty.

## Important interpretation rule

The research contains evidence from different years, models, professions, languages and experimental settings. Do **not** turn a result from one experiment into a universal claim about all AI systems or all users.

For example:

- A study showing a 40% reduction in time on short professional writing tasks does not mean “AI makes all work 40% faster.”
- A study showing experienced open-source developers were 19% slower with early-2025 AI tools does not mean “AI makes programmers slower.”
- A tutoring RCT in one Harvard physics course does not mean “AI tutors are better than teachers.”

The correct lesson is that outcomes are **task-dependent, user-dependent, tool-dependent and workflow-dependent**.

## Recommended chapter-level takeaway

**AI is best understood as a general-purpose assistant with a wide but uneven set of abilities. Give it work that benefits from language, pattern recognition, transformation, iteration or generation; keep human judgement in the loop when correctness, context or consequences matter.**
