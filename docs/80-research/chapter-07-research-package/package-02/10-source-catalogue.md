# Source Catalogue / Working Bibliography

**Research retrieval date:** 30 September 2026 unless a publication date is stated. Vendor pages are explicitly time-sensitive and should be rechecked before publication.

## S01 — Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence

- **Author/organisation:** Noy, S. & Zhang, W.
- **Publication/source:** Science
- **Date:** 2023-07-13
- **Evidence type:** Controlled experiment
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586
- **Supports / caveat:** 453 professionals; writing tasks; ~40% lower time and ~18% higher quality. Strong drafting evidence; controlled-task caveat.

## S02 — Generative AI at Work

- **Author/organisation:** Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D. & Raymond, L. R.
- **Publication/source:** NBER / Quarterly Journal of Economics
- **Date:** 2023 working paper; 2025 publication
- **Evidence type:** Field deployment / quasi-experimental
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
- **Supports / caveat:** 5,179 support agents; ~14% average productivity gain; ~34% novice/lower-skilled; minimal experienced/high-skilled effect.

## S03 — Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

- **Author/organisation:** Dell'Acqua et al.
- **Publication/source:** Harvard Business School / BCG study
- **Date:** 2023
- **Evidence type:** Experiment with professionals
- **URL:** https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/
- **Supports / caveat:** 758 consultants; gains inside tested AI frontier and poorer performance on an outside-frontier task.

## S04 — Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI

- **Author/organisation:** Dillon, E. W., Jaffe, S., Immorlica, N. & Stanton, C. T.
- **Publication/source:** NBER Working Paper 33795
- **Date:** 2025-05; revised 2025-11
- **Evidence type:** Randomised field experiment
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795
- **Supports / caveat:** 66 firms, 7,137 knowledge workers; treated users spent ~2 fewer hours/week on email in later period; limited broad task-composition change.

## S05 — Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity

- **Author/organisation:** Becker, J., Rush, N., Barnes, E. & Rein, D.
- **Publication/source:** METR
- **Date:** 2025-07
- **Evidence type:** RCT
- **URL:** https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/
- **Supports / caveat:** 16 experienced developers, 246 real tasks; AI allowed increased completion time ~19% in this specialised setting; strong workday-test counterexample.

## S06 — Generative AI and labor market matching efficiency

- **Author/organisation:** Wiles, E. & Horton, J. J.
- **Publication/source:** Working paper / field experiment
- **Date:** 2025
- **Evidence type:** Field experiment
- **URL:** https://john-joseph-horton.com/papers/generative-ai-and-labor-market-matching-efficiency/
- **Supports / caveat:** AI first drafts reduced employer writing time ~44% and increased posting, but posts were more generic and matching did not improve. Local efficiency vs downstream outcome.

## S07 — What can happen when employers use AI to write job posts

- **Author/organisation:** MIT Sloan
- **Publication/source:** MIT Sloan Ideas Made to Matter
- **Date:** 2025-08-11
- **Evidence type:** Institutional summary of S06
- **URL:** https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/what-can-happen-when-employers-use-ai-to-write-job-posts
- **Supports / caveat:** Accessible description of job-post field experiment; reports AI-written posts 15% less likely to lead to hire.

## S08 — Generative AI and the SME Workforce: New Survey Evidence

- **Author/organisation:** OECD
- **Publication/source:** OECD Publishing
- **Date:** 2025-11-05
- **Evidence type:** Representative multi-country SME survey
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1787/2d08b99d-en
- **Supports / caveat:** 5,000+ SMEs in seven countries; adoption, self-reported performance, workload and skill-gap data. Self-report, not causal productivity measurement.

## S09 — The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking

- **Author/organisation:** Lee, H.-P. et al.
- **Publication/source:** CHI 2025 / Microsoft Research
- **Date:** 2025-04
- **Evidence type:** Survey of knowledge workers
- **URL:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/
- **Supports / caveat:** 319 workers, 936 examples; confidence relationships and shift toward verification/integration/stewardship. Self-reported.

## S10 — Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content

- **Author/organisation:** Doshi, A. R. & Hauser, O. P.
- **Publication/source:** Science Advances
- **Date:** 2024-07-12
- **Evidence type:** Controlled online experiment
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290
- **Supports / caveat:** AI ideas improved rated individual stories especially for less-creative writers; AI-assisted stories more similar to one another.

# Source-use notes

## Primary evidence versus vendor documentation

Do not use S19–S25 to prove productivity or independent safety. They support current product/account behaviour and capability claims from the vendor itself.

## Historical-model caution

S14 is useful for the mechanism of fabricated citations but its GPT-3.5/GPT-4 percentages must not be presented as the error rate of 2026 systems.

## Occupation-specific caution

S02, S03 and S05 study different occupations and task structures. Their percentages should remain attached to those populations/settings.

## Self-reported evidence

S08 and S09 are valuable, but the difference between self-report and measured outcomes should be preserved.

## Australian guidance

S16–S18 are particularly useful for an Australian author and examples, but legal/professional obligations should be attributed to the relevant jurisdiction/profession rather than universalised.
