# Better Reserved for Later Chapters

The supplementary research surfaced important material that should not dominate Chapter 7.

## Detailed AI governance frameworks

Topics such as enterprise model inventories, risk committees, formal impact assessments, procurement controls, audit frameworks and standards implementation are useful but would turn the chapter into enterprise governance.

**Chapter 7 use:** one sentence that organisations may have governance processes and approved tools.  
**Later use:** dedicated governance/risk material.

## Cybersecurity and prompt-injection detail

AI systems can introduce security risks through malicious content, connectors, credentials and tool execution. These are important but too specialised for a general workplace-use overview.

**Chapter 7 use:** never paste credentials/secrets; follow approved security policy.  
**Later use:** security chapter or technical volumes.

## Privacy-law analysis

The OAIC material is useful to support minimum-data and due-diligence principles, but detailed Australian Privacy Principles, consent bases and jurisdictional comparisons would overwhelm the practical goal.

## Automated hiring/performance/credit decisions

Consequential algorithmic decision-making raises fairness, explainability, privacy, employment and regulatory issues. Use only as a clear boundary example for “assist versus decide.”

## Workplace monitoring and surveillance

Important future-of-work issue but distinct from “how an ordinary professional can use AI today.”

## Labour-market replacement forecasts

Predictions about which jobs disappear or grow are contested, fast-moving and outside Chapter 7's reader-state goal. Keep any mention tightly focused on task assistance versus whole-job automation.

## Copyright/IP doctrine

Workers should respect organisational rules around intellectual property, but detailed copyright questions belong elsewhere.

# Editorial observations on the existing Chapter 7 template

## 1. “AI is becoming a workplace multiplier” needs one sentence of qualification

Keep the key idea, but avoid implying universal gains. The strongest evidence supports task-dependent augmentation.

### Suggested research-backed editorial direction

“Workplace multiplier” can mean removing friction from suitable tasks while professional judgement and verification remain human responsibilities.

## 2. The report and meeting sections should demonstrate the Chapter 5 pattern more explicitly

The template has good example wording but can be strengthened by showing:

- weak request;
- added audience/source/format constraints;
- what the model should not infer;
- what gets verified.

## 3. Meeting summaries deserve more space than a generic paragraph

They are the best place for the chapter's full demonstration because the same example can carry prompting, extraction, confidentiality and verification.

## 4. Spreadsheet material should distinguish explanation from calculation authority

Current vendor docs themselves warn of inaccurate results. A small formula test example is stronger than merely saying “check the numbers.”

## 5. Research assistance should explicitly separate “finding leads” from “evidence”

The template says first pass, which is good. Add the source-checking sequence so readers know what “verify” actually means.

## 6. Confidentiality wording should avoid the “public forum” analogy as the sole rule

“If you wouldn't post it publicly, don't paste it into AI” is memorable but too coarse. Organisation-managed tools can have different controls, while some information that is not embarrassing/publicly secret may still be restricted by policy or privacy rules.

Better conceptual rule: **know the information, know the system, know the policy.**

## 7. Company-policy advice should not imply every workplace has an IT department or manager who knows the answer

Include small-business/contractor readers. Possible sources of authority include owner, client agreement, professional body, security/privacy policy, or vendor/workspace administrator.

## 8. Verification should be proportional rather than universalistic

The template says anything AI-assisted seen by someone else gets a human check. This is a reasonable beginner safety rule, but research can refine it: the intensity of checking should rise with consequences and the reviewer's ability to recognise errors.

## 9. Add a short “when not to use AI” moment

This would protect the chapter from evangelism. Good examples: trivial calculation, existing reliable template, too-sensitive information, no approved tool, or verification cost greater than the saved work.

## 10. Myth vs Reality should focus on behaviour-changing misconceptions

Best candidates:

- AI automatically saves time.
- Paid account = approved/safe.
- Removing a name = safe.
- Professional-looking output = ready to send.
- No company ban = approval.
- AI at work means automating entire jobs.
