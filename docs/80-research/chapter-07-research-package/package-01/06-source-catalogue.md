# Source Catalogue / Working Bibliography

Research checked through **30 September 2026**. URLs below are intended as the working source list for the later chapter-writing system. Product documentation should be rechecked near publication.

## A. Productivity and workplace-performance research

### A1. Noy & Zhang — professional writing experiment

**Title:** Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence  
**Authors:** Shakked Noy; Whitney Zhang  
**Publication:** *Science*, 381(6654), 187–192  
**Date:** 14 July 2023 (online 13 July 2023)  
**Source type:** Peer-reviewed preregistered experiment / strong causal evidence  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37440646/

**Supports:** 453 college-educated professionals; occupation-specific midlevel writing tasks; ChatGPT group completed work about 40% faster with output quality about 18% higher; reduced measured worker inequality in the experiment.

**Useful for:** report drafting, writing, productivity evidence.

**Caveat:** specific writing tasks and 2023 model/tool environment; not a universal knowledge-work productivity estimate.

---

### A2. Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond — customer support

**Title:** Generative AI at Work  
**Authors:** Erik Brynjolfsson; Danielle Li; Lindsey R. Raymond  
**Publication:** *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 140(2), 889–942  
**Date:** May 2025  
**Source type:** Large real-world field study  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044  
**NBER working-paper page:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

**Supports:** final paper covers 5,172 customer-support agents; AI assistance increased issues resolved per hour about 15% on average; gains were larger for less experienced/lower-skilled workers.

**Useful for:** customer support, workplace productivity, skill diffusion.

**Caveat:** one organisational/tool context; do not present 15% as universal.

---

### A3. Dell'Acqua et al. — jagged technological frontier

**Title:** Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality  
**Authors:** Fabrizio Dell'Acqua; Edward McFowland III; Ethan Mollick; Hila Lifshitz; Katherine C. Kellogg; Saran Rajendran; Lisa Krayer; François Candelon; Karim R. Lakhani  
**Publication:** *Organization Science*, 37(2), 403–423  
**Published online:** 11 March 2026  
**Source type:** Peer-reviewed preregistered experiment / strong causal evidence  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838

**Supports:** 758 knowledge workers; on 18 within-frontier tasks AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster and at higher quality; on a deliberately outside-frontier complex managerial task AI users were 19% less likely to be correct.

**Useful for:** central conceptual model, task fit, verification, limits.

**Caveat:** exact frontier moves as models improve; task-specific results.

---

### A4. Microsoft Research — work-pattern field experiment

**Title:** Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI  
**Authors:** Eleanor Dillon; Sonia Jaffe; Nicole Immorlica; Christopher Stanton  
**Date:** April 2025  
**Source type:** Six-month cross-industry randomised field experiment  
**URL:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/shifting-work-patterns-with-generative-ai/

**Supports:** 6,000 workers; users spent roughly three fewer hours / 25% less time on email weekly (intent-to-treat 1.4 hours), documents appeared moderately faster, no significant reduction in meeting time.

**Useful for:** email/document productivity, limitation of coordination-heavy work.

**Caveat:** Microsoft Research/product environment; distinguish user-effect estimates from intent-to-treat estimate.

---

### A5. METR — experienced open-source developers

**Title:** Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity  
**Authors:** Joel Becker; Nate Rush; Beth Barnes; David Rein  
**Organisation:** METR  
**Date:** 10 July 2025  
**Source type:** Randomised controlled field experiment  
**URL:** https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/  
**Paper:** https://metr.org/Early_2025_AI_Experienced_OS_Devs_Study-paper.pdf

**Supports:** 16 experienced developers, 246 real tasks in mature repositories, average ~5 years repository experience; AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer; developers forecast speed-up and perceived one even after measured slowdown.

**Useful for:** counter-hype example, productivity perception vs measurement.

**Caveat:** narrow specialised sample and early-2025 tools; authors explicitly reject broad “AI slows software developers” generalisation.

---

### A6. OECD — review of AI productivity evidence

**Title:** The effects of generative AI on productivity, innovation and entrepreneurship  
**Authors:** Daniele Calvino; Joris Reijerink; Lea Samek  
**Series:** OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 39  
**Date:** 20 June 2025  
**Source type:** Research synthesis / policy analysis  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1787/b21df222-en

**Supports:** productivity impact depends on task and worker experience; human-AI collaboration and organisational context matter; long-term firm-level evidence remains developing.

**Useful for:** balanced overview and caveats.

## B. Adoption and workplace-use surveys

### B1. OECD — SME workforce survey

**Title:** Generative AI and the SME Workforce: New Survey Evidence  
**Organisation:** OECD  
**Date:** 5 November 2025  
**Source type:** Representative cross-country SME survey / self-reported outcomes  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1787/2d08b99d-en  
**Landing page:** https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-and-the-sme-workforce_2d08b99d-en.html

**Method:** 5,232 SMEs; Austria, Canada, Germany, Ireland, Japan, Korea and UK; fieldwork 14 October–6 December 2024.

**Supports:** around 31% adoption; 65% of users report improved employee performance; 83% report no overall staffing change; 39% of users with a skill gap report AI helped compensate; legal/copyright/data-handling/skills concerns remain significant.

**Useful for:** current adoption, ordinary use, governance/training gaps.

**Caveat:** survey and self-reported effects; not causal productivity estimates. Countries surveyed do not include Australia.

## C. Critical thinking, creativity and human factors

### C1. Lee et al. — critical thinking with GenAI

**Title:** The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers  
**Authors:** Hao-Ping (Hank) Lee; Advait Sarkar; Lev Tankelevitch; Ian Drosos; Sean Rintel; Richard Banks; Nicholas Wilson  
**Publication:** CHI 2025  
**Date:** April 2025  
**Source type:** Survey / 319 knowledge workers / 936 first-hand examples  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778  
**Microsoft Research:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/

**Supports:** higher confidence in GenAI associated with less reported critical-thinking effort; higher self-confidence associated with more; critical thinking shifts toward verification/integration/stewardship.

**Useful for:** verification, human oversight, overreliance discussion.

**Caveat:** self-reported associations; not proof of causal cognitive decline.

---

### C2. Doshi & Hauser — creativity and diversity

**Title:** Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content  
**Authors:** Anil R. Doshi; Oliver P. Hauser  
**Publication:** *Science Advances* 10(28), eadn5290  
**Date:** 12 July 2024  
**Source type:** Randomised online experiment  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290  
**Open-access copy:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/

**Supports:** LLM idea access improved judged creativity/writing/enjoyment, especially for less creative writers; AI-assisted stories were more similar to each other.

**Useful for:** brainstorming trade-offs, anchoring/homogenisation analogy.

**Caveat:** short-story writing study; do not state it directly proves business brainstorming becomes less diverse.

## Source-use notes for the later writing system

### Prefer primary sources for claims

Examples:

- use the *Science* paper/PubMed record rather than an article saying “ChatGPT makes workers 40% faster”;
- use QJE/NBER for customer support;
- use OAIC directly for Australian privacy;
- use Federal Court sources for Australian legal examples;
- use vendor help/privacy pages for current vendor-specific capabilities.

### Keep evidence types separate

Do not merge these into one undifferentiated statement:

- randomised trial result;
- self-reported survey result;
- product feature description;
- vendor privacy promise;
- government guidance;
- editorial recommendation.

### Avoid false precision

If a study reports a measured effect in one context, keep the context attached to the number. Do not turn it into a universal productivity claim.

### Recheck before publication

At minimum, recheck:

- Microsoft/Google/OpenAI enterprise privacy pages;
- Teams/Excel/PowerPoint/Sheets feature pages;
- National AI Centre guidance;
- Australian Government AI policy;
- OAIC AI guidance;
- Federal Court AI practice note if discussing current rules.
