# Education and Life Decisions as an Over-Reliance Example

This area should be used as a **decision-process example**, not as an argument for or against university, VET, TAFE, Free TAFE, bootcamps or online study.

# Scenario

A prospective student asks:

> What's the cheapest or easiest way to get into cybersecurity?

An AI system may list:
- certificate;
- university degree;
- bootcamp;
- vendor qualification;
- online course;
- subsidised/Free TAFE option.

The problem is not that any category is automatically wrong. The problem is reducing a multi-factor life decision to one optimisation variable such as **cheapest**, **fastest** or **easiest**.

# Better use of AI

Ask AI to help identify decision criteria:

- target occupation;
- accreditation;
- employer expectations;
- prerequisites;
- course content;
- delivery mode;
- time commitment;
- work placement;
- cost;
- subsidies;
- location;
- completion requirements;
- employment outcomes;
- pathways into further study;
- personal interest and constraints.

AI can then:
- create a comparison framework;
- identify missing facts;
- generate questions for providers;
- explain terminology.

The user still investigates authoritative information.

# Higher-education outcomes

QILT's 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey received more than 120,000 responses from recent graduates.

Reported 2025 full-time employment rates:
- undergraduate: 75.4%;
- postgraduate coursework: 88.3%;
- postgraduate research: 84.0%.

QILT's longitudinal survey reports domestic undergraduate full-time employment rising from 79.5% shortly after graduation for the 2022 cohort to 91.7% three years later in 2025.

These are aggregate outcomes. They vary by field, cohort and labour market, and do not tell an individual which pathway is right.

# VET outcomes

Jobs and Skills Australia's VET National Data Asset reports strong aggregate labour-market outcomes, including:
- 88% employment after qualification;
- a 16 percentage-point increase compared with before enrolment;
- median income uplift of $14,100 in the year following completion.

Again, this is not evidence that every qualification has the same return.

# VET completion rates

NCVER's *VET qualification completion rates 2025*, published 29 September 2026, reports:

- observed four-year completion rate for qualifications commencing in 2021: **48.9%**;
- projected 2022 cohort: **50.9%**;
- projected 2023 cohort: **54.7%**.

NCVER notes completion varies by factors including:
- state/territory;
- qualification/training package;
- provider;
- occupation;
- student characteristics;
- student intentions/circumstances;
- training quality;
- flexibility/support;
- economic conditions;
- policy settings.

Strong lesson:
> a raw completion statistic should not be turned into a simple story about motivation or price.

# Free TAFE

Australian government reporting in September 2026 states that from January 2023 to June 2026 Free TAFE supported:
- more than **850,000 enrolments**;
- more than **275,000 course completions**.

These should **not** be divided into a crude “completion rate”.

Why:
- enrolments started at different times;
- many students remain in progress;
- course durations differ;
- formal cohort completion rates require observation over time.

Parliamentary analysis of earlier Free TAFE figures explicitly warned that preliminary completion ratios should not be treated as final cohort completion rates because some courses take years.

# Price, motivation and completion

The expanded brief asks whether free/subsidised study reduces commitment.

The evidence collected does not establish that claim.

NCVER's 2021-cohort aggregate completion rates by funding category include:
- government-funded: 48.9%;
- domestic fee-for-service: 44.8%;
- international fee-for-service: 62.6%.

These categories differ in many ways besides price. The figures do not support “paying more causes more commitment”; domestic fee-for-service was below government-funded in this aggregate comparison.

Do **not** make:
> Free courses make people less committed.

# Human advisers

No strong evidence was found establishing that consumer AI is causally reducing engagement with Australian course/career advisers.

Safer use:
> AI can help prospective students prepare better questions before contacting providers, career advisers, employers or training organisations.

# Strong Chapter-5 demonstration

## Weak
> What's the best course to become a data analyst?

## Stronger
> I want to move into data analysis within two years. I currently have [background], can study about eight hours a week and need to keep working. Help me identify the pathway types I should compare in Australia. Do not choose one for me. For each pathway, tell me which facts I should verify from providers or official sources, including cost, duration, prerequisites, accreditation if relevant, and employment evidence.

Follow-up:
> What assumptions are you making about my goal, and what questions should I answer before comparing options?

This keeps AI in a research-support role.
