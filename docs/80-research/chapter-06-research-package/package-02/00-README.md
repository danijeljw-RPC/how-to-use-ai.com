## Core research conclusion

The evidence supports a balanced position:

1. AI is already present in ordinary consumer activities: photo search and editing, translation, writing, visual assistance, search, planning and accessibility.
2. Context and specificity improve usefulness because they reduce the assumptions a general-purpose AI system must make.
3. AI is particularly useful for generating options, explaining, reorganising, transforming formats and helping users explore problems.
4. Live, consequential or highly personalised facts often require independent checking or professional/official sources.
5. Cognitive offloading is normal and can improve performance. The important question is what is being offloaded.
6. Current evidence does **not** justify broad claims such as “AI makes people stupid” or “AI destroys critical thinking”.
7. AI can support learning and reasoning when it is used to coach, question, scaffold and provide feedback rather than simply supply finished answers.
8. For consequential decisions, AI is better framed as an investigation and comparison aid than as the final decision-maker.
