# Additional Findings and Strongest Chapter-Ready Findings

# Additional findings

## AI at home does not need to start with prompting

Current consumer features suggest a wider interaction model:
- tap;
- circle;
- photograph;
- speak;
- search naturally;
- ask about a screen.

Why it matters:
- makes AI less technical;
- avoids implying AI literacy means “prompt engineering”;
- gives the introduction immediately relatable examples.

## Verification is not one behaviour

The research suggests several levels:

### Visual/common-sense check
Appropriate for a photo edit or low-stakes suggestion.

### Check against the user's own facts
Appropriate for a drafted message or organised list.

### Check against a live source
Appropriate for opening hours, prices, transport and availability.

### Check against an authority/professional
Appropriate for visa rules, important financial decisions, legal obligations and health matters.

This is more useful than repeating “always verify everything”.

## “Give me sources” is incomplete advice

Because citations themselves can increase trust, the stronger behaviour is:

> Ask for sources, then open the important ones.

## AI can deliberately preserve useful friction

For learning and consequential decisions, removing all friction is not always the goal.

Useful interaction patterns:
- ask the user questions first;
- delay the finished answer;
- require an attempt;
- offer hints;
- challenge assumptions;
- ask the user to compare options.

A concise chapter line could be supported by this research:
> Sometimes the best way to use AI is to ask it not to give you the answer immediately.

## Accessibility should be framed as capability expansion

The current chapter draft mainly frames accessibility around simpler text. Research supports a broader set:
- visual interpretation;
- document interaction;
- product/object identification;
- screen guidance;
- translation;
- transcription.

This materially strengthens the chapter.

## Australia has strong consumer-facing AI guidance

Smartraveller and Moneysmart both published domain-specific AI guidance in 2026.

They are especially valuable because they are:
- Australian;
- consumer-facing;
- practical;
- non-promotional;
- aligned with the chapter's “helpful but not sole authority” thesis.

# Strongest Chapter-Ready Findings

## 1. AI is already inside ordinary tools

Use:
- natural-language photo search;
- photo cleanup;
- translation;
- screen/image questions;
- accessibility.

Why:
- immediate relevance;
- minimal jargon;
- non-work context.

Evidence:
official product documentation.

## 2. Smartraveller's plan-versus-live-fact distinction

Core:
> AI can help create the trip plan; verify visas, safety, schedules, local laws, prices and availability.

Why:
- directly teaches judgement;
- Australian;
- current;
- fits planned Travel section.

## 3. Moneysmart's learn-versus-decide distinction

Core:
> AI can help explain money and organise questions; general-purpose AI should not become the user's personal financial adviser.

Why:
- excellent fit with the chapter's effort/judgement boundary.

## 4. Accessibility as independence

Use:
- Seeing AI;
- Be My Eyes;
- Copilot Vision.

Why:
- demonstrates that utility depends on the user's needs;
- moves accessibility beyond “nice to have”.

## 5. Performance versus learning

Core:
> AI helping you finish a task is not automatically the same as AI helping you become able to do it.

Why:
- strong bridge between hobbies/parenting and over-reliance.

## 6. Cognitive offloading is normal

Core:
> Humans have always used tools to reduce mental load. The question is what you choose to offload.

Why:
- prevents a fear-based/moralising tone.

## 7. Trust calibration

Core:
> Fluent, personalised or well-cited output can feel authoritative without necessarily being correct.

Evidence:
- critical-thinking confidence study;
- AI-search trust/citation experiment.

Use with caveats because this is still developing research.

## 8. Answer-first systems can shorten investigation

Core:
> Generated answers can make it easier to stop at the answer rather than open underlying sources.

Evidence:
Pew browsing study.

Counterpoint:
Google reports stable aggregate organic click volume and increased “quality clicks”.

## 9. Ask AI to challenge rather than merely choose

Reader behaviours:
- alternatives;
- assumptions;
- what would change the answer;
- counterarguments;
- questions the user should answer.

Why:
- turns AI from answer machine into thought support.

## 10. Education choice example

Use:
- AI maps pathways and criteria;
- user verifies providers, accreditation, costs, time and outcomes;
- user makes the decision.

Avoid:
- “free study reduces commitment”;
- university-versus-VET hierarchy;
- raw Free TAFE completion arithmetic.
