# Organising Everyday Life

This is one of the clearest places to show AI removing friction, while also teaching that AI is not automatically the best tool.

## Useful tasks

- shopping lists;
- packing lists;
- weekly routines;
- chore planning;
- event preparation;
- holiday preparation;
- maintenance checklists;
- breaking a large personal project into smaller steps;
- turning a messy brain-dump into grouped tasks;
- summarising discussion into decisions/actions.

## Where AI adds value

AI adds most value when input is:
- messy;
- unstructured;
- context-heavy;
- difficult to categorise;
- different each time.

Example:
> Here is everything I need to do before we leave Friday afternoon. Group it into buy, book, pack, house and day-of. Put dependencies first.

## Where conventional tools are better

### Calendar
Better for:
- exact date/time;
- recurrence;
- reminders;
- shared scheduling;
- notification reliability.

### Reminder app
Better for:
- one future action;
- time/location-based notification.

### Spreadsheet
Better for:
- exact calculations;
- stable tables;
- repeatable formulas;
- sorting/filtering;
- auditability.

### Checklist/notes
Better when:
- the task is already obvious;
- no interpretation is required.

Chapter-ready anti-hype principle:

> Good AI use includes knowing when the boring old tool is better.

## Vague → better

### Weak
> Organise my weekend.

### Stronger
> I need to do these things this weekend: [list]. The hardware store closes at 5 pm Saturday, the dog groomer is booked for 10 am Saturday, groceries need refrigeration so I want them near the end of an outing, and I want Sunday afternoon free. Group the errands into a sensible order and show me anything that cannot be scheduled from the information I gave you.

Why it works:
- fixed constraints;
- dependency;
- preference;
- explicit instruction to expose missing information rather than invent it.

## Breaking down overwhelming tasks

Useful for:
- moving house;
- preparing for a party;
- clearing a spare room;
- holiday preparation;
- organising paperwork;
- renovation preparation.

Prompt pattern:
> Help me break this into stages. Do not decide dates yet. First identify major groups of work and anything that depends on something else.

## Convenience becoming capability

Mostly a friction-removal section, but capability can be added:

> Explain why you grouped these tasks this way so I can use the same method next time.

That turns a one-off answer into a reusable organising technique.

## Companion-site exercise

Give a deliberately messy list of household tasks and compare:
1. alphabetical sorting;
2. priority sorting;
3. AI grouping by location/dependency/time.

Ask:
- what extra context made the AI version useful?
- what did it assume?
- would a checklist have been simpler?
