# Search behaviour and adoption

## Pew — AI summaries and click-through
- **Authors:** Athena Chapekis, Anna Lieb
- **Date:** 22 July 2025
- **Sample:** 900 U.S. adults; 68,879 unique Google searches in March 2025
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/
- **Supports:** lower observed click-through when AI summaries appear; cited-link click rate.
- **Limitations:** U.S.; observational; search interface changes.

## Pew — Americans and AI 2026
- **Date:** 17 June 2026
- **Sample:** 5,119 U.S. adults
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/
- **Supports:** consumer chatbot adoption and uses.
- **Limitations:** U.S. self-report; not Australian prevalence.

## Reuters Institute — Generative AI and news report 2025
- **URL:** https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2025-how-people-think-about-ais-role-journalism-and-society
- **Supports:** cross-country encounters with AI search answers, source-clicking and trust.
- **Limitations:** survey behaviour rather than tracked behaviour.

## Google — AI in Search and click claims
- **Date:** 6 August 2025
- **URL:** https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/
- **Supports:** Google's report that organic click volume was relatively stable year-over-year and “quality clicks” increased slightly.

# Human cognition and decision support

## Risko & Gilbert — Cognitive Offloading
- **Journal:** Trends in Cognitive Sciences
- **Year:** 2016
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002
- **Supports:** definition and normality of cognitive offloading.
- **Limitation:** predates GenAI.

## Burnett & Richmond — Cognitive offloading meta-analysis
- **Journal:** Memory & Cognition
- **Year:** 2026
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40500483/
- **Supports:** offloading can improve memory-task performance and reduce interindividual variability.
- **Limitation:** not specifically consumer GenAI.

## Goddard, Roudsari & Wyatt — Automation bias
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21685142/
- **Type:** systematic review
- **Supports:** over-reliance on automated decision support and new error modes.
- **Limitation:** predates GenAI and includes specialised decision-support contexts.

## Parasuraman & Manzey — Complacency and bias
- **Year:** 2010
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21077562/
- **Supports:** attentional/complacency mechanisms in automation use.

## Lee et al. — GenAI and critical thinking
- **Venue:** CHI 2025
- **URL:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/
- **Sample:** 319 knowledge workers, 936 examples
- **Supports:** confidence effects; self-reported critical thinking; verification/integration.
- **Limitations:** self-report; knowledge workers; not evidence of long-term cognitive decline.

## Reicherts et al. — AI, Help Me Think—but for Myself
- **Venue:** CHI 2025
- **URL:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/ai-help-me-think-but-for-myself-assisting-people-in-complex-decision-making-by-providing-different-kinds-of-cognitive-support/
- **Supports:** reasoning-support versus recommendation-centric AI.
- **Limitations:** specific experimental decision task; do not universalise.

## Banks & Rintel — Better Thinking Through AI
- **Organisation:** Microsoft Research
- **Date:** March 2026
- **URL:** https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/better-thinking-through-ai-10-strategies-for-using-ai-to-improve-your-thinking/
- **Type:** research-informed practical guide
- **Supports:** alternatives, questions, outcomes, related material and learning strategies.

## Li & Aral — Human Trust in AI Search
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435
- **Type:** preprint; large-scale experiment
- **Supports:** citations/reference links can increase trust even when wrong.
- **Limitation:** preprint; label as emerging evidence.

# Source explicitly NOT suitable as evidence

## Retracted 2025 ChatGPT meta-analysis

A 2025 Humanities and Social Sciences Communications article titled *The effect of ChatGPT on students' learning performance, learning perception, and higher-order thinking* was **retracted on 22 April 2026**.

Do not use its original headline findings as evidence.

Retraction/source page:  
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04787-y

This is recorded deliberately so a downstream research/writing system does not rediscover and cite the retracted result.
