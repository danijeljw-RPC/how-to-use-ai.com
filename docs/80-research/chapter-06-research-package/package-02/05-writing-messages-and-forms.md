# Writing Emails, Messages and Forms

The strongest framing is:

> AI can help a person express their intent. It should not quietly become the author of their intent.

## Practical household uses

- make an email clearer;
- shorten a message;
- adjust tone;
- draft a complaint;
- follow up a delayed return or repair;
- communicate with a school;
- translate a message;
- explain a formal letter;
- simplify a form question;
- turn rough notes into a coherent response;
- help a user with literacy or language barriers express what they mean.

## Context that improves output

For a message:
- recipient;
- relationship;
- relevant facts;
- chronology;
- desired outcome;
- desired tone;
- facts that must remain;
- facts AI must not invent;
- length.

For a form:
- exact question;
- definitions/instructions supplied with it;
- the user's facts;
- what the user does not understand.

## Vague → better

### Weak
> Write an email to my landlord.

### Stronger
> Help me write a polite but firm email to my landlord. I reported a leaking tap on 4 September and followed up on 15 September. It still has not been repaired. I want to ask for a repair date this week. Keep it under 180 words. Do not add legal claims.

Why it improves:
- facts are supplied;
- objective is explicit;
- tone is explicit;
- length is bounded;
- invention is constrained.

## Preserve user ownership

Useful workflow:

1. User states what happened.
2. User states what outcome they want.
3. AI drafts.
4. User checks facts, promises, tone and whether the wording reflects what they mean.
5. User edits before sending.

Potential failure modes:
- invented facts;
- stronger claims than intended;
- unfamiliar tone;
- commitments the user did not mean to make.

## Forms and letters: three modes

### Explain
> Explain what this question is asking in plain English.

### Prepare
> Based only on the facts I provide, turn these notes into a concise draft answer.

### Check
> Does my draft appear to answer every part of the question? Do not change my facts.

These preserve more ownership than “fill the form in for me”.

## Personal voice

The gathered research does not justify a broad claim that AI necessarily “flattens” everyone's voice. A defensible practical observation is that generated wording often trends toward conventional clarity, politeness and structure. Users should therefore edit drafts so they still sound like themselves.

Treat this as editorial guidance, not a proven population-level cognitive effect.

## Important boundary

For consequential material—contracts, tax, visa applications, legal rights, medical consent, financial products—AI can explain wording, but the original document and authoritative guidance remain primary.

## Accessibility bridge

Writing assistance can be more than convenience for:
- users with dyslexia;
- people with limited literacy;
- second-language users;
- people using voice because typing is difficult;
- users under stress who know what they want to say but struggle to structure it.
