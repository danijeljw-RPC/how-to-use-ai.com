# Travel Planning

Travel is one of the strongest sections because current Australian government guidance describes almost exactly the workflow Chapter 6 needs.

## Current Australian evidence

In July 2026 Smartraveller published dedicated guidance, *Travel planning with AI*.

DFAT research quoted there reported among Gen Z travellers:
- 30% used AI tools to plan overseas trips;
- 28% used AI for visa/entry information;
- 27% used AI for safety advice;
- 19% used AI for local laws/customs;
- 82% were likely to cross-check AI travel information.

Smartraveller says AI can help with:
- destination ideas;
- activities;
- itineraries;
- budgets;
- language;
- early-stage organisation.

It warns that AI can provide:
- outdated flights/transport;
- incorrect visa/border information;
- wrong interpretations of local laws;
- closed hotels;
- unavailable or seasonal attractions;
- inaccurate costs;
- missing safety warnings.

## Core chapter-ready idea

> AI is good at constructing a candidate trip. Live facts still belong to live sources.

This is more concrete than a generic warning about hallucinations.

## Context that improves a travel request

- destination or candidates;
- dates/season;
- number/ages of travellers;
- budget;
- interests;
- mobility;
- children;
- transport preferences;
- pace;
- existing bookings;
- dietary needs;
- amount of free time.

## Vague → better

### Weak
> Plan four days in Lisbon.

### Stronger
> I'm planning four days in Lisbon in October with one other adult. We have a moderate budget, prefer walking and public transport, care more about food and neighbourhoods than nightlife, and don't want more than two major activities in one day. Give me a rough day-by-day structure. Group nearby things together. Mark anything whose opening hours, ticket availability or price I should verify before booking.

Why better:
- interests;
- pace;
- budget;
- transport;
- explicit verification.

## Strong workflow

1. Ask AI for several candidate destinations meeting constraints.
2. Narrow to two.
3. Ask for trade-offs and missing information.
4. Verify official travel advice, entry rules, flights, accommodation, transport and attractions.
5. Feed confirmed bookings back to AI.
6. Ask for a day-by-day structure using confirmed facts.
7. Export to checklist/table.

Verification is not “rejecting AI”; it is part of using AI well.

## Low-risk travel use: packing

Packing lists are easy to inspect and relatively low consequence.

Useful context:
- destination;
- season;
- duration;
- activities;
- laundry access;
- luggage limit;
- formal events.

## Companion-site exercise

### Plan vs fact

Show a sample itinerary containing:
- neighbourhood walk;
- museum;
- train;
- restaurant;
- visa statement.

Ask readers to classify each as:
- useful brainstorm;
- needs checking;
- should come from an official source.
