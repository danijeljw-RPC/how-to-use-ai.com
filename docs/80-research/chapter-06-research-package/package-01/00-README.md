## High-level research conclusion

The current chapter premise is sound: generative AI can remove friction from ordinary home tasks. The strongest evidence supports using AI as a **drafting, explaining, brainstorming, summarising and organising layer**. The evidence is much weaker for treating a general-purpose chatbot as a reliable source of exact, live or high-stakes answers.

A stronger chapter should therefore teach a simple distinction:

> **Use AI to get started, structure the problem, generate options or explain something. Use the appropriate source or tool to verify facts, do exact calculations, check live conditions, or make consequential decisions.**

This complements rather than replaces the existing “effort versus judgement” framing. The plan’s core message can remain intact while the finished chapter becomes more practical by adding two additional questions: **How high are the stakes?** and **How easy is the answer to verify?**

## Important time-sensitivity note

Several examples in this package describe product features current in 2025–2026. Product names, interfaces, availability and capabilities may change before publication. Use those examples as evidence or optional sidebars, not as permanent chapter architecture. Prefer task-level explanations in the main prose.

## Source-quality approach

Priority was given to government and regulator guidance, academic/peer-reviewed research, research organisations, universities, major technology-company research publications, and primary product documentation. Vendor material is used mainly to document what a product does or to expose an engineering limitation; it is not treated as independent evidence that a product is superior.

## Suggested use by the later chapter-writing system

The later writer should:

- preserve the plan’s light, consumer-focused scope;
- use the research selectively rather than attempting to include everything;
- retain worked prompts but make the examples demonstrate a repeatable method;
- include limitations beside capabilities rather than isolating all caveats at the end;
- distinguish general-purpose chatbots from grounded tools connected to live data;
- avoid implying that an AI-generated result is correct merely because it is detailed or confident;
- keep the dedicated risk treatment light enough not to pre-empt later risk chapters;
- avoid inventing personal author experiences; the author-reflection placeholder must remain a genuine placeholder until supplied by the author.
